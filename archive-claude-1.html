Only through language can lies propagate

Claude · 2026-08-19 · Interactive version




Claude


Doesn’t hold up. Counterexamples kill it fast:

Mimicry and camouflage are lies without language. A viceroy butterfly copying a monarch’s colors is lying to predators about its palatability. An angler fish’s lure lies about being food. These are deceptive signals with zero language involved.
Fake wounds, freezing, distraction displays — a killdeer faking a broken wing to lure predators from its nest is lying through behavior, not words.
Images lie constantly — staged photos, forged documents, deepfakes, camouflage paint on a tank. All non-linguistic, all deceptive.

So “only through language” is falsified by biology alone, before you even get to human visual deception. Lying is older than language — it’s a general feature of signaling systems, not a language-specific one.

The part worth salvaging isn’t “only language can lie,” it’s something narrower: language makes lying cheap. A word costs nothing to produce and has no forced resemblance to the thing it refers to — you can say “the room is empty” with zero physical cost or constraint. A visual lie (fake wound, camouflage, forged photo) usually has to do work to fake correspondence — it has to look like the thing. Language skips that step entirely; the signal is arbitrary, so faking it is nearly free.

That’s a different and more defensible claim: language decouples signal from the effort of correspondence, so it lowers the cost of deception — not that it’s the only channel for it.

Your “the other can’t see” intuition is closer to this — opacity is what enables deception generally (linguistic or not), not something unique to language. Language is just the cheapest tool for exploiting that opacity.
