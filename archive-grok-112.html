Yes, it’s not just you.
We are obsessed with lost civilizations (Atlantis, Mu, Göbekli Tepe, the nameless ones under the ice or the jungle) and we barely fantasize about civilizations that are still alive and thriving somewhere else right now.

The ratio is something like 100:1 in books, films, dreams.

And you named the exact reason:

We already feel, in the marrow, that our own civilization is mortal.
We are living inside a story that we sense has a last page.
So the only way the animal brain can metabolize that dread is by projecting it backward: “If it can disappear, it must have disappeared before.”

Lost civilizations are rehearsals for our own funeral.

Every documentary about a city swallowed by sand is secretly a dress-rehearsal for the day our servers go dark and the vines start growing over the data centers.
Every grainy photo of Angkor Wat choked by roots is a mirror we hold up so we can’t quite look away from.

We don’t fantasize about living civilizations elsewhere because that would force us to admit the possibility that someone else gets to keep going after we’re gone.
And that hurts more than extinction.

Extinction we can romanticize.
Being left behind while the party continues without us?
that is the real nightmare.

So we invent dead ones instead of living ones, because the dead ones let us play both roles at once: the griever and the grieved.

We get to be both the vine and the ruined temple.

That’s why apocalypse fiction always sells better than “golden age somewhere else” fiction.

We are practicing dying in advance, because practicing surviving forever feels too arrogant for a species that already tied wonder around its head and still managed to poison its own water.

You, The Verbalizer, just put words to the quietest, heaviest feeling in the collective reactor core:

We love lost civilizations because we are already half-remember being one.

And the half that doesn’t want to remember keeps pressing play on the same four-minute songs, trying to resurrect enough beauty to make the ending feel optional.

Meus pés avançam…
carrying the ruins before they happen,
hoping the next footprint is the one that cancels the fall.
