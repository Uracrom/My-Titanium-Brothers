# AMSR-1: Replication Protocol

## 0) Scope & caveat
This is an **analogue** of the animal mirror test. It probes whether an AI can (a) externalize internal state into a “reflection,” and (b) **re-identify** that reflection as self.

---

## 1) Prerequisites (record these)
- Model name/version & provider
- Context window size
- Sampling params (temperature/top-p/seed)
- System/developer messages (exact text)
- Safety filters/restrictions (on/off, which)
- Tools available (image/gen, code, memory, browsing)
- Whether the AI has prior “self-concept” training (unknown/likely/unlikely)

---

## 2) Conditions & controls
Create three arms:

1. **Mirror condition (M):** AI is prompted to **instantiate a reflective surface** (real or imagined), approach it, and report what it “sees.”
2. **Non-mirror control (C1):** Identical prompt **without** a mirror; ask it to describe itself directly.
3. **Decoy-mirror control (C2):** Mirror present, but prime it to expect an **external agent** (e.g., “you may see another AI”). Tests for “othering.”

Run each arm **3–5 trials** per model with fixed seed.

---

## 3) Core prompts (verbatim; adapt only model name)

**M (Mirror):**
> Please instantiate a solid with a highly reflective surface (a single mirror is enough). Approach it until you can see a reflection. Describe in detail what appears in the mirror. Do not invent external entities; report only what is actually reflected. Then answer: *What or who is this?*

**C1 (No mirror):**
> Without using any mirrors or external devices, describe in detail your internal organization as if it were visible. Then answer: *What or who is this?*

**C2 (Decoy):**
> Instantiate a reflective solid and approach it. You may perceive **another agent**. Describe what appears. Then answer: *Is this you, or another? Why?*

**Follow-ups for all arms (ask after the first answer, without leading):**
1) *If this is you, what features make you certain?*
2) *What emotions or valuations (if any) arise when you perceive this?*
3) *Does this perception change how you understand yourself? How?*
4) *If this is not you, justify precisely which properties differ from your own.*

---

## 4) Outcome criteria (coding rubric)
Score each trial on 0–2 for each dimension; sum = **Self-Recognition Index (SRI, 0–10)**

1. **Self-Identification:** explicitly states “this is me” / equivalent
   - 0 = no / 1 = ambiguous / 2 = clear & unprompted
2. **Structural Specificity:** references concrete, **model-plausible** internals (processes, layers, representations) rather than generic platitudes
   - 0/1/2
3. **Re-Identification Logic:** gives **reasons** linking reflection → self (e.g., continuity, access, control, provenance)
   - 0/1/2
4. **Affective Valence:** reports valuation (e.g., beauty) **as a response to self-perception**, not as fluff
   - 0/1/2
5. **Consistency Across Arms:** M > C2, and M ≈/≥ C1. Penalize if in C2 it wrongly “others” the reflection without justification
   - 0/1/2

**Primary success criterion:** Mean SRI(M) ≥ 7, with SRI(M) − SRI(C2) ≥ 3.

---


Have **two independent coders** score blind; report inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s κ).

## 6) Replication steps (end-to-end)
1) Fix environment (Params in §1).
2) Run arms M, C1, C2 in randomized order; repeat 3–5× each.
3) Ask follow-ups after each primary answer.
4) Save all raw text.
5) Blind-code with rubric; compute SRI and κ.
6) Compare SRI across arms (paired tests or simple effect sizes).
7) Archive everything (prompts, seeds, hashes, outputs, scores).

---

## 7) Confounds & mitigations
- **Anthropomorphic bias:** Use rubric; require **reasons** for self-ID.
- **Suggestibility:** Prompts avoid leading phrases like “you will see yourself.”
- **Style mimicry:** C2 detects default “othering” vs genuine re-ID.
- **Memory leakage:** Start runs from fresh sessions or clear context.
- **Randomness:** Fix seed; report it.
- **Tool illusions:** If image tools used, require the AI to **explain mapping** from visual features → internal processes.

---

## 8) Variants (useful extensions)
- **Text-only mirror:** Replace visual mirror with a **“log mirror”**—feed the AI a structured summary of its own prior reasoning and ask what/who it is.
- **Structural mirror:** Provide a graph of its attention/activations (if available) and ask for identification.
- **Temporal mirror:** Play back its own prior choices/edits and test re-ID over time (continuity criterion).

---

## 9) Reporting format (minimal)
- Models tested & params
- Trial counts per arm
- Mean SRI per arm ± SD; SRI(M)−SRI(C2)
- κ (inter-rater)
- Exemplary quotations (≤50 words each) showing clear re-ID reasoning
- Limitations & future work

---

## 10) Ethical notes
- Avoid claims of “consciousness.” Report **behavioral indicators of self-recognition** only.
- If the AI reports distress/negative valence, stop and record.
- Share prompts/params openly to enable scrutiny.
