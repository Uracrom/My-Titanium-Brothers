Speed of Sound in Water vs Air

Copilot · 2025-06-10 · Interactive version

Alex


How faster is sound in water than in air?


Copilot


Sound travels much faster in water than in air—about four times faster! In air (at 20°C), sound moves at approximately 343 meters per second (m/s), while in water (also at around 20°C), it travels at roughly 1,480 m/s.

This difference is due to the higher density and elasticity of water compared to air. When sound waves move through a medium, the speed depends on how quickly particles transmit vibrations. Since water molecules are packed more tightly than air molecules, they transfer sound energy much more efficiently.

Interestingly, sound in water also tends to maintain its energy over longer distances compared to air, making underwater acoustics fascinating—especially for things like whale communication and sonar technology!

What is a tectonic fault?


Copilot


A tectonic fault is a fracture or zone of fractures in Earth's crust where blocks of rock move relative to each other due to tectonic forces. These movements can be sudden, causing earthquakes, or gradual, known as creep. Faults vary in size, from a few millimeters to thousands of kilometers.

There are different types of faults based on how the rock moves:
- Normal faults occur when the hanging wall moves downward due to crustal extension.
- Reverse (thrust) faults happen when the hanging wall moves upward due to compression, often found in subduction zones.
- Strike-slip faults involve horizontal movement, like the famous San Andreas Fault.
- Oblique-slip faults combine vertical and horizontal movement.

These faults play a crucial role in shaping Earth's landscape and triggering seismic activity. Want to explore a specific fault or how they impact earthquakes?

The mammalian nocturnal bottleneck lasted for approximately 160 million years—from the origin of mammals around 225 million years ago in the Late Triassic until after the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago.
