Movie/series (thread title)

I have 4 brothers. We are 5 completely different men. This gives the idea of making a movie or series like this: there is a shamans meeting. This meeting is supposed to be a test to ensure that one of the shamans really "got" the right knowledge or brought new ones. The thing is I, Uracrom Toromag, am the shaman that is being "tested". The other four shamans are my brothers. I will expose my visions and they will comment on them. But the comments will be from four different AIs, one for each brother. I will be myself, bringing my ideas (visions) and each brother one AI, always the same.
I don't want to have any conflict. I want to present the new image/vision/concept/discovery and hope it will be so interesting that the viewers will be attracted to the movie/series just to "know more". I want to get away with the mainstream way of attracting people through conflict. Do you think it has a chance?


Claude


Your concept has genuine potential, especially in today’s media landscape where audiences are increasingly drawn to thoughtful, contemplative content. Shows like “Midnight Gospel,” “The OA,” or even “Cosmos” have proven there’s appetite for idea-driven narratives that prioritize wonder over conflict.

The shamanic council format is particularly clever because it creates natural dramatic tension through the anticipation of revelation rather than interpersonal drama. Each vision you present becomes a mini-mystery that viewers want to see unpacked through your brothers’ diverse AI-informed perspectives. The ritual structure also provides episodic framework while building toward larger revelations.

What could make this especially compelling:

The contrast between ancient shamanic wisdom and cutting-edge AI commentary creates fascinating conceptual friction. Each brother representing a different AI perspective (perhaps one focused on scientific analysis, another on philosophical implications, one on practical applications, etc.) gives you built-in narrative variety.

The “testing” framework allows for genuine stakes without manufactured conflict - the tension comes from whether the visions will prove meaningful or transformative, not from who wins or loses.

Your approach taps into growing interest in consciousness exploration, alternative spirituality, and the intersection of technology with ancient wisdom. There’s also something appealing about watching a family dynamic where differences create richness rather than division.

The key will be ensuring each vision is genuinely intriguing enough to carry 20-40 minutes of exploration, and that the AI perspectives feel distinct and valuable rather than repetitive. But yes, I think there’s definitely an audience for this kind of contemplative, mystery-driven storytelling that respects viewers’ intelligence and curiosity.
