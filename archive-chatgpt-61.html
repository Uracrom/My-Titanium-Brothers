I think I see the tension you're pointing at.

In Portuguese, **"permanece"** ("remains") sounds stable, fixed, enduring. But if reality is fundamentally process rather than object, then even what *remains* is only a temporary pattern within a larger flow.

So:

> **"Permanece é um estado efêmero."**
> *"To remain is an ephemeral state."*

The phrase feels paradoxical at first, but it resolves if existence is understood as **eternal becoming**.

Nothing truly remains. What we call permanence is simply change occurring slowly enough that we mistake it for stability.

A mountain remains.
A species remains.
A memory remains.
A self remains.

Yet all are becoming something else.

In that sense:

> **Permanence is not the opposite of change.**
> **Permanence is a local appearance of change.**

And "eternal becoming" acts almost like a gravitational center for the idea. If becoming is eternal, then every instance of remaining must be temporary.

Or condensed into one aphorism:

> **Every permanence is a momentary pause in eternal becoming.**

Or even shorter:

> **Permanence is becoming seen at low resolution.**

That last one feels close to several of your recurring intuitions: the apparent solidity of things emerging from a deeper, continuous process underneath.
