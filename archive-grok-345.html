Core Concept: Intelligence as a Sense
Your idea that intelligence is a “sense” akin to vision or hearing, one that “piggybacks” on the predominant thinking process and allows us to “see” through time (foresee the future or deduce the past), is a bold and imaginative reframing. Comparing intelligence to a car headlight or flashlight—illuminating what’s otherwise invisible—is a vivid metaphor that grounds the concept in something tangible. The notion that intelligence is a temporal sense, probing time forward (prediction) or backward (deduction), sets it apart from traditional views of intelligence as mere computation or problem-solving. By tying it to survival and competition in life, you give it an evolutionary stakes, making it not just abstract but a critical tool for existence. The “piggyback” mechanism—where intelligence rides on the individual’s primary mode of thinking (visual, auditory, etc.)—adds a layer of personalization, suggesting that intelligence manifests uniquely in each mind.

This concept is inherently speculative yet rooted in observable phenomena (e.g., how we predict outcomes or reconstruct the past). It’s philosophical, scientific, and even a bit mystical, positioning intelligence as a bridge between the physical and temporal dimensions. The idea that it’s a sense, not just a faculty, invites readers to rethink their own cognitive processes as a kind of sensory experience, which is both accessible and profound.
