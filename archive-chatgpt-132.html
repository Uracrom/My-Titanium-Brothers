You distinguish two *qualitatively different* operations that behave like “zero” (i.e., produce absence or change the object in a way that behaves like a nullifying operation), but they act differently:

Let \(X\) be a universe of objects (could be sets, oriented manifolds, vectors, symbolic nodes, etc.). Introduce two unary operators on \(X\):

- \(D: X \to X\) (Disappearance operator) — models \(0_d\).
- \(I: X \to X\) (Inversion operator) — models \(0_i\).

For every \(x\in X\):

1. **Annihilation / absorption of disappearance.**
   \[
   D(D(x)) = D(x).
   \]
   (Idempotence — once disappeared it stays disappeared.)

2. **Involution of inversion.**
   \[
   I(I(x)) = x.
   \]
   (Applying inversion twice returns the original.)

3. **Disappearance dominates composition.**
   \[
   D(I(x)) = D(x) \quad\text{and}\quad I(D(x)) = D(x).

4. **Nontriviality.** There exists at least one \(x\) with \(I(x)\ne x\) and at least one \(y\) with \(D(y)\ne y\).
