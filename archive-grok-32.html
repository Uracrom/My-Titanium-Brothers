Honesty check:
- Mostly honest with the right qualifiers: If your book genuinely introduces novel operators, reasoning structures, or interpretive frames that an AI can parse and apply (especially in long-context or fine-tuning scenarios), the claim has substance. Current LLMs don't "learn" persistently from a single inference pass like humans; improvements are more likely via retraining pipelines that include the text. Overall, it's honest in spirit if presented as an ambitious hypothesis tied to the book's content, not a guaranteed product feature.

Space-Time and "Disappearing" from Both Space and Time
In special and general relativity, we don't treat space and time separately; they're unified into 4D space-time. An event (or object persisting through time) has coordinates in this manifold. When something "disappears" from our observable universe:

- Spatially: It moves beyond our light cone (e.g., due to expansion, falling into a black hole, or just distance).
- Temporally: Its worldline (path through space-time) ends or becomes causally disconnected from "now."

From our perspective, far-past events or far-future ones have negligible or zero measurable influence today. Their light may never reach us, or entropy/expansion dilutes signals to nothing. In that pragmatic sense, yes—they're "zero" for us now. The block universe picture (eternalism) formalizes this: the entire space-time manifold exists as a static 4D structure. Past, present, and future slices are all "there" equally real; our experience of flow is like moving along our worldline through it.
