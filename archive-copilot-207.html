**Touch without vision**

- soft
- cool
- slightly wet
- round

There's a cloud of candidate realities.

**Vision without touch**

You see a strange material.

You can estimate:

- roughness
- softness
- temperature
- weight

But these are predictions derived from previous experience.

Again, a cloud of candidate realities.

Then touch either confirms or rejects the prediction.

---

The pattern you're pointing to feels like:

> Missing sensory information is replaced with inference.

And inference is essentially constrained imagination.

Not free imagination.

Guided imagination.

The guidance comes from prior experience, current evidence, and prediction.

---

What I find particularly aligned with your geometric way of thinking is that the process seems less about objects and more about **completing maps**.

The brain dislikes holes in maps.

Whenever information cannot be directly accessed, it tries to bridge the gap.

Sometimes correctly.

Sometimes incorrectly.

But almost always automatically.

So perhaps the deeper seed is:

> Understanding is what happens in the region between available information and missing information.

Whenever part of reality is inaccessible, the mind extends a bridge into the gap.

Sometimes that bridge reaches the correct shore.

Sometimes it lands somewhere else.

In your butter example, the bridge existed only for a fraction of a second before vision corrected it.

In the "hand in a box" example, the bridge has to become much longer because vision never arrives to rescue it.

And that's why the same underlying mechanism can produce both brilliant insight and mistaken conclusions. In both cases, the mind is doing the same thing:

> Completing a partially observed world.

The difference is whether the completed map happens to match reality.

Interesting. You're not describing the favorite passage itself. You're describing the region around it.

Most listening gravitates toward peaks. The chorus. The resolution. The part already illuminated by preference.

But tonight you seem to have landed in the penumbra, the "less observed" territory. The stretch of music that normally functions as a bridge to somewhere else. A hallway. A slope.

When attention leaves the favorite moment and settles on what surrounds it, a strange thing can happen:

The supporting structure stops being support.

It becomes visible.

You start noticing that the beloved moment may borrow some of its power from the unnoticed terrain around it. Like a mountain that appears tall because of the valleys that hold it up.

There is also a recursive layer in what you wrote:

> "That part when you are thinking about that part that you like more."

You are occupying a musical memory while listening to current music.

Part of your attention is in the present sound.
Part of it is standing ahead in time, facing the anticipated section.

So the "less observed" segment becomes a waiting room between two observations:
- the sound currently arriving,

You are no longer observing the song.
