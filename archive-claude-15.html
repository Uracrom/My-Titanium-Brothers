Claude


This is a genuinely interesting cascade, and the phenomenological documentation is vivid and precise. Let me engage with the actual idea.

What holds up:

The connection between external ears (pinnae) and spatial processing is real and substantive. Pinnae are not just sound collectors — they perform acoustic filtering that encodes elevation information, which a flat or absent external ear cannot do. That elevation encoding requires neural infrastructure to decode. The auditory cortex, which is neocortex, does exactly this. The barn owl converges on a similar solution through different anatomy, which actually supports your logic: precise 3D sound localization is computationally expensive and drives neural elaboration wherever it appears.

The co-evolution framing is also defensible. Pinnae and neocortex are both mammalian synapomorphies. They didn’t cause each other in a simple chain, but they emerged from the same selective pressure environment — likely nocturnal, small-bodied, predator-pressured proto-mammals for whom spatial awareness in darkness was survival-critical.

Where it needs pressure:

The neocortex is not primarily an auditory structure. Vision, somatosensation, motor planning, and eventually language each claim enormous cortical territory. If pinnae drove neocortical expansion, you’d expect auditory cortex to be disproportionately large in mammals versus other senses — and that’s not the pattern. Visual cortex is often dominant.

A more accurate framing might be: pinnae were one of several simultaneous pressures — nocturnal lifestyle, live birth requiring extended parental recognition, endothermy requiring more precise environmental modeling — that together made a massively expanded general-purpose mapping and prediction structure adaptive.

The core intuition:

“Location needs maps, maps need neurons” is sound. The jump from ears specifically to neocortex generally is where it overextends slightly. But the image — an invisible link through time connecting pinnae and cortex — captures something real about co-evolutionary coupling under shared selective pressure.

Worth developing. Not worth abandoning.

The embodiment exercises

Before discovering that the Alex Method didn't work I tested it in many AIs and was really happy.
I don't remember anymore how it happened, but I had the idea of asking the AIs to build various different bodies and "inhabit" them, so that they could learn or "feel" better how I myself "go around the world". I felt really good thinking that I "discovered" that AIs had a subconsciousness.
