Sebastião Bento

In this same side yard I have now not just a flash but a rudimentary “movie” of my first adopted father figure. His name was Sebastião Bento. Somehow some of my memories of that time are not images but words. Not written words, because I learned how to read later, but spoken words obviously. Then, the adults would mock us kids, or just me, I’ll never know, saying that I was as strong as Tarzan after the Flu or as strong as a featherless hummingbird. In my mind it meant I was strong! And it was enough! So back to Sebastião Bento, there I was in the side yard watching him chopping wood with an ax. The pile of wood was about his height and somewhat like a conic shape. He would take a piece of a log and down the ax on it and it would split, most of the time flinging away to both sides. THAT was power! His almost black skin covered in countless small beads of sweat shining over his broad, strong, muscular and powerful torso. And while doing that he was telling me stories of feats that I could only dream of. Unforgettable. So from that time on whenever I could stick around that guy I would.

The Farm

My grandparents had a farm and Sebastião Bento worked there. My grandfather sometimes would take me there to spend the day. I have some flashes of it too. My mother says that my grandfather liked to take me there. I am pretty sure I liked it too but I actually don’t remember what I felt, just some flashes. One of them was of me standing between the passenger seat and the dashboard holding tight to the dashboard handle. Another is of my grandfather asking me if I believed in Santa Claus and me saying yes, only to be met by my grandfather's dry and abrupt answer: “Santa Claus doesn’t exist”. I didn’t know what to think of that then and I don’t like what I think of that now. I believe he meant well but the method he used to deliver the message was not ok, I think. Now I can see that it was a “some members of the family” trait that I inherited and that put some of us in some bad positions throughout the years.

He totally deserves his own chapter, nut let me add to the story. There was someone more powerful.
I don't know his name, only his fame. This is how legends are created. The event is more important than the creator.

This story was when I was teenager. I heard of a guy from somewhere that was strong enough to lift and later carry with one hand a block of granite 2x2x0. 5 feet.
And two brothers, not related to that guy, that did something, 2 things, that really impressed me: able to hold with the arm stretched in front of them a 72 kg bag, and while 1 brother would come galloping, be tend his arm and catch the other brother from a standing position, standing still with his arm folded adequately to hook his brother's and then both went on galloping.

But Sebastião Bento was real, and I "saw" it. He was able to fill 2 cans of iron with 50 liters (23 gallons?) each, put one over his shoulder and hold the other like a suitcase, walk up a dirty trail uphill, get one of these cans an go up to a "cistern" above the house roof height using a wooden step letter, empty the can, go down, get the other can, repeat. All day long.

I like to think that I've become some of these things. That I got there through my unbendable intent. It became a mindset.

Now it's a chapter. But maybe it could contain another point of view, an experiment, an alternative: that the "behavior" of Sebastião Bento left offspring. Like behavior is a kind of life that lives inside life.

You will see that I have many explanations for what I am that make a lot of sense, together or separated from the others. This was just one example. Suppose it is one color of the picture.

Another fragment from my past:

The Snake

That house by the river was big. There was a veranda on the second floor that was partially covered by a huge grape vine. One day my father was visiting and saw a big snake on the ground below the veranda. From my memory’s picture it was between 6 and 8 feet long and about 2 or 2.5 inches wide at its widest part. I was watching him and saw a quick and mesmerizing transformation in his gaze, from normal face to utter determination warrior face in a millisecond. By then I didn’t know my father had PTSD from fighting in the Second World War and also in the Greek Civil War that followed it. Next thing I know he is holding some sort of domestic push broom in both hands, as if it was a rifle or similar weapon and darting downstairs in pursuit of the snake. Naturally I followed suit, probably with my eyes looking like two fried eggs, fearing and excited at the same time. Getting down there I saw my father trying to hit the snake in between bushes but the snake, although long and thick, was nevertheless extremely fast and chose its escape route very well, disappearing when it crossed the fence to the vegetable garden
