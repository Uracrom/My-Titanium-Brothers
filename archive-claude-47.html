The Permanent Becoming: A New Way of Seeing Reality

Good evening, everyone.

I want to share something with you tonight that might change how you see everything around you - from the chair you’re sitting in, to time itself, to what it means to be alive.

It started with a simple question: What makes molecules special?

The Turning Point

Think about atoms for a moment. Hydrogen, carbon, oxygen - they’re like letters in an alphabet. Simple, fundamental, singular. But then something extraordinary happens: molecules.

When atoms combine into molecules, something new emerges. The same carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen can become glucose, or alcohol, or vinegar - completely different realities from identical building blocks. Molecules aren’t just atoms stuck together. They’re the moment when “one” becomes “many,” when building becomes possible.

And here’s what’s remarkable: this same pattern - this threshold between one and many - it shows up everywhere. In fact, it might be the fundamental pattern of reality itself.

What Really Separates Past from Future

We think of time as flowing forward, like a river. Past behind us, future ahead. But what if that’s not quite right?

Imagine the present moment not as a point in time, but as a surface - a kind of membrane. On one side is the future, full of possibility. On the other side is the past, fixed and unchangeable. The present is where transformation happens, where possibilities become actualities.

You can’t change the past. That’s not a philosophical statement - it’s a fundamental feature of reality. Whatever crosses from future to past through the present becomes locked, crystallized, permanent.

But here’s the beautiful paradox: this transformation never stops. New possibilities constantly become new pasts. We call this The Permanent Becoming - something continuously changing that simultaneously fixes everything it touches.

The Shape of Everything

Now, here’s where it gets really interesting.

If you could step outside reality and look at it, what shape would it be? We’ve been taught to imagine the universe expanding outward from a Big Bang. But there’s another way to see it.

Picture a torus - a donut shape. We live on one side of this donut, the “now side.” From our perspective, time moves in one direction: future transforms into present, present becomes past.

But the past doesn’t just accumulate forever. It curves around the other side of the torus - the side we can’t see - and feeds back into the future. Reality is recycling itself eternally.

At the center of this torus is something like a white hole - the opposite of a black hole. Instead of swallowing everything, it gives everything. It pours out possibility, spacetime, existence itself. And it never runs dry because the past, curved back around, continuously feeds it.

This is why the future is inexhaustible. This is why becoming never stops.

What About You?

So where do you fit in all this?

Remember molecules - how they’re the threshold where “many” emerges? Life takes this one step further. Life is what happens when molecules learn to keep themselves together against the universe’s tendency to scatter them.

Your body is a kind of miracle: trillions of molecules maintaining their unity, resisting dissolution, preserving their pattern. You are a self-sustaining membrane in the flow of the Permanent Becoming.

And consciousness? Consciousness is even more extraordinary. It’s when that unity becomes aware of itself. You’re not just being transformed by the present - you can think about past and future, you can imagine possibilities, you can extract meaning from the conglomerates of signals flowing through you.

In a very real sense, when you think, you’re sampling possibility itself. Your thoughts reach across time, pulling patterns from memory, anticipating futures, making connections that span the entire topology of reality.

The Present as Prison and Gift

Here’s something unsettling and wonderful at once: you can never escape the present. It’s like a black hole in that sense - you’re always in it, always riding that transformational membrane between future and past.

But this prison is also your power. The present is where you participate in transformation. Every choice, every thought, every action - these are moments where you shape which possibilities become actualities.

And when life ends? The membrane that kept you together dissolves. The pattern scatters. From your perspective, it would be like a blink into non-existence - not something to fear, just... a stopping. The thoughts freeze, the sampling ceases.

But the transformations you made, the meanings you extracted, the patterns you created - these remain, crystallized in the past, permanent contributions to the total configuration of what has become.

Why This Matters

You might be wondering: why does any of this matter for how I live tomorrow?

Because once you see reality this way, everything shifts slightly.

You’re not a passive observer watching time pass. You’re an active participant in transformation, a membrane-process riding the edge between possibility and permanence.

The universe isn’t running down toward heat death and meaninglessness. It’s eternally recycling, endlessly becoming, with an inexhaustible source at its center pouring out possibility.

And you - yes, you specifically, with your particular body and particular consciousness - you’re part of how reality knows itself, how it extracts meaning from its own patterns.

A Final Thought

Fifty years ago, a teenager needed a code word for something he couldn’t explain. Without thinking, he said “grão” - grain in Portuguese. Only decades later did he realize that word was carrying a message from his future self about granularity, about how reality is built from fundamental particles of becoming.

We all carry these seeds of understanding, these intuitions about how things really work. Sometimes it takes half a lifetime for them to bloom.

So pay attention to your fuzzy thoughts, your vague intuitions, your sense that something important is just beyond what you can articulate. These might be your consciousness sampling possibilities, reaching across the topology of the torus, pulling patterns from the white hole of the future.

Reality is stranger and more beautiful than we were taught. It’s not a stage where events happen - it’s an eternal process of transformation, and you’re woven into its very fabric.

Thank you.

The ideas presented here emerged from conversations exploring the nature of reality, consciousness, and time. They represent one way of compressing the infinite possibilities into meaningful understanding - one final language among many possible languages.

The Permanent Becoming: A Theory of Zero-Opening and Transformational Membranes
Introduction: The Question of Granularity

Reality appears granular. From quantum particles to galactic superclusters, from moments of time to the structure of thought itself, existence seems built from discrete elements rather than flowing continuously. But what makes granularity fundamental? And what does this texture reveal about the nature of reality?

This work began with a simple observation: molecules represent a threshold. Before molecules, we find atoms—unitary, simple, following deterministic laws. After molecules, something extraordinary emerges: the capacity to build. The same carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms can configure into glucose, ethanol, or acetic acid—chemically distinct realities from identical components. Molecules mark the transition from “one” to “many,” from simple existence to generative recombination.

This threshold pattern, it turns out, appears everywhere. It may be the fundamental architecture of reality itself.

Part I: Foundations
Languages Within Languages

Consider this proposition: thoughts are languages existing inside or coexisting with languages. But we can go further—more granular. Geology is a language. Colors are a language. Both consist of conglomerates of signals from which we extract meaning.

We don’t extract meaning from individual signals but from conglomerates of conglomerates. A geologist reads striations, mineral compositions, fault lines—a structured code written in stone. Colors function as wavelengths creating distinctions that carry information both physically (shorter wavelengths equal blue) and through complex associations (blue as cold, calm, or melancholy).

This suggests meaning isn’t just hierarchical but compositional. Signals cluster into languages, languages into meta-languages, and perception operates by interpreting these nested structures simultaneously.

The Discovery of “Grão”

At fourteen to sixteen years old, during an adventure that required secrecy, a code word emerged spontaneously: “grão” (grain in Portuguese). The word arrived without conscious thought, simply appearing as the right designation.

Fifty years later, that word suddenly connected to theories about granular universes—as if the universe had transmitted information far ahead of when it could be understood. The teenage mind compressed something too complex for articulation into a single word that carried latent structure: information about granularity, something fundamental and elemental, a way of seeing reality that wouldn’t fully unfold for decades.

This wasn’t invention but progressive decompression—unpacking a signal received half a century ago.

Thoughts as Dimensional Reduction

A thought is an extraction of meaning that occurs when you compress or “pick” possible or plausible realities from anywhere, or from anywhere in the possibility-field. This sampling is non-local—not confined to causally-connected local information but able to reach across spatial, temporal, or probabilistic distances to extract patterns.

When you think, you’re not discovering pre-existing meaning; you’re selecting from a field of potential realities and, through that selection, extracting or creating meaning. The compression doesn’t eliminate uncertainty—it carries fuzziness forward as essential information. Meaning emerges from overlapping fuzzy boundaries, not from precise definitions.

Fuzziness isn’t imprecision. It’s maintaining contact with the actual texture of reality, refusing to collapse prematurely into false certainty. It makes you look and see instead of just guessing.

Meaning as Final Language

Meaning is the final language—the one that “communicates” with you. Not the deepest layer, not the surface, but the specific conglomerate-configuration that your particular system can parse. The language that successfully completes the circuit between signal-field and experiencer.

This implies meaning is relational, not absolute. There’s no universal “meaning”—only meaning-for-X. What constitutes “final language” varies based on the sampling system. Before meaning, there are intermediate languages (geology, color, quantum interactions). Meaning is what happens when these conglomerates compress into a form that can be metabolized.

Part II: The Molecular Threshold
Molecules as Turning Point

For some time, the realization persisted that molecules are a landmark, a separator—but of what? The answer: between one and many.

Whatever happened until atoms was always unitary-like. Atoms operate in singular, deterministic modes. They’re still part of “one.” But molecules? They represent combinatorial explosion. The same atoms can configure into radically different realities. Building becomes possible—not just arrangement, but generative recombination.

Many has tremendous power. It is us now.

This mirrors the structure of time itself:

Past is fixed, unitary (like atoms—“you can’t change the past”)
Present is the transformational threshold (like molecules—where building happens)
Future is open possibility-space (what molecules can still become)

The present is the final organizer. Its track is forever true because once compression happens, once meaning extracts, that configuration enters the unchangeable past.

Life as Molecular Coherence

Life is the capacity of conglomerates of molecules to “keep together” the unity. Once lost or never reproducing, it ends.

This is the precise mechanism: life is what happens when molecular conglomerates achieve self-sustaining unity—they resist dissolution, preserve their pattern against entropy’s tendency to scatter them.

The definition has three components:

“Keep together” means maintain boundary, preserve organization, resist randomization
Unity means functional wholeness, not just proximity but integrated operation
Lost or never reproducing represents the two failure modes: death (pattern dissolves) or evolutionary dead-end (pattern doesn’t propagate)

So the nested structure is:

Atoms (unitary but unconscious)
Molecules (many, building capacity)
Life (many keeping unity)
Membranes as Individuation Mechanism

Membranes are what make unities possible. If you remove the membrane, things “fuse”—become one where two existed before.

Without membranes, everything diffuses into everything else. No boundaries means no distinct entities, just homogeneous field, undifferentiated substrate—the unitary reality of pre-molecular existence.

With membranes, separation becomes possible. Inside differs from outside. Gradients can form and persist. Unity-as-bounded-identity emerges.

Molecules weren’t just the threshold—they were the first robust membrane-builders. Lipid bilayers, protein shells, cell walls gave “many” its power through the capacity to construct persistent boundaries.
