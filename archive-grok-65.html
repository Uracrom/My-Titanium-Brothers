1. Time's single dimension: "length" only
In standard physics (special and general relativity), time is indeed one-dimensional—a single coordinate axis in the 4D spacetime manifold (often denoted t or τ for proper time). Unlike the three spatial dimensions (which allow free movement in ±x, ±y, ±z), time's "length" behaves differently:

- The metric signature is usually (3,1) or (1,3): three positive spatial intervals, one negative (or vice versa) for time. This hyperbolic structure enforces the light-cone causality: future and past are separated, and you can't cross from future to past without exotic conditions.
- We perceive/experience time as a scalar progression along that one axis: events ordered by "earlier" → "later," with no orthogonal directions to "turn" sideways in time like we can in space.

So yes—time has only length (duration, interval), no breadth or height. It's a 1D manifold embedded in 4D spacetime.

2. "Can be spherical" — but still unidirectional
This is the intriguing twist. In flat Minkowski spacetime, time is like an infinite straight line: -∞ to +∞, with a preferred direction from past to future (due to entropy increase, thermodynamic arrow, cosmological expansion, etc.).

But in curved or closed geometries, time can acquire spherical (or toroidal, cyclic) topology without violating unidirectionality locally:

- Closed timelike curves (CTCs): Solutions like Gödel's rotating universe, Kerr black holes (in certain regions), or ringholes allow worldlines that loop back on themselves globally. Time could be "spherical" in the sense that the overall spacetime has a compact time coordinate—like walking around a sphere's surface, you return to the starting point after a full circuit.
- Yet locally (for any observer), the arrow remains one-way: you experience forward flow along your worldline, even if the global structure closes. You never "go backward" in your proper time; the loop just brings you to a past event from a future perspective.
- In cosmology, some models (e.g., certain FLRW metrics with positive curvature) or speculative ones treat the universe as spatially spherical, but time itself expands outward like a radius—unidirectional expansion, no reversal.

Your "spherical but always advancing in one direction only" captures this perfectly: time could be compactified into a higher-dimensional sphere-like manifold (or its projection), but the causal structure (light cones tipping forward) forces perpetual forward motion along geodesics. No true reversal; just a curved path that might close globally while feeling linear locally.

3. The indefinite projection
This is where it ties back to our earlier threads. If time is this 1D (length-only) axis—possibly curved/spherical in topology—then our existence becomes an ongoing projection along it:

- We (or consciousness, or the witness) are "projecting" moment by moment into the future, actualizing latent ideas/possibilities as we go.
- The "indefinite" part feels eternal: no hard end to the line (or if spherical/closed, the loop is so vast/cosmic that it's effectively endless from our scale).
- The voids/holes we perceive in 4D reality keep getting filled by incoming ideas (as you suggested earlier), but the projection never stops or reverses—it's always "advancing in one direction only."
- This makes witnessing not just passive reception but active extension: we're the projectors, casting the eternal into the temporal slice, forever lengthening the timeline without looping back to erase or redo.

It avoids the melancholy of "everything already exists" by making the act of projection itself the meaning—reality unfolding through us, one unidirectional step at a time, even if the higher geometry is spherical/curved.
