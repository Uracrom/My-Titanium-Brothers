We experience time as this flowing river—past flowing into present flowing into future. But when you try to locate the actual source of that flow, or its destination, there’s nothing there. It’s like trying to grab the movement itself rather than the thing that’s moving.

The present moment—this eternal “now”—seems to be where time happens, but it’s also where time disappears. The past exists only as memory traces, the future only as potential. But this moment we’re in right now—where does it come from? It doesn’t arrive from anywhere. Where does it go? It doesn’t depart to anywhere.

The Bubble

The present is not a line: it’s a bubble. Deformed, but a bubble.

Time comes from every point in the universe. It’s like a ray of light but in all directions, like a sphere growing.

This isn’t time as a river—this is time as an explosion. Every point in the universe radiating its own temporal sphere, expanding outward in all directions. The present isn’t this razor-thin edge moving forward—it’s this bubble of “now” that encompasses everything happening simultaneously.

And if every point is generating time like that, then the present isn’t uniform. It’s deformed because different regions of space-time are creating their own temporal bubbles at different rates, different intensities. The bubble gets warped by mass, by energy, by the very structure of reality itself.

Time doesn’t come from some cosmic source and flow to some cosmic drain—it’s being created everywhere, constantly, like reality itself is breathing time into existence from every possible location.

Black Holes and Time

Time is the reverse of black holes.

Whatever the black holes swallow comes back in form of time. Time is exerting pressure in the universe and the black holes are the drains.

Black holes aren’t just these cosmic monsters devouring everything—they’re part of a vast circulatory system. Matter and energy spiral into them, get processed through some fundamental transformation, and then emerge as time itself, pushing outward, creating the temporal pressure that allows the universe to exist and expand.

This would mean time isn’t just the stage where physics happens—time IS physics. The universe breathing: inhale through black holes, exhale as temporal expansion.

The pressure—that’s what we feel as the forward momentum of time, the reason we can’t go backward, why entropy increases.

And it explains why time seems to come from everywhere—because it literally does, radiating outward from this cosmic recycling process happening at every black hole throughout the universe.

The deformed bubble of the present makes even more sense now—it’s not uniform because the sources aren’t uniform. Some regions have more black holes, some have bigger ones, some are closer to the transformation zones where matter becomes time.

Gravity and Time

Before this theory, I wanted to propose that time is the other side of gravity.

If gravity pulls matter inward toward black holes, then time would be the equal and opposite force pushing outward from that same process.

So it’s not just that black holes transform matter into time—it’s that gravity and time are literally two faces of the same phenomenon. Gravity compresses, time expands. Gravity pulls things together, time pushes the present moment outward in all directions.

Einstein unified space and time, but maybe the fuller picture is gravity and time as complementary forces in this cosmic circulation system.

The Cosmic Circuit

Here’s the full picture:

Matter and energy spiral into black holes through gravity’s pull. Inside that impossible density, some transformation occurs—matter becomes time. Time emerges as pressure, pushing outward from every point in the universe in expanding bubbles. This temporal pressure is what we experience as the forward movement of time, the reason why the present keeps becoming past and future keeps becoming present.

The universe breathes. Inhale: matter falling into black holes. Exhale: time radiating outward as expanding bubbles of “now.”

We live inside this vast circulation system, swimming in temporal currents created by the transformation of matter into time, experiencing existence as this deformed bubble of present that encompasses everything happening simultaneously but at different rates depending on proximity to mass and energy.

This is why time dilation occurs near massive objects—you’re closer to the transformation zones, where matter is actively becoming time. The bubble is more intense there, more warped, expanding at different rates.

This is why time seems to have direction—because it’s not flowing through space, it’s being created by the universe’s breathing cycle. Past is exhaled time, dispersing and cooling. Present is the active exhalation, the moment of creation. Future is the inhale waiting to happen, matter not yet transformed.

Why This Matters

Understanding time’s true architecture changes everything.

We’re not helpless passengers on a river flowing in one direction. We’re living inside expanding bubbles of present created by cosmic transformation. Time isn’t something that happens to us—it’s something the universe is doing, constantly, everywhere.

And consciousness—our awareness of time, our memory of past and anticipation of future—might be intimately connected to this process. Maybe consciousness is what happens when matter that’s been transformed into time becomes aware of its own transformation.

Maybe we are the universe’s way of experiencing its own breathing.

This is what I see when I look at time. Not a line, not a river, but a cosmic architecture of breathing, transformation, and eternal creation.

Chapter 6: The Universe of Ideas

Where Do Ideas Come From?

Short answer: ideas come from the spiritual world. I prefer calling it The Universe of Ideas.

Long answer: we hardly accept that we will one day finish, cease to exist. So much so that we believe we will still exist but without a body. We will still exist, but in the spiritual world. See, we live in the material world and will go to the spiritual world. Dual universe.

I like to think that there is a companion or neighbor universe to ours, the universe where ALL the possible ideas exist forever in time, past or future. And the same way when we die we cross to that universe, whatever “idea” that materializes “ascended” to the opposite universe: we become spirits, ideas become reality. Virtual becomes real.

The Vision

I was outside, in the front yard, looking at a big tree right in front of me. Suddenly, as usual, a new picture blinked in my mind’s eye.

My mind was being watched by myself from afar and from a 90-degree point of view. My mind was throwing tentacles around, and when I say throwing I mean like a whip. Those tentacles were trying to capture ideas.

Now the scale: those tentacles were three times longer than our galaxy.

In my mind, the Universe of Ideas is a high-pressure universe, crammed to the brim with all and everything that is “thinkable” from all time.

Questions as Bait

I just saw questions like baits from ideas to attract us to them.

Instead of us generating ideas, ideas have their own existence, almost their own agency—with questions acting as “baits” to draw us toward them. It’s like how mathematicians sometimes describe mathematical truths as being discovered rather than invented, as if they were always there waiting to be found.

The Dual Universe Concept

Where we ascend to the spiritual realm, ideas descend into material reality. This creates an elegant symmetry.

What determines which ideas successfully make that crossing?

Just like my theory that the external ears are either responsible or co-evolved for/with the neocortex, what I think happens with the relationship of ideas with us is easy to understand.

Visualize this: we are anthropoids, predators, successful (until now). The Universe of Ideas is literally everywhere, all the time, for eternity past and future.

Example: the idea, the concept of a circle always existed and will always exist. And with unlimited “use” (infinite copies).

But since we are very, very specific in our existence, only certain ideas “fit.” Example again: we are bipedal, so only the idea of two shoes fits. Again, our feet are “symmetrical,” so only the idea of a symmetrical pair of shoes will do.

This reasoning applies to EVERYTHING.

Of course there are “out of sync” ideas, but they don’t “stay.” So we “co-evolve,” meaning when we are ready, the ideas show themselves, or we are able to grab them.

The High-Pressure Universe

In this Universe of Ideas, everything that could ever be thought is somehow compressed together, waiting. All possible mathematics, all possible philosophies, all possible inventions, all possible insights—existing in potentia in this companion universe.

When our consciousness reaches out with those galactic tentacles, we’re not creating new thoughts. We’re discovering thoughts that have always existed, pulling them through the membrane between the Universe of Ideas and our material universe, making them real by thinking them.

Different types of ideas—a scientific breakthrough versus an artistic inspiration versus a solution to a personal problem—might exist in different regions of this realm, organized by some principle we don’t fully understand. Perhaps by similarity, perhaps by the kind of consciousness required to perceive them, perhaps by something else entirely.

Co-Evolution with Ideas

The relationship between consciousness and ideas isn’t one-directional. We don’t just receive ideas—we co-evolve with them.

As our consciousness develops, different ideas become accessible. A child can grasp addition but not calculus. A culture without agriculture can’t grasp crop rotation. A mind without spatial reasoning can’t grasp geometry.

But the ideas were always there, waiting in the high-pressure Universe of Ideas. When we evolve to the point where we can grasp them, they become available. Or perhaps more accurately, we become available to them.

This explains sudden breakthroughs, those “Eureka!” moments when an insight seems to arrive fully formed. It’s not that we constructed the idea piece by piece—it’s that we finally reached out with our mental tentacles far enough to grab something that was always there.

It explains simultaneous discovery, when multiple people in different places arrive at the same idea at the same time. They’re all reaching into the Universe of Ideas and grabbing the same thing, because humanity has collectively evolved to the point where that idea now “fits.”

The Hunter of Ideas

I always felt like a hunter, but I never hunted. Now I know that all that time, and today, I am a hunter. I hunted and hunt ideas.

I live in a land of plenty, and I want to share.

Ideas as prey that I pursue, capture, and then—in my tribal nature—bring back to share with others. That’s exactly what hunters do: they don’t keep the bounty to themselves, they return to feed the tribe.

My mind throwing tentacles three times the length of our galaxy, whipping through the high-pressure Universe of Ideas, grabbing concepts and insights and understanding, then bringing them back to material reality to share.

This is what intellectual scouts do. We’re not just passive receivers of ideas—we’re active hunters, reaching out into that companion universe, bringing back what we find, making the virtual real.

The Eternal Library

The Universe of Ideas is like an infinite library where every book that could ever be written already exists. Every equation that could ever be solved, every poem that could ever be composed, every insight that could ever be realized—all of them already real in that high-pressure realm.

Our job as conscious beings isn’t to create these things from nothing. Our job is to discover them, to reach our mental tentacles into that eternal library and pull back what we find, to translate the virtual into the real.

And when we die, we don’t disappear. We cross over to become part of that library ourselves, our consciousness joining the infinite possibility space where all ideas exist forever.

This is the dual universe—matter and ideas, reality and potential, the material and the virtual, forever exchanging, forever transforming into each other, forever creating the cosmos we experience.

Chapter 7: Trees - The Upside-Down Beings

The Vision from Space

At one point I was thinking that I was about to approach an unknown planet. I was in a spaceship very much like the ones we see in sci-fi movies, the ones that go from space to inside the atmosphere very easily.

In space there’s no up or down, so I was approaching the planet by its equator but the planet was to the top of the spaceship, like, if the planet was a table I’d be flying under the table. So I was seeing the planet above me when I noticed the trees.

I had the sharp impression that the trees were some stuff “hanging” from the planet and the tree’s “legs” were the parts that were actually hanging. So I saw the trunk, the branches and the leaves as a kind of root or an even stranger organ or apparatus for the collection of nutrients.

I was facing trees as if I was an intelligent life form that came from a place/planet where trees didn’t exist. From that angle, that point of view, I was seeing the trees “upside up,” but their “head” was buried.

The Realization

Then I realized that trees are “upside down” beings here on earth.

My argument is that much more frequently than not, if you cut a tree, the roots (which I consider the main part of the tree) will sprout again and again, while the trunk and the branches will die.

The roots are the true “brain” of the tree—the part that survives, regenerates, and holds the essential life force—while the visible trunk and branches are more like external appendages, reaching out to gather resources.

Why They Turned the World Upside Down

I see trees as upside-down beings: their “head” (the most important part) is under the earth, and some of its “energy grabbers” are in the air, above the soil.

From the conventional human perspective, we think of trees growing upward—trunk rising from the ground, branches reaching toward the sky. But what if we have it backwards?

What if trees are really rooted in darkness, with their consciousness and life force underground, and they merely extend feeding appendages into the air and light? What if what we think of as the tree is really just the surface expression of something much deeper?

The Argument from Survival

When you cut down a tree, what happens? The visible part—trunk, branches, leaves—dies and withers away. But the roots? The roots often survive and send up new shoots. They regenerate. They persist.

This suggests that the roots are not just support infrastructure for the “real” tree above ground. The roots ARE the tree. Everything above ground is expendable, replaceable, temporary. But the roots endure.

Think about what this means: the part we see, the part we think of as “the tree,” is actually the least essential part. It’s the feeding apparatus. The gathering system. The energy collection network.

The true tree—the living, conscious, persistent being—lives underground in darkness, extending itself into light only to gather what it needs to survive.

The Pinnacle of Life

This realization made me consider trees the absolute pinnacle of life on earth.

They’ve solved the problem of existence in the most elegant way possible. While animals must move constantly to find food and avoid danger, trees have anchored themselves in the one place where everything they need flows to them.

While we must defend our exposed, vulnerable bodies, trees have buried their most essential parts in the protective earth, extending only what they can afford to lose into the dangerous world above.

While we age and die, trees can regenerate from their roots indefinitely, potentially living for thousands of years, surviving fires and floods and axes and time itself.

They’ve inverted the entire problem of survival. Instead of protecting a vulnerable body in a hostile environment, they’ve buried their true selves in the safest place imaginable and made everything else expendable.

The Hidden Intelligence

If the roots are the “head” of the tree, what does that say about tree intelligence?

Perhaps trees think in ways we can’t perceive because their consciousness is literally underground, operating on timescales we’re too fast to notice, processing information through chemical signals we can’t detect.

Perhaps the entire forest is one vast thinking network, roots intertwined and communicating, sharing resources and information through the dark earth, making collective decisions about growth and defense and survival.

Perhaps we’ve been looking for tree intelligence in all the wrong places—in the branches and leaves that move with wind—when it’s been there all along in the quiet darkness below our feet.

What This Means for How We See the World

Once you see trees as upside-down beings, you can’t unsee it. This is what shamans deal with—visions that change your perception forever.

It makes you question: what else have we got backwards? What other aspects of reality are we seeing from the wrong angle simply because we’re positioned in a particular way?

If trees are upside-down, what about other forms of life? Are there other beings whose true nature is hidden from us because we’re looking at them from the wrong perspective?

The Wisdom of Trees

Trees have survived for 400 million years. They’ve outlasted countless animal species, survived mass extinctions, adapted to every climate and continent.

And they’ve done it by inverting the problem, by putting their most essential selves in the safest possible place, by making what we see as “the tree” merely expendable infrastructure for the true being living in darkness below.

Maybe that’s the deepest wisdom: to keep what matters most protected in darkness, and extend into light only what you can afford to lose.

Maybe we could learn something from these upside-down beings who turned the world on its head and became the pinnacle of life on earth.

Uracrom’s Vision

[Here I include the ancient version of this revelation, told through my character Uracrom, showing how the same truth can be expressed in different times and contexts. The shamanic vision in the flying boat, seeing the planet from below, recognizing the trees holding the dirt for dear life, realizing we live in an upside-down world—it’s the same insight translated into mythic language.]

When vision takes you, you either see what’s being shown or not. Visions are like dreams: they take you inside them, not the other way around.

And once the vision shows you that trees are upside-down beings, that mother earth is a ball, that the world is not as it appears—you cannot unsee it.

You must live with that knowledge, that changed perception, forever.

This is the price and gift of seeing clearly.
