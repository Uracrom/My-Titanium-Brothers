The mind is already running a sparse, high-compression codec on reality; the language simply inherits that codec.

It's the same reason good mathematicians or systems engineers can explain extremely complex things in surprisingly few words: the conceptual skeleton is clean and rigid, so the verbal covering can be thin.

Does that match how it feels from the inside — like the words are almost forced to be sparse because the structure won't tolerate extra mass? Or is there another layer to it (e.g., a deliberate stylistic choice on top of the structural habit)?
