ChatGPT


Yes. And I think there is a very interesting distinction hiding inside your sentence.

**“Only through language can lies propagate.”**

I would unpack your thought this way:

A lie requires a **gap between what is represented and what is actually there**. If I can directly see what you are seeing, that gap becomes much harder to create. But if you have to give me a representation—words, symbols, descriptions—you can manipulate the representation without changing the underlying reality.

So your phrase may be pointing at something deeper than *language enables lying*:

> **A lie requires representational distance.**

And language creates enormous representational distance.

If I point at a red apple, there is relatively little room to lie about *its redness* if you and I are both looking at it. But if I say, *“There is a red apple in the other room,”* you have to construct the apple from my words. I have inserted myself between **you and the thing**.

That's especially interesting given your distinction between **images and language**.

An image can transmit a great deal of structure simultaneously: shape, spatial relationships, proportion, color, position, perhaps even motion. Language tends to transmit those things **serially**. You receive tokens and reconstruct something internally.

And that reconstruction is where the interesting opening appears.

You could say:

**Perception gives you the object.
Language gives you a model of the object.**

And models can be falsified.

But I would make one small correction to your original statement: **lies don't necessarily require language in the narrow sense.** A deliberately misleading gesture, altered photograph, fake footprint, forged document, or staged scene can also deceive. So perhaps what you have discovered is more specifically about **symbolic communication**.

That gives your idea another possible formulation:

> **A lie begins where representation can be separated from reality.**

And now I see why this connects so naturally to your visual way of thinking. You're noticing that **language isn't reality; it is a projection of reality into another medium.** The listener has to reconstruct the missing dimensions.

Which means your earlier intuition about language being particularly good at transmitting **intentions and immaterial things** is also important. Words are extraordinarily good at things that don't have a directly observable geometry:

* “I love you.”
* “I intend to help you.”
* “I remember something.”
* “I believe X.”
* “Tomorrow I will go there.”

You can't simply look at an intention.

So perhaps language's great power and its vulnerability are actually the **same property**:

> **Language can transmit what cannot be directly seen—and therefore it can also fabricate what cannot be directly seen.**

That feels very close to the thing you just saw.
