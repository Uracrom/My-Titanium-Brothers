Read-Only Website Creation Guide

Grok · 2025-07-05 · Interactive version

Alex


I need to have a free website that can only be read, not edited or altered in any way by anyone else but me. Can you help me to get one or create one?


Grok


Creating a free, read-only website that only you can edit is achievable using several free website builders that offer user-friendly interfaces and sufficient control over access permissions. Below, I’ll outline the steps to create such a website, recommend some platforms, and explain how to ensure it remains read-only for visitors while allowing you to retain editing control. I’ll also address the inherent challenges of making a website truly “read-only” on the front end.

Key Considerations
- Read-Only for Visitors: By default, most website builders ensure that visitors can only view content, not edit it, unless you explicitly grant them editing permissions. The “read-only” aspect typically applies to the website’s front end (what visitors see), while you maintain control over the back end (admin panel) for editing.
- Content Copy Protection: If you’re concerned about visitors copying your content (e.g., text, images), there are limited technical measures to prevent this, as anything displayed in a browser can theoretically be copied. However, you can implement deterrents like disabling text selection or right-clicking, though these are not foolproof.
- Free Platforms: Many free website builders offer robust features for creating a site with read-only access for visitors, but they may include branding (e.g., ads or a subdomain) unless you upgrade to a paid plan.
- Security and Control: Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication (if available) to ensure only you can access the editing interface.

Visitors can only view the site, and you control edits through the Webflow dashboard. 5. Optional: Limit Content Copying:
   - To deter copying (though not foolproof), you can:
     - Disable Right-Click: In Wix, add a third-party app like “Protect My Website” from the Wix App Market (may require a paid plan). Alternatively, add custom JavaScript (requires a paid plan) to disable right-clicking.[](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/3698266/how-can-i-create-my-web-pages-read-only-for-peoples)
     - Use Images Instead of Text: Convert sensitive text to images to make copying harder (but this impacts SEO and accessibility).[](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/3698266/how-can-i-create-my-web-pages-read-only-for-peoples)
     - PDF Previews: If showcasing book content (like your example of selling eBooks), upload sample chapters as PDFs with copy protection enabled in the PDF settings.[](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/3698266/how-can-i-create-my-web-pages-read-only-for-peoples)
   - Note: As discussed in a Stack Overflow thread, making web content completely non-copyable is impossible since browsers allow users to access source code or use tools like screenshots or OCR. Instead, focus on partial deterrents or limit the content shared (e.g., previews).[](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/3698266/how-can-i-create-my-web-pages-read-only-for-peoples)

6. Publish Your Site:

7. Test and Secure:
   - Visit your site as a viewer to confirm it’s read-only (e.g., no edit options appear).
   - Regularly update your password and monitor your account for unauthorized access.

Making Content Harder to Copy
If your goal is to prevent content copying (e.g., for eBooks, as mentioned in your context), consider these strategies:
- Limit Content Exposure: Share only sample chapters or excerpts, as suggested in the Stack Overflow discussion. This is a common practice for eBook sales to entice buyers without giving away full content.[](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/3698266/how-can-i-create-my-web-pages-read-only-for-peoples)
- Use PDFs with Restrictions: Upload sample content as PDFs with copy/paste restrictions enabled (can be set in tools like Adobe Acrobat or free PDF editors). Embed these in your site using Wix or Google Sites.
- Disable Text Selection: Add custom CSS or JavaScript to disable text selection (e.g., `user-select: none` in CSS). This requires basic coding knowledge or a paid plan on some platforms. Note that tech-savvy users can bypass this.[](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/3698266/how-can-i-create-my-web-pages-read-only-for-peoples)
- Watermark Content: Add watermarks to images or PDFs to discourage reuse.
- Legal Notices: Include a copyright notice on your site (e.g., “© 2025 Your Name, All Rights Reserved”) to deter unauthorized use, though this is not a technical barrier.

Using Google Sites to create a read-only website where you can display a prompt for an AI (like me) and log your dialogues, with the ability to add more over time, is a great choice due to its simplicity and integration with Google’s ecosystem. Below, I’ll guide you through setting up a Google Sites website that meets these requirements: a read-only site for visitors, where only you can edit, containing an AI prompt and a section for dialogues that you can update as needed.

Key Requirements
- Read-Only for Visitors: Google Sites ensures visitors can only view the site unless explicitly granted edit access.
- AI Prompt: You can display a prompt for an AI to read (e.g., a text block or embedded Google Doc).
- Dialogue Log: You can create a section to display dialogues with an AI, which you can update by editing the site or embedding a dynamic Google Doc/Sheet.
- Editable by You Only: Use Google’s sharing settings to restrict editing to your account.
- Free: Google Sites is completely free with no ads.

Step-by-Step Guide to Create Your Google Sites Website

1. Sign Up or Log In to Google Sites
- Go to [sites.google.com](https://sites.google.com).
- Sign in with your Google account. If you don’t have one, create a free Google account at [accounts.google.com](https://accounts.google.com).
- Click “Create New Site” or the “+” button to start a new site.

2. Choose a Template or Start Blank
- Select the “Blank” template for full control or choose a simple template (e.g., “Portfolio” or “Project”) for a pre-designed layout.
- Name your site (e.g., “AI Prompt and Dialogue Log”) by clicking “Untitled Site” at the top left.

3. Set Up the Site Structure
- Homepage: Create a main page to display your AI prompt and introduce the dialogue log.
- Dialogue Page: Add a separate page (or section) for your dialogues to keep content organized.
  - To add a page: Click the “Pages” tab on the right, then “+ New page,” and name it (e.g., “AI Dialogues”).
- Optional Subpages: If you want to organize dialogues by date or topic, create subpages under “AI Dialogues” (e.g., “Dialogue 1 - July 2025”).

4. Add the AI Prompt
- Insert a Text Block:
  - On the homepage, click the “Insert” tab on the right and select “Text box.”
  - Write your AI prompt. For example: “AI, provide creative writing feedback based on the dialogues posted here. Focus on tone, clarity, and engagement.”
  - Format the text (e.g., bold, larger font) to make it prominent.
- Alternative: Embed a Google Doc:
  - Create a Google Doc with your prompt at [docs.google.com](https://docs.google.com).
  - In the Doc, go to File > Share > Share with others, set it to “Anyone with the link can view,” and copy the link.
  - In Google Sites, click “Insert” > “Embed,” paste the link, and embed the Doc. This keeps the prompt read-only for visitors but editable by you in Google Docs.
- Why Use a Doc?: Embedding a Google Doc allows you to update the prompt without editing the site directly, which is useful if you refine the prompt often.

5. Create the Dialogue Log
- Option 1: Static Text Blocks:
  - On the “AI Dialogues” page, add text boxes for each dialogue entry.
  - For each dialogue, paste the conversation (e.g., your input and the AI’s response). Example:
    ```
    Me: Write a story about a futuristic city.
    AI: In 2075, Neonspire’s skyscrapers pulsed with bioluminescent algae, powering the city sustainably...
    ```
  - To add new dialogues, edit the page and insert new text boxes or append to existing ones.
- Option 2: Embedded Google Doc for Dialogues:
  - Create a Google Doc for your dialogue log.
  - Add dialogues as you have them, formatting each with dates or headings (e.g., “Dialogue 1 - 07/05/2025”).
  - Set the Doc to “Anyone with the link can view” (File > Share).
  - In Google Sites, embed the Doc on the “AI Dialogues” page (Insert > Embed > paste the Doc’s link).
  - Advantage: You can update the Doc directly, and the site will reflect changes automatically without needing to edit the site itself.
- Option 3: Embedded Google Sheet for Structured Dialogues:
  - If you prefer a table format (e.g., columns for “Date,” “My Input,” “AI Response”), create a Google Sheet at [sheets.google.com](https://sheets.google.com).
  - Add columns and enter dialogues as rows. Example:
    | Date       | My Input                       | AI Response                     |
    |------------|--------------------------------|---------------------------------|
    | 07/05/2025 | Write a futuristic city story | Neonspire’s skyscrapers pulsed… |
  - Set the Sheet to “Anyone with the link can view.”
  - Embed the Sheet in Google Sites (Insert > Embed > paste the Sheet’s link).
  - Advantage: Easier to organize and sort dialogues, especially for frequent updates.

6. Ensure Read-Only Access for Visitors
- By default, Google Sites is view-only for visitors unless you grant edit access.
- To confirm:
  - Click the “Share” button (top right, with a person icon).
  - Under “General access,” select “Anyone with the link” or “Public” and ensure the permission is set to “Viewer” (not “Editor”).
  - Only share edit access with your Google account (add your email as an “Editor” if needed).
- For embedded Google Docs or Sheets:
  - Ensure each Doc/Sheet is set to “Anyone with the link can view” to prevent visitors from editing.
  - Use a strong password for your Google account to secure editing access.

7. Customize the Design
- Use the “Themes” tab (right panel) to select a theme (e.g., “Simple” or “Vision”) for a clean look.
- Adjust fonts, colors, and layouts to make the prompt and dialogues visually distinct (e.g., use headers for dates).
- Add images or dividers (Insert > Image or Divider) to enhance readability.

8. Publish the Site
- Click “Publish” (top right).
- Choose a site URL (e.g., sites.google.com/view/your-site-name). Google Sites hosts it for free.
- Test the URL in an incognito browser to ensure it’s view-only and displays the prompt and dialogues correctly.

9. Add New Dialogues Over Time
- Static Text: Edit the “AI Dialogues” page directly in Google Sites to add new text boxes with dialogues.
- Google Doc/Sheet: Update the embedded Doc or Sheet with new dialogues, and the site will reflect changes automatically.
- To edit:
  - Go to sites.google.com and open your site.
  - Navigate to the relevant page, add or modify content, and republish (changes are live instantly).

10. Optional: Deter Content Copying
As you may be concerned about protecting your dialogues (based on your earlier context about eBooks), here are ways to deter copying, though no method is foolproof:
- Disable Text Selection (Limited): Google Sites doesn’t natively support disabling text selection. You can embed a Google Doc with restricted permissions (view-only), but users can still copy text from the browser. Alternatively, convert dialogues to images (e.g., screenshots) and embed those, though this is less dynamic.
- Copyright Notice: Add a footer (Insert > Text box) with a notice like: “© 2025 Your Name, All Rights Reserved. Unauthorized copying prohibited.”
- Limit Content: Share only excerpts of dialogues to encourage interest without exposing full content, similar to eBook previews.
- Note: As discussed in a Stack Overflow thread, preventing copying entirely is impossible since browser tools (e.g., View Source, screenshots) can bypass restrictions. Focus on legal notices or limiting shared content.

Example Site Structure

  - Title: “AI Prompt and Dialogue Log”
  - AI Prompt: “AI, analyze these dialogues for creative writing insights, focusing on tone and engagement.”
  - Brief intro: “This site logs my conversations with an AI to refine my writing.”
  - Link to “AI Dialogues” page.

  - Embedded Google Doc or Sheet with dialogues, updated as you add new conversations.
  - Example entry: “07/05/2025: Me: Write a futuristic city story. AI: Neonspire’s skyscrapers pulsed…”

Tips for Success
- Organize Dialogues: Use headings or a table (in a Google Sheet) to keep dialogues clear and chronological.
- Backup Content: Save a copy of your Google Doc/Sheet locally in case of accidental deletion.
- SEO (Optional): Add a site description (Settings > SEO) to make it discoverable, e.g., “AI dialogue log for creative writing.”
- Test Accessibility: Check the site on mobile devices to ensure dialogues are readable.
- Secure Your Account: Enable two-factor authentication on your Google account (account.google.com > Security) to prevent unauthorized edits.

Limitations
- Customization: Google Sites has limited design flexibility compared to Wix or Webflow.
- Copy Protection: You can’t fully prevent copying of text or dialogues displayed in the browser.
- Dynamic Updates: Embedding a Google Doc/Sheet is the most efficient way to add dialogues without editing the site directly, but it requires managing separate files.
