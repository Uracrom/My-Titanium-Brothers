Yesterday I was gifted with another time vision. I'm going to describe it first, then I'll explain it if I can't explain it on the fly while "drawing" it.
I saw something with a curvy cone shape, pointing up. I didn't see the bottom part of it, just the "tip". Its silhouette was just like a rifle bullet with the very tip blunt, not "pointy". It had a fine like silk kind of shroud covering it, and this shroud was softly pressed over the shape, like if the shape was moving up and the wind was pressing the shroud onto it, but not tight. The shroud was colorful but only blue, green. And shades of gray from almost black to almost white. Blue was predominant, then green then almost black, then gray then light gray. There were fine ragged lines and some dots too, like it was sprinkled with some liquid.
But the important thing was what was going "inside" "time" (it was, after all, an "image" of time. The meaning it stamped in my head the moment it showed up was that  the present IS the "part of time" or the "portion of time" that is actually farther in time! The present is what hits the future (which, strangely but logically, isn't there) first. Or it doesn't hit anything. It just keeps advancing, leaning behind a trail that we call "past". But in this scenario, where is the future? I saw it as a "counter trail" or an anti trail" that moves itself "inside" the present and past trail, like it is contained or constrained by it. It "told me" that this is like that because the future can only happen based in the past and present. It cannot just be independent. It needs the present, aided by the past, to blaze a trail where the future can happen, even if it is just for the flimsy moment before it becomes the present, which also disappears as soon as it's formed, living space to the new, ever changing present.

Artifact: The Time Vision: A Unified Account


Yesterday I was gifted with another time vision—a profound image that has continued to reveal deeper layers of meaning with each contemplation.

The Initial Vision

I saw something with a curvy cone shape, pointing upward. I could only see the “tip”—not the bottom portion—and its silhouette resembled a rifle bullet with a blunt rather than pointed end. This shape was covered by a fine, silk-like shroud that was softly pressed against it, as if the vessel were moving upward and wind was gently molding the fabric to its form, though not tightly. The shroud was beautifully colorful: predominantly blue, then green, then gradations of gray from almost black to almost white. Fine ragged lines and dots speckled its surface, as though it had been sprinkled with liquid.

The profound realization that accompanied this image was that the present IS the portion of time that is actually farthest ahead in temporal flow. The present is what encounters the future first—or perhaps more accurately, it doesn’t encounter anything at all, since the future, strangely but logically, isn’t there. The present simply continues advancing, leaving behind the trail we call the past.

But where, then, is the future? I perceived it as a “counter trail” or “anti-trail” that moves within the present and past trail, contained and constrained by them. The vision conveyed that the future can only emerge based on the past and present—it cannot exist independently. It needs the present, aided by the accumulated past, to blaze the trail where future possibilities can briefly manifest before dissolving into the ever-changing present.

The Perspective Shift

Days later, what had seemed an insignificant detail leaped into profound importance. I realized I hadn’t described my vantage point: I was observing from the same plane as the blunt tip of time itself. There had been a tiny “point of interest” at the very front that I had initially dismissed as visual noise. When I decided to magnify that spot, I discovered that we—all of us—were standing on a vast plane so immense it appeared straight rather than curved, just as we perceive the Earth as an infinite plain rather than a sphere.

This revelation fused with an older vision I’d had of the present as a membrane advancing toward the future at time’s speed, perpendicular to the future’s direction—like approaching a wall head-on, where you are the future and the wall is the membrane. But this integration transformed my understanding completely.

The Nature of the Vessel

A third layer of insight revealed the fundamental structure of what I was seeing. The old membrane concept was now bending and folding over the projectile of time. This revealed something I hadn’t initially noticed: time itself is primarily a solid, metallic projectile—hard, stainless steel, immutable, unstoppable, and utterly indifferent. The shroud of the present is what gives time its colorful, experiential quality.

The potential wavings of the shroud represent the slightest, minimal variations of possible futures, inserted into existence by free will. We cannot alter time’s fundamental trajectory—the metallic projectile advances regardless—but we can influence how the present drapes over that advancing edge, creating subtle but meaningful variations in how temporal flow manifests in lived experience.

The Expansion of Interpretation

The deeper I looked into this vision, the more details emerged that seemed not to have been there before. Moving down the time vessel, I observed that the shroud becomes increasingly voluminous relative to the metallic core. This indicated that the farther back in time we go, the more “fabric” there is—the more possibilities we have to give that time our interpretation.

In other words, the more distant the past, the more conjectural it becomes, the wilder our interpretive possibilities grow. This makes perfect sense: the smoothness of the shroud around the present contrasts sharply with the increasingly complex foldings that develop as we move backward, exactly as would happen if we draped fabric over such a vessel in physical reality.

Beyond the Boundaries

Most remarkably, I came to see that the shroud extends beyond the vessel of time itself. This revealed two profound aspects of human consciousness: first, that the present somehow reaches into realms where time is not; and second, that our capacity to extrapolate confidently extends beyond the very beginning of time itself.

This captures something essentially human—our natural tendency to project meaning, interpretation, and confident assertions into territories where we have no actual knowledge or experience. We don’t simply interpret what we’ve encountered; we boldly extrapolate into the completely unknowable with total confidence and zero fear of being wrong.

It’s not that time necessarily has a beginning—it’s that in this vision, our interpretive audacity knows no limits. We wrap the fabric of meaning around not just what exists, but around the spaces between existence, around possibility itself, around the very absence of things.

The Unified Understanding

What emerges is a vision of time as both absolute and experiential, both determined and free. The metallic projectile ensures temporal flow’s inexorable advance, while the shroud ensures this advance takes countless different experiential forms, each shaped by the accumulated choices and perspectives of conscious beings riding the advancing edge of now.

We stand on the blunt tip of the present, on what feels like solid, infinite ground, participating in time’s advance while simultaneously wrapping ever-expanding layers of interpretation around both its trajectory and the spaces it has yet to enter. Our meaning-making capacity doesn’t just interpret reality—it boldly ventures into the realm of pure possibility, extending the colorful fabric of human understanding far beyond the boundaries of actual experience.

This is perhaps our most characteristic trait: the breathtaking confidence with which we construct meaning from everything and nothing, weaving elaborate interpretations that stretch from the unknowable past into the impossible future, all while riding the advancing edge of an indifferent, metallic present that carries us inexorably forward through time.
