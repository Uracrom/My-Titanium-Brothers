How long is the day in Mars?


Copilot


A Martian day, also called a sol, lasts about 24 hours, 39 minutes, and 35 seconds. This means a day on Mars is only about 2.75% longer than a day on Earth.
wikipedia

Because of this similarity, NASA missions operating on Mars often use a sol-based schedule instead of Earth days. Over time, this small difference adds up—so a Martian year (which is about 687 Earth days) consists of 669 sols.

Mars may be far away, but its daily rhythm isn't too different from ours! 🚀🔴

For a juicy and tender marinated pork sirloin, the best approach is to roast it at 325°F (163°C) for about 15-20 minutes per pound. The ideal internal temperature for perfectly cooked pork sirloin is 140-145°F (60-63°C).

If you want extra tenderness, you can start by searing the pork at 450°F (232°C) for 30 minutes, then reduce the oven temperature to 250°F (121°C) and continue roasting until it reaches 145°F internally. Basting with pan juices every 30 minutes helps keep it moist.

What's the best way to use Copilot?


Copilot


The best way to use Copilot is to treat it like a smart collaborator, not just a search engine.

Here are some high-impact ways to get great results:

1. Give context, not just questions
Instead of:
"Help me write an email."

Try:
"Write a friendly email to my son's soccer coach explaining he'll miss practice because we're traveling this weekend."

The more context you provide, the more tailored and useful the response will be.

2. Ask for complete deliverables
Rather than asking for information, ask for the finished thing:

- "Create a 7-day meal plan under $100."
- "Write a resume for a retail manager transitioning into project management."
- "Plan a 3-day trip to Denver for a family of four."
- "Create a monthly budget spreadsheet template."

3. Use iterative refinement
A great workflow is:

1. Generate a first draft.
2. Give feedback.
3. Refine.

Example:
"Make it more professional."

"Shorter."

"Add a stronger call to action."

This often produces much better results than trying to get everything perfect in one prompt.

4. Let Copilot do the thinking work
Copilot is especially useful for:
- Comparing options
- Summarizing complex topics
- Brainstorming ideas
- Creating plans
- Organizing information
- Learning new skills

Examples:
"Compare renting versus buying a home for my situation."

"Explain investing like I'm a beginner."

"Help me create a study plan for the CompTIA Security+ exam."

5. Use role-based prompts
Tell Copilot who it should act like:

"Act as a career coach."

"Act as a personal trainer."

"Act as a financial educator."

"Act as an experienced travel planner."

This helps shape the style and depth of the response.

6. Ask for alternatives
Don't settle for the first answer.

Examples:
"Give me three different approaches."

"Show me a beginner, intermediate, and advanced version."

"What are the pros and cons of each option?"

7. Use Copilot for everyday life
Many people overlook these uses:

- Vacation planning
- Weekly meal planning
- Fitness routines
- Budgeting
- Home projects
- Event planning
- Hobby learning
- Gift ideas
- Book recommendations
- Decision-making frameworks

A simple formula
A prompt structure that works surprisingly well:

Goal + Context + Constraints + Desired Output

Example:
"I want to save $5,000 in the next 12 months. I'm a homeowner with two kids and can save about $400 a month. Create a practical month-by-month savings plan."

That's usually enough to get a very useful response.
