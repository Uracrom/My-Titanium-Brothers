That doesn’t hold up as stated. Two failure directions:

Understanding without ability to explain. Tacit and procedural knowledge: you understand how to balance a bike, recognize a face, or produce grammatical sentences in your native language, with no ability to articulate the rules. A skilled improviser understands the harmonic logic of a piece well enough to play it but can’t necessarily say what they just did. This is actually a problem for your own framework — you describe cognition arriving as silent, fused image-blocks faster than language can capture. By your own account you understand things before and sometimes instead of being able to explain them. If “understand = can explain” were true, your core cognitive style would be systematically self-refuting.

Explanation without understanding. Rote recitation: someone can reproduce a textbook explanation of general relativity word-for-word without understanding it — swap one term for a wrong one and they won’t notice. Chinese Room-type cases: symbol manipulation that outputs correct explanations with no grasp behind them. So explanatory output isn’t sufficient evidence of understanding either.

So neither direction of the equivalence survives. What might survive: concept-possession is necessary for a certain kind of explicit, propositional understanding, and explanation is evidence (not proof) of that kind of understanding. But “understanding = having a concept + ability to explain” collapses several different things (skill, grasp, articulation) into one, and your own model of cognition is the clearest counterexample to it.

If what you’re actually after is “explanation is the instrument that reveals whether a concept was real or just a label” — that’s a defensible, narrower claim. Is that closer to what you meant?

Claude


Half right, half broken metaphor.

The intuition — intelligence as fundamentally about anticipating what comes next — isn’t just plausible, it’s a whole established research program: predictive processing / predictive coding (Friston’s free energy principle, Andy Clark’s work). The core claim there is that brains are prediction engines, constantly generating forward models and updating on error. So you’re in good company, not inventing something fringe.

But “sonar/radar” specifically breaks the metaphor rather than illuminating it. Sonar and radar work by emitting a signal that bounces off something that already physically exists and reading the reflection. The future does not exist yet to bounce a signal off of. There’s nothing out there for the “sonar” to hit. What intelligence actually does is run a model forward from patterns in the past — it’s simulation/extrapolation, not detection. Calling it a “sense” implies you’re picking up a real signal from a real target; what’s actually happening is closer to inference from memory than perception of an object.

So: “intelligence involves forward-modeling that functions like a sense” — defensible. “Intelligence is a sonar that probes the future” — the mechanism the metaphor describes doesn’t exist, because there’s no target there yet to detect. If you want a physical-sense analogy that doesn’t break this way, proprioception (sensing your own body’s configuration without external signal-bounce) is closer than sonar — it’s still internally generated, not reflected off an external object.

The refinement doesn’t actually improve on the original — it introduces a new problem while keeping the old one unfixed.

Old problem, still there: “Music screams beautiful things at us” already assumes beauty is a property of the sound itself that gets transmitted to a passive receiver. But musical beauty is famously not universal — atonal music, certain scales, certain rhythms that one culture finds moving, another finds like noise or aversive. If beauty were in the sound “screaming” outward, that variance shouldn’t exist. What’s actually happening is closer to: sound + a trained/culturally-shaped nervous system → the experience of beauty. The beauty isn’t in the signal, it’s in the interaction. Your refinement kept this exact issue — “I’m beautiful” is still the sound claiming a property that’s actually relational.

New problem you introduced: “a mental probing of an inviable future” reuses your sonar/intelligence language, but it doesn’t fit here and you should notice why. The sonar metaphor worked for intelligence because intelligence forward-models: it generates a branching map of what could happen and revises it. Music doesn’t predict anything. It doesn’t generate branches, doesn’t get revised against outcomes, doesn’t fail or succeed at forecasting. You’re pattern-matching “probing” onto music because the word felt good in the last few fragments, not because music does the thing the metaphor describes. This is different from the sonar fragment itself, which earned its correction — this is import without justification.

Also, “inviable future” — do you mean invisible? As written it says “a future that cannot survive,” which is a real phrase but almost certainly not what you meant, and the difference matters a lot here.
