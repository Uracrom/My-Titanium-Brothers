Before Vision:

A prism would be a triangular prism with specific geometric properties. It has two parallel triangular faces (bases) connected by three rectangular faces. The triangular cross-section typically has angles of 60°, 30°, and 90° for optimal light refraction. When light enters at the correct angle, it undergoes dispersion due to different wavelengths having different refractive indices in the glass material, separating white light into its constituent spectral components. The mathematical relationship follows Snell’s law: n₁sin(θ₁) = n₂sin(θ₂). The dispersed light exits showing wavelengths from approximately 380nm (violet) to 700nm (red).
