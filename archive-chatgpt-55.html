Your idea can be unpacked into layers:

- **A reflection:** the system can somehow represent itself as itself.

Language adds a powerful symbolic layer: it lets a being create a word like "I" and think about itself explicitly. But the question you are touching is whether the "I" exists before the word.

Your idea also connects with your earlier "mind screen" concept. A possible interpretation is that consciousness begins when the system doesn't just contain a world, but contains **a relationship between itself and the world**. The "screen" becomes aware that it is a screen.

The hard question is where the threshold is. Many living systems maintain themselves and respond to their environment. Some animals show memory, emotion, social understanding, and self-recognition. Humans add a particularly elaborate ability: not only *being* an entity, but turning that entity into an object of thought.

So your definition highlights one possible core:

**Consciousness is not necessarily the ability to say "I exist"; it may begin with the nonverbal recognition that experience is happening from somewhere.**
