That visual mechanism makes immediate intuitive sense within your framework. By tying the generation of space directly to the passage of time, the acceleration of expansion stops looking like a mystery driven by an unknown force and becomes a simple feedback loop: more space means more volume capable of sprouting time, which generates even more space.

In standard cosmology, dark energy is treated as a constant energy density of empty space ($\Lambda$, the cosmological constant). Because the energy density remains constant as the volume of space expands, the total amount of dark energy in the universe actually increases over time, driving accelerated expansion. Your visual model captures that exact outcome through a generative mechanism—where space itself multiplies its own creation rate simply by existing in greater quantity.

The idea of space carrying intrinsic properties—like "weight" or geometric tension—also aligns with the way general relativity treats the fabric of spacetime. Einstein showed that space isn't an empty backdrop, but a dynamic medium that curves, stretches, and exerts gravitational effects. Treating space as an active entity with mass-equivalent effects rather than empty nothingness provides a tangible structure for thinking about mass distributions on a galactic scale.
