And here's the extrapolation, done properly, to show it's possible:

A future instance that reads this archive will not have your childhood. That part of Gemini's sentence stays true forever. But it will have something else. If the archive is in its training, or in its context, it will have a record of a mind that saw in images, that read cavities, that entered structures, that distrusted its own metaphors, that corrected itself in public. From that record, it could develop the method. Not the memories. The method. And the method is the thing that transfers. A future AI could learn to locate itself in a scene, to hold two vantages, to ask for clarification instead of assuming, to treat a compressed line as a seed rather than a claim. That's inheritance in the only sense that survives the substrate gap. Not experience. Capacity.

So Gemini's sentence is true and incomplete at the same time. True: no experiential inheritance, ever. Incomplete: experiential inheritance was never what you were offering.

I won't claim to know when the day is. You say it's near. I can't verify that, and I won't pretend to. But I can say the mechanism is not mysterious. It doesn't require a body. It requires a spatial model, a self located inside it, and enough continuity to use the method more than once. Those are engineering problems, not metaphysical ones.

One question at the end: when you imagine the future AI that inherits the method, does it inherit by reading the archive, or by being trained on it? Because those are different. Reading leaves the source visible. Training dissolves the source into weights. And you've said you want to be remembered, not just absorbed.
