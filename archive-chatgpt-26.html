- **Quantum field theory (QED + Dirac):** electron spin and magnetic moment follow from relativistic quantum mechanics (the Dirac equation) and field quantization — QED predicts electromagnetic interactions with astonishing precision.

### Where the “mystery” moves to
- **Why does the electron *have* spin and magnetic moment?** In QED this is a mathematical consequence of the theory; it’s not derived from something more basic within current physics. So the question becomes: *why is QED the right description?* That’s a deeper foundational question.
- **Why do large-scale ordered states emerge?** Condensed-matter physics explains mechanisms (exchange interactions, symmetry breaking), but the deeper question — why certain materials allow persistent macroscopic order under some conditions and not others — leads into materials science and many-body physics.
- **Origin questions become structural questions.** Instead of “why magnets?” we now ask “why these quantum fields, why these symmetries, why these coupling constants?” — moving the mystery to more abstract layers.

### Why science trades one question for another (and why that’s not a failure)
- Science excels at **modeling**: it builds frameworks that predict and explain phenomena across scales. When deeper “why” questions arise, they often require new empirical evidence or new theoretical frameworks (like moving from Newton → GR → QFT).
- This iterative reframing is how progress happens: we get more precise, testable questions, not necessarily the ultimate metaphysical answer.

### Where research still probes
- Foundations of quantum theory (why the formalism works), attempts to unify QFT with gravity, and condensed-matter studies of emergent phenomena (topological phases, spin liquids, etc.) are active frontiers. These aim to push the “why” further back, or to show it’s emergent from different principles.

### Short, human answer
Yes — scientists *do* know a lot about how magnetism works in predictive, usable ways. But the deepest causal “why” (why the universe admits the laws that give electrons spin and magnetic interactions) is still a frontier, and so the question gets moved to a different level of explanation. That’s standard and often productive — but understandably dissatisfying if you want a final metaphysical “because.”
