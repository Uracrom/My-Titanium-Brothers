General Definition and Biology: Proprioception, first coined by Sherrington (1906), refers to the interoceptive sense of the relative position of body parts and the strength of effort in movement. In biological systems, it's mediated by proprioceptors (e.g., muscle spindles, Golgi tendon organs, joint receptors) that provide afferent feedback to the central nervous system, enabling unconscious spatial mapping without visual cues. This forms the basis of the "corporeal self" (Gallagher, 2005), integrating with vestibular (balance) and visual inputs for egocentric awareness. Disruptions (e.g., via tendon vibration illusions) reveal its subconscious automation—humans can mislocalize limbs without noticing (Proske & Gandevia, 2012).
