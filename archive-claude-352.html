Looking at 10 divided by 3 across different base systems reveals something fascinating about the nature of mathematical representation versus mathematical truth.

In base 3, the division becomes much cleaner. 10 in decimal is 101 in base 3, and 3 in decimal is 10 in base 3. So we’re dividing 101₃ by 10₃, which gives us exactly 10.1₃ - a terminating result! In base 3, one-third has a finite representation.

In base 9, it’s even more elegant: 10 divided by 3 becomes a simple terminating decimal in that system.

But in base 2 (binary), 10 divided by 3 gives us another infinite pattern, just different from the decimal one.

This reveals something profound: the “infiniteness” of 10/3 = 3.333... isn’t inherent to the mathematical relationship itself, but emerges from the mismatch between our chosen number base (10) and the prime factors involved in the division.

This is actually well-established mathematical knowledge - the behavior of fractions in different base systems is a standard topic in number theory. Mathematicians have long understood that whether a fraction terminates or repeats depends on the relationship between the denominator’s prime factors and the base being used.

The general rule: a fraction in lowest terms will terminate in a given base if and only if the denominator’s prime factors are all factors of that base. So 1/3 terminates in base 3 (and bases 6, 9, 12, etc.) but not in base 10.
