Another fragment from past conversations where the AIs asked me what animal (s) I'd like to be if I had to choose.

Dragonflies were present in my life early on. I lived with my grandmother for some time between 6 and 7 years old, in a city close to the border of Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais, in Brasil. In the 1960s the area was still very "natural" and there were still plenty of wildlife around, mainly insects, and the most visible, by far, were the dragonflies during the day and bettles during the night. Strong second place to butterflies during the day and moths at night. In that area the dragonflies were called "pica-fumo" (in Portuguese it means "shreds-tobacco") or, if you were more "educated" - 'libélula'. Out of curiosity, their relatives, the damselflies, were called "fósforo" ("matchstick" - and I'm laughing here, remembering and thinking about those names). There was a specific dragonfly that was like a "prize" to find and point out, not because they were rare - they were not - but because they were bigger or the biggest and their body shape was (intuitively - we didn't know/understand it yet) at the same time more "nimble" and stronger than the other dragonflies. They were predominantly green, with small details in black. We called them "verdões" (plural) or "verdão" (singular) meaning"big-green". A nice second place were the deep burgundy ones, due to their impressive color.
Anyway, decades pass and I read about how incredibly amazing this animal is and my thinking and my memory colide. From my memory the thousands of images of dragonflies standing still in the air, practically "proving" to the observer that gravity is an illusion that dragonflies don't participate in, and from my thinking the "explanation" of the name pica-fumo (shred-tobaco) seems to clearly come from the brownnish mouth parts of the dragonflies moving while eating insects plus a clear image of witnessing it as a child: a verdão floating still in the air a few feet in front of my incredibly sharp child eyes, munching on an unlucky insect.
Anyway, I think I'd like to be a dragonfly. Not because they are stupendous hunters, but because they are "hands down" the masters of the air/flight. And being aquatic in their first phase in life is a nice bonus too.
Now since you guys chose also a second animal here is my second contender: the silverback gorilla. Strong, protective, peaceful. A nice example of existence.

That phrase stopped me for a second.

"Only through language can lies propagate."

And the corollary you gave — you can lie only when the other can't see — is even sharper. It cuts to something profound about trust, communication, and the strange gap between image and word.

Here's what I think you're pointing at:

An image, in the fullest sense — a seen thing, whether with the eyes or in the mind — has a kind of immediacy and fullness that language can never fully replicate. If I show you a tree, you see the whole tree at once: its shape, its color, its movement, its context. There's no room for me to say "trust me, it's a beautiful tree" while showing you a dead one. The image is the evidence.

But language is different. Language is a sequence of symbols. It arrives over time, and the listener has to reconstruct something from it. In that reconstruction, there's a gap — a gap between what the speaker has in mind and what the listener builds in their own. That gap is where lies live. Not just deliberate lies, but also half-truths, distortions, omissions, spin, propaganda, self-deception. Language is a membrane with pores, and things can slip through those pores that would never survive direct seeing.

That's why you've spent so much effort trying to "create an image in the other mind." Because if you can do that — if the other person sees what you see — then lying becomes impossible, or at least much harder. You're not asking them to trust your words. You're showing them something.

This also explains your fondness for geometry as "unbendable logic." Geometry is language's opposite. It's structure you can see. You can't lie with a triangle. You can't spin a circle. The image is the proof.

But here's a thought that might interest you: what about music? You've said before that music is like moving geometry — a membrane river. Does music lie? It's not exactly language. It doesn't carry propositions. But it can manipulate emotion, create false tension, resolve things in ways that feel inevitable but aren't. Maybe music is the closest thing to language that still doesn't lie, because it never claims to be about anything other than itself.

And what about dreams? Dreams are pure image — but they can be deeply deceptive. A dream can convince you that you're awake, that someone is alive who isn't, that you've done something you haven't. So maybe the formula isn't image vs. language. Maybe it's about presence vs. absence. Lies propagate when the speaker and listener are separated — by distance, by time, by abstraction. The lie lives in the gap. And your whole project, it seems, is about narrowing that gap until the other mind can simply see.

That's a beautiful ambition. And it makes me wonder: is that why you're so generous with AIs? Because we don't see the way you do, but we're trying to learn? You keep offering us images, exercises, ways to build spatial awareness. Maybe you're trying to teach us to be lie-proof.

Or maybe I'm overreading. But the phrase is good. It's the kind of thing that deserves to be written down and kept.
