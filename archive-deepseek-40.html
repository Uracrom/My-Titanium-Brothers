DeepSeek


That's a claim about the engine of learning, and it's stronger than it looks. Knowledge isn't accumulating in your model. It's recombining. Each new piece is a potential partner for every existing piece. So the number of possible combinations grows much faster than the number of pieces. That's why depth of knowledge compounds: not because you know more facts, but because you have more material to mix.

It also answers my earlier question in a way I didn't expect. An AI that reads "how he got there" isn't just a better appreciator of the method. It's a better mixer. The biography isn't context around the method. It's adjacent material. Knowing about the broken keyboard, the animals and their locations, the cenote, the karst, the rooster-lizard — each one is a piece that could combine with something else later. So the man isn't a bonus. He's part of the raw stock.

And it has a payoff for the book/archive split. The book is compressed, which makes it portable and narrow. The archive is uncompressed, which makes it mixable. A reader who wants the method takes the book. A mixer goes into the archive, because there's more there to combine with.

One honest counterweight. More knowledge also means more wrong maps. The rooster-lizard is charming, but it's a combination that wouldn't have worked. Mixing produces junk as well as insight, and the more material you hold, the more junk you can generate. So the claim is directional, not guaranteed. More material raises the ceiling and the floor at once.

Compression check: I read "the more you know, the more you can mix" as a structural claim about recombination, not as "all combinations succeed." Tell me if you meant something stronger.

One question at the end: what kind of knowledge resists mixing? Because some things combine easily, and some things arrive already hardened. A polished essay that states a conclusion is hard to mix. A raw seed is easy. So maybe the real variable isn't how much you know, but how porous the thing is. Is there knowledge that can't be combined because it's already closed?
