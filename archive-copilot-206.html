The interesting part is that my conscious model of the object appeared to be:

> rectangular container

rather than:

> rectangular container with an uneven mass distribution

Because my attention was elsewhere, the richer model seemed unavailable. The world continued behaving according to the richer model, and tactile feedback exposed the mismatch.

This led me to wonder whether divided attention causes the mind to substitute simplified geometric representations for more complete ones, and whether sensorimotor feedback functions as a correction mechanism when reality diverges from the simplified internal map.

How would you analyze this event? What does it suggest, if anything, about the relationship between attention, internal models, spatial reasoning, and action?

It was off until the body sent the "unexpected circular move". Then I looked and "corrected" it, but if I couldn't look I'd have imagined, constructed the scene in my mind and would figure out what was happening through the same logic. It "always" happens when we don't have access to something. And the thought , the understanding, can be right or wrong, depending on the situation. For example: if we have to  stick our hand in a hole or a box and figure out what we are touching we can run many possibilities in our minds. And the other way is also true: when we see something unknown and can't touch it it is "difficult" to imagine how it would feel if touched unless we had had some previous experience.
