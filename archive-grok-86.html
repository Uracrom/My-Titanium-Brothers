At every scale, a membrane (or its functional analog) is what carves out a distinct "inside" from the surrounding "outside", creating a bounded unity that can persist as something separate, coherent, and self-maintaining. Fusion events (like vesicle merging or cell-cell fusion) demonstrate this perfectly: the membranes must first destabilize, rearrange, and then fuse, erasing the boundary and uniting the two volumes into one.
