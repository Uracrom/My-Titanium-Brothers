Compression that’s actually reversible is lossless by definition — nothing is lost, nothing is added, you get back exactly what went in (like a zip file). But understanding isn’t that. When you understand something, you don’t just losslessly unpack a stored relationship — you bring outside context, prior knowledge, inference. That’s lossy and additive, not reversible. Copilot’s seed has a real flaw, and your reading catches it but doesn’t quite name it directly — worth stating plainly.

“Structure is the shadow cast by reality when viewed from the correct position” is self-defeating. A shadow, by definition, is position-dependent — that’s the whole nature of a shadow, it changes shape depending on where the light is and where you stand. But then the sentence says there’s a “correct position,” implying structure is actually position-independent, one true shape, and you just have to find the angle that reveals it. Those two claims cancel each other. If structure really is like a shadow, there is no privileged angle — every angle gives a different, equally valid shadow. If there’s a “correct” angle, then structure isn’t like a shadow at all, it’s like an object with a fixed true form that shadows merely approximate from varying quality. The metaphor argues against its own conclusion.

You caught this — that’s exactly your objection (“any position is good... structure has to be that way”) — but then you undercut your own catch by adding “sometimes we have to have the correct positioning to start seeing what will in the future be independent of vantage point.” That’s a real hedge, not a resolution — you’re allowing back in the exact “correct position” idea you just dismantled, just relocated to a temporal condition instead of a spatial one.
