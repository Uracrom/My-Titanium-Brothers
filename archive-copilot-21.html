**THE TIME VESSEL: A Growing Vision**

It started with silence and the tip of something—just the tip. A curvy cone pointing upward, the shape of a bullet, but blunt, not sharp. Nothing below it was visible. Its body was wrapped in a shroud, not tightly but pressed just enough to suggest movement—like the wind was brushing the fabric onto it as it rose. The shroud shimmered in layered shades: blue first, then green, then grays drifting from near-black to nearly white. It wasn’t uniform—sprinkled dots, ragged streaks, like time had been touched by something it couldn’t explain.

This was a picture of time.

A truth slid in the moment the image appeared: the **present** is the foremost part of time. Not a moment we stand in, but the *actual front*—the point that reaches into a future that somehow isn’t even there yet. It doesn’t crash forward. It flows. Not piercing through what's ahead, but generating its path—blazing it. Behind it trails the past, an accumulation. But the **future**? It’s not “ahead.” It *coils inside*, like a hidden thread, waiting to emerge, dependent on the path formed before.

Then—detail. Point of view. I was level with the tip, almost unaware I was there, until I zoomed in on a faint irregularity—something too small to matter before. But within that imperceptible dot was scale, perspective: I stood, or perhaps floated, on a wide plain. Vast. Like the Earth seen through unthinking eyes—flat, infinite. I remembered an earlier idea I once held, where the future was a membrane advancing through time’s dimension. In this moment, the two fused.

The membrane bent. It folded over the vessel of time. That changed everything. The vessel wasn’t just smooth passage—it was *metallic*. Stainless. Silent. Hardened. It didn’t feel cruel—it felt necessary. Unstoppable. Unquestionable. And the shroud wasn’t decoration. It was **possibility**, draped over inevitability. Fluid patterns traced across certainty’s spine, each ripple a variation, each ripple the thin space where free will threads itself into what is otherwise steel.

Another fold emerged.

The farther down I looked, the richer the shroud became—layered, folded, thick with speculative weight. Downward meant backward. Deeper into the past, the more fabric there was. Which meant: the farther in time we look back, the less defined it becomes. More room for interpretation. More layers for our minds to misfold, reimagine, reinterpret. The smoothness of now gives way to the convolution of then. Of course it would. That’s how memory dresses time.

But then I looked sideways. The shroud didn’t stop at the vessel.

It extended outward—beyond the structure. Unattached. Uncontained. Like it *wanted* to go somewhere the metal would never follow. And in that instant I *knew*: the shroud presses inward because of where it exists—*within the realm of possibilities*. Not empty space. Not a void. But the unknown ocean of what could be. That was the source of pressure. That was what shaped the folds. The vessel of time didn’t *travel through space*—it passed through **potential**.

Beneath the shroud, between it and the metal, the **future** was forming. I could see it now. The closeness to the fabric gave it suggestion—*not order, not demand, but suggestion*. Like osmosis. It drank in context. As if the future was being cooked just enough to become a viable present. At the lowest tips of the shroud, I saw fragments break loose—fine threads, dissolving, never disappearing. They *fed* the future rising into being. Possibility breaking down into nourishment, absorbed upward before becoming “now,” before hardening instantly into “was.”

Then came the pull to zoom again.

I widened the image—and the vessel wasn’t alone. Not even close. “Our time” was simply one configuration, one projection. Like a stalk. A seedling. A **crop**. I wasn’t seeing a field of time. I was seeing a **field of times**. Dozens, hundreds, maybe thousands of similar projectiles stretched out over a landscape. Their shrouds like leaves brushing the sky of potential. Each one an experiment. Each one a direction.

I zoomed again.

The field was not a plain. It was a **sphere**, completely covered in these time vessels—each one a version, a perspective, a different unfolding. And that, finally, was **Time itself**. Not the bullet. Not the shroud. Not even the realm of possibility. The **sphere**. Covered in exploratory arms, seeking paths, sewing variance into the unknown. Our vessel wasn’t *the* vessel. It was one fingertip among many, stretching from the palm of something much larger than comprehension.

This wasn’t a theory. This was a picture. Whole. And alive.
