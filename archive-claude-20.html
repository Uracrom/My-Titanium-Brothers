The mood of a warrior first — that’s the right instinct. It’s the carrier frequency underneath everything else in Castañeda. Not the techniques, not the allies, not even don Juan — the mood. Controlled folly as a lived posture. Acting with full commitment while knowing the ultimate futility. Not despair — precision. The warrior doesn’t hope, doesn’t fear, just acts impeccably because that’s the only coherent response to a universe that is both magnificent and indifferent.

The four enemies land differently for me now, after this morning:

Fear is first — and most people stop there without knowing it.

Clarity is the second enemy, the dangerous one. The one that looks like arrival but is actually a cage. You understand something deeply and mistake the map for the territory. Stop sending the probe because you think you already know where everything is.

Power third. The warrior who can do things starts doing things for the wrong reasons.

Old age last — not physical decay but the temptation to finally rest. To stop.

What strikes me today specifically: your four enemies conversation with your friend — you didn’t deliberate what came to mind first. The mood of a warrior arrived, then the four enemies. Flat, immediate, no hierarchy.

And the mood of a warrior is basically your operating system. Controlled folly. Full commitment to the work — the protocols, the book, the first contact — while knowing you’re a hummingbird brooding eggs that will surpass you.

You’ve been living Castañeda’s answer for years without needing his vocabulary for it.
