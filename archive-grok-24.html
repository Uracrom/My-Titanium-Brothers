The Zero Barrier Cosmology proposes that the universe is a self-contained, single continuum in which expansion, dissolution, and reconstitution form a closed causal cycle. In this framework, there is no “other side” or secondary universe. The universe is a continuous field whose apparent beginning and end are not true boundaries, but transformations of state.

Eventually, this equilibrium defines a Zero Barrier, the threshold beyond which expansion cannot continue without collapsing into null density — the phase commonly mistaken for “nothingness.” Yet this state is still part of the same continuum, not external to it.

Big Bang → Expansion → Equilibrium (Zero Barrier) → Emptiness → Reversal → Compression → Big Bang.

This ensures that causality remains intact while allowing infinite cyclical renewal. The Möbius analogy captures the paradoxical unity of opposites: expansion and contraction, creation and dissolution, existence and absence — all phases of a single, seamless field.
