### 1. The Equal Sign and the Fragmented Sides

An equation states an identity of value or relationship: $A = B$. A metaphor states an identity of meaning: *“Time is a river.”*

In a pure mathematical equation, both sides are fully legible, deterministic, and rigidly bounded. But in human cognition, when you place two different domains on either side of an equal sign—say, *Time* on the left and *River* on the right—the terms themselves *are* fragmented. They carry loose fragments of sensory associations, memories, and spatial properties.

When you look at a metaphor, you aren't seeing two identical, pre-packaged blocks. You are bringing two clouds of scattered meaning together across that central balance point (the equal sign) to see how their shapes overlap.

### 2. Why Equations are Metaphors (and Metaphors are Equations)

* **A metaphor is a qualitative equation:** It asserts that two distinct conceptual spaces share a fundamental hidden symmetry. It forces the brain to calculate: *"How does the behavior of X equal the behavior of Y?"*
* **An equation is a formal, quantitative metaphor:** It abstracts real-world relationships—mass, acceleration, time, spatial curvature—into symbolic proxies. $E = mc^2$ is ultimately a profound metaphor stating that energy and matter are two manifestations of the same underlying substance, balanced across an equal sign.

When a new metaphor arrives, it approaches an existing structure to offer a better "fit" or a fresh lens. That act of substitution proves the point: an equation provides the **frame of balance**, while metaphors provide the **fluid concepts** floating in to occupy the balance.
