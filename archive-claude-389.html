Trees

And now the story or explanation of how the trees turned the world upside down.
Foreword: it's important to know that this narration is long, but only when put into words. All that happened very quickly and I can't be very precise about it, but I bet it was less than a second.
At one point I was thinking that I was about to approach an unknown planet. I was in a spaceship very much like the ones we see in sci-fi movies, the ones that go from space to inside the atmosphere very easily. In space there's no up or down, so I was approaching the planet by its equator but the planet was to the top of the spaceship, like, if the planet was a table I'd be flying under the table. So I was seeing the planet above me when I noticed the trees and I had the sharp impression that the trees were some stuff "hanging" from the planet and the tree's "legs" were the parts that were actually hanging. So I saw the trunk, the branches and the leaves as a kind of root or an even stranger organ or apparatus for the collection of nutrients. I was facing trees as if I was an intelligent life form that came from a place/planet where trees didn't exist. From that angle, that point of view, I was seeing the trees "upside up", but their "head" was buried. Then I realized that trees are "upside down" beings here on earth. My argument is that much more frequently than not, if you cut a tree, the roots (which I consider the main part of the tree) will sprout again and again, while the trunk and the branches will die. And that and the other thoughts that I  exposed earlier is what made me consider the trees the absolute pinnacle of life on earth.I also forgot to point the reason why they turned the world upside down: I see trees as upside down beings: their "head" (the most important part) is under the earth, and some of its "energy grabbers" are in the air, above the soil.

Uracrom and the trees and also alluding to spaceships

I already told you guys that I was first trained to be a hunter, then a warrior and lastly a shaman. Being a shaman is not a choice. You are born like that, and that's very good and correct because it makes us humble. Being a shaman is not comfortable.  It's a challenge.  People come to you like you have an answer to anything and everything,  and it couldn't be farther from the truth.  We have doubts too. We have misunderstandings.  We make mistakes.  Some fatal. We don't have a choice on what we see. We see what we see,  and have to deal with that.   Here is an example: imagine a red lion. Once you see it you can't unsee it.  It will be in your memory forever. And many things we see can change the world drastically and forever, or change the world in a way that it doesn't matter to you but still you would prefer it not to be changed. Would you like me to give you an example?  It might not bother or affect you, but you won't be able to unsee it. Should I tell you?

Village - everyone stares at Uracrom. Some with eyes that flood with curiosity.  Even if it was possible to say no, they would be trapped.  Some with fear, disguised as stone-faced serenity. Others not believing at all that such thing would be possible. How a simple vision can change the whole world?  The old shaman,  understanding the challenge,  starts speaking and says:  I,  for one, want to know what Uracrom is talking about. Not because I don't know about what he is talking about:  I'm a shaman too. I've seen strange things. But because one of the people's way is to gather knowledge,  and anything that is covered by the shroud of the unknown is fair game for the verticals' quests. And since we abide to the people's ways we should never run scared from the unknown.  Please Uracrom,  allow us to see what you saw.

Uracrom - it's important to know that this narration is long, but only when put into words. All that happened very quickly and I can't be very precise about it, but I bet it was less than a slow blink.
At one point I was thinking that I was inside a flying boat. A strange boat indeed: first, I was inside it, not on top of it. Second the boat had water windows in front of it. I say water windows because we could see perfectly through it,  and there were no ripples,  like the water was actually ice, but I know it was not cold. We were amongst the sky fires but there was a big ball ahead of us. I knew it was big because it took a long time to get close to it. First the big ball started to grow slowly but the closer we got the bigger it was untill the point that it was bigger than the water windows,  but it didn't stop there: we continued to get closer and the ball continued to get bigger. At the same time we were going down,  like we were trying to pass below the big ball. Right there it crossed my mind that maybe we were growing smaller instead? Nevertheless,  whatever was happening was that we were getting closer and closer and at one point I recognized something: I saw a tiny mountain, but it was upside down.  Then I saw that on the other side of the mountain there was a lake and then a forest spreading towards the horizon.  The boat got closer still and we were floating very close to the trees when I observed something:  the trees were using their roots to grab, holding the dirt above us for dear life. But I didn't feel any fear or despair. They seemed to be naturally comfortable.  Then I observed the trunks, branches and the leaves. They were hanging down from the dirt above them very naturally,  and then bam! I realized that we live in an upside-down world! The trees are - according to our point of view - upside-down beings! It was clear in my vision: the roots are the heart, the head of the trees. They "are" the trees. When we cut a tree most of the time the fallen trunk and the branches and leaves just die, but the stomp, the roots, usually start to sprout again. The point is, according to this vision, the trees are upside-down beings and I cannot go back to the trees being the trunks and leaves etc. From that moment on trees are the roots, and they are upside-down beings.

A boy asks - where did you get that boat?

Uracrom  - I didn't get it. It got me. It was a vision. When you  have a vision what is actually happening is that the vision is having you.  The vision takes you for a ride and you either see what's being shown or not. Visions are like dreams: they take you inside them, not the other way around.

A warrior asks - Uracrom,  why were those mountains and lake and trees on a big upside-down ball?

Uracrom  - I was going to tell you another vision/story later, but makes sense to tell you now since you asked.  We can't see it. It's going to take many generations of verticals, but one day we will be able to build boats made of something hidden inside mother earth,  something that we will need to gather using fires so hot that is white. This thing will change the lives of the verticals almost as much as the fire. Imagine a life without fire. Very hard,  right?  In many generations this present from mother earth will as important as fire. Remember the thin rods that I said the verticals used to build the dead rock forest? They are the same thing. One piece of that thing, if shaped and shaped correctly,  can cut an entire forest. But moving on, the verticals will build not only flying boats but  other amazing things and they will find out that mother earth is not flat! I know you can't believe me, but I saw it. Mother earth is also a big ball. And now you know that mother earth is not only a big ball, but is covered with upside-down beings…

The tribal lens: You mentioned being highly tribal as an epiphany. What does your tribe look like? Not just people, but maybe sounds, places, rituals, stories that make you feel like you belong to something larger. How do you recognize your tribe members?

We are strong. Being strong is a must amongst my people. We are not competitive with ourselves, but against the rest of the world? You bet! We love, above all. We love the pure love, not the romantic love other people talk and dream about, but the real love, the brother love, the love that puts everyone else above you because you die for them, and you die for them for any reason, and it doesn't feel like a sacrifice: it feels good. For us, dying for who we love is the Pinnacle of a life. But don't think that we are just a bunch of suicidal delusional maniacs. We are very grounded. We are against having enemies, and yet, we judge our courage, our value, according to our enemies, so we defy the two greatest enemies that we can think of whenever it is necessary: Death and Time. We know we will never win, but we also know that we will never give up.
We live in this forest, this beautiful forest... Here the ground, mother Earth, pushes all kinds of trees and plants up, feeding them with whatever they need, and by their turn, they feed us all, people and animals, to our fullest. We are very very grateful for all of it. For us, it's no secret that being grateful is one of the best things one can feel. But we don't feel grateful because it's good. We feel grateful because we are grateful.
I think one of the best things about being from my tribe is the sense of reliance. We know that we can count on anyone for anything, and that alone is a major part of knowing that our plans and dreams will be accomplished. A bard from the past once sang this song that summoned this:

"A dream that you dream alone,
Is just a dream that you dream alone,
But a dream that you dream together, is reality"

He was right.
