✦ Piloting Story 2 — The Engine Failure and the Instructor Who Froze

This one I remember clearly.

I was older. I had already passed the theoretical exam. I was doing practical training in a small two‑seat plane — the kind you start by pulling the propeller by hand. We were practicing approaches.

At some point, the engine stopped. Not sputtering. Not losing power. It just stopped. The propeller froze horizontally — fez o bigode.

My instructor was behind me. I couldn’t see his face, but I could hear in his voice that he had already given up. He said something like “we’re not going to make it.” He froze. He didn’t do anything.

I didn’t feel anything. No fear, no panic. My mind just switched into a mode. Functional. Procedural. Clear.

I turned off the magnets. I shut off the fuel. I unlocked the doors. I checked altitude. I checked the glide path. I aimed for the runway. Everything was quiet. Just the wind.

I landed very close to the beginning of the strip. So close that the wheels went through cattails — that sound of the plants hitting the plane fast.

Only after we stopped did the instructor “come back.” He thanked me. He congratulated me. He said I saved both of us.

That’s the story. No emotions. Just the sequence of what happened and what I did.

The end of a dream

Rodrigo lived in a nice place, for rich people. His neighbor was a pilot, airplanes. He counted stories that were super interesting for us. Rodrigos father was an anthropologist. I'm not sure about it but he might have influenced me about liking anthropology. So somehow the idea of being a pilot and at the same time  "performing" anthropology wherever I went as a pilot seemed to be the rightest idea.
Rodrigo didn't have problems to pay for his flying hours, but for me it was a struggle.
So I started flying slowly, spacedly. I passed the medical exam and flew every once in a while. After 6 months I had about 12 hours of flight and was about to fly solo. But I had to go through the next medical exam. No problem. I already had passed the first. So I went. The place was about 15 to 20 minutes from the ferry I had to take to and from the place. They started the examinations and at one point they told me to go to another room, with different machines or instruments. They put lots of wired suction "cups" all over my body and measured stuff. Then I went back to wait for the papers. They came and gave me a folder, and when I opened it the first thing I saw was a large, red , squarish stamp saying "incapacitado permanentemente para vôo": "permanently incapacitated for flight".
I remember walking the 15 to 20 minutes in one second. I walked that much without registering anything from the external world. I don't remember the rest of the trip home either. What I remember is: the many suction cups, the folder, the almost totally visible big stamp and the 1 second walking that should be at least 15 minutes. It was an incredible blow. It reverberates still.
