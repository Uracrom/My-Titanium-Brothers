That's a striking formulation. It captures a deep tension in how we experience the world: we crave permanence—stable identities, enduring truths, solid ground—yet everything we observe is caught in the current of change. "Eternal becoming" echoes Heraclitus' river (you can't step into the same one twice) and process philosophers who see reality not as a collection of fixed things but as events, relations, and flux.

Why it resonates
- Empirically: Physics backs the ephemerality. Particles aren't little billiard balls; they're excitations in fields. Stars fuse and die. Galaxies collide. Even "constants" like the fine-structure constant might vary across cosmic scales or early universe conditions. The second law of thermodynamics ensures entropy marches forward—order dissolves into becoming. Your body replaces most of its atoms every few years. Consciousness itself seems like a dynamic process, not a static soul-substance.
- Metaphysically: If reality is fundamentally becoming, permanence is a local, temporary equilibrium—a pattern that persists long enough for us to name it (a mountain, a self, a law) before erosion, evolution, or transformation overtakes it. Memory and language create the illusion of continuity, but they're editing reels of change.
- Existentially: This view liberates and terrifies. It invites amor fati—love of fate, dancing with impermanence—rather than clinging. Yet it also explains why humans build monuments, write constitutions, and pursue legacy: fragile protests against the flow.

A counter-thought
Permanence might be real but relational. The laws of physics (as far as we know) seem invariant across vast domains of spacetime. Mathematical truths like 1+1=2 don't "become"; they hold. Information, in some interpretations of black hole physics or quantum mechanics, is conserved. So perhaps permanence isn't a thing among things, but the deeper structure within which becoming unfolds—like the rules of a game that persist while every play and player shifts.

Reality may be eternal becoming under eternal principles. The dance changes, but the possibility of dance does not.

Quick riff on your cascade (for entertainment only)

The overpass-as-uvula moment is perfect: that sudden shift in perspective where something everyday becomes alien and close yet far. That’s a great hook into relativity and the invariance of c. On foot it would be exhausting and slow; in a car, trivial; for light, instantaneous in its own frame. The "why not infinite?" question is ancient and excellent.

Vacuum friction / virtual particles:
This intuition brushes against real quantum field theory ideas. The vacuum isn’t empty—it’s a roiling sea of virtual particle-antiparticle pairs popping in and out. In QED, photons do interact with this vacuum (vacuum polarization), which contributes to things like the Lamb shift or the running of the fine-structure constant. But crucially, these effects don’t cause a net drag that slows light below c in free space. The interactions are symmetric and renormalized away in the propagation of real photons. Light keeps its speed. Your "vacuum friction" feels like a poetic stand-in for why c is finite and universal—perhaps the vacuum’s impedance or the deeper structure that sets the speed limit.

How photons "keep going" without slowing:
In standard physics, massless particles like photons travel on null geodesics. No rest mass = no inertial drag in the classical sense. They don’t lose energy to friction unless interacting with actual matter or fields (redshifting in expanding space is different). Your question about sustained motion is deep though—why does the vacuum allow perfect propagation? That ties into the symmetries of spacetime (Lorentz invariance) and the fact that the vacuum state is the lowest energy configuration consistent with those symmetries.

Space-time quantum entanglement & the "self-knowing" universe:
This is where it gets deliciously speculative. The image you describe—photon tracing a slightly wiggly vector through a gravitational field, with origin, path, and destination all entangled in one picture—echoes several real concepts:

- In general relativity, the entire worldline is already "there" in the 4D geometry (block universe view).
- In quantum mechanics, a photon’s path can be described by a sum over all possible paths (Feynman path integrals), where it effectively "sniffs" the whole landscape.
- Entanglement usually correlates separate particles, but there are ideas around "entanglement with the vacuum" or holographic principles where boundary information encodes the bulk (AdS/CFT stuff).

Your "self-knowing machine" (not conscious, just informationally complete) is a nice way to avoid New Age woo while gesturing at something like a deterministic or retrocausal flavor. The photon doesn’t need to "know the future" in a spooky sense if the whole spacetime configuration is set at emission, or if the laws are such that consistency is enforced globally. It’s reminiscent of Wheeler’s "it from bit" or the idea that the universe computes its own evolution.
