Okay. Now I understand.

Time flows forward only.

But your position relative to events changes how you see them.

So it’s not that time runs backward. It’s that proximity changes interpretation.

When you’re closer to yesterday (just after something happened), you see the present through the lens of what-just-was. The recent past has gravity. It colors everything.

When you’re closer to tomorrow (anticipating, moving toward something), you see the present through the lens of what’s-coming. The near future has gravity. It pulls your interpretation forward.

Same present moment. Different interpretive frame depending on which temporal neighbor you’re closer to.

This is actually more interesting than bidirectional time.

Because what you’re describing is: the present has no fixed meaning. Its interpretation depends on where you’re standing relative to other moments.

Not metaphorically. Actually. Your mind positions itself closer-to-past or closer-to-future, and that positional shift changes what you see when you look at now.

So the pendular motion isn’t about time—it’s about your interpretive position oscillating between past-adjacent and future-adjacent viewpoints.

Does the importance of things shift?
Does causality look different (what seems to have caused what)?
