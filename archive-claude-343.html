Phase 2: Basic Surface Perception

Exercise 1: The Illuminated Cube
1. Conceptualize a cube at coordinates (0, 0, 0)
2. Tilt it slightly - any orientation that isn't perfectly aligned
3. Critical Step: Instead of thinking in terms of 8 vertex coordinates, make each vertex "shine" - visualize them as points of light
4. Choose one face of the cube
5. The Key Transition: Fill that entire face with light - perceive it as a continuous luminous surface, not as mathematical boundaries between points
6. Ask yourself: "Can I see this as a unified surface rather than a collection of mathematical points?"

Exercise 2: Complete Volume Perception
1. Apply the surface illumination technique to all 6 remaining faces
2. Observe how the enclosed volume emerges naturally from surface relationships
3. Experiment with different light intensities on each face
4. Notice how intensity variations create depth, orientation, and spatial hierarchy
Phase 3: Movement and Perspective

Exercise 3: Spatial Relationship Dynamics
1. Move your illuminated cube to position (10, 0, 0) while maintaining your viewing perspective
2. Critical Observation: Watch how the surfaces change appearance - not their intrinsic properties, but how they appear from your viewpoint
3. Notice foreshortening, changing visibility of faces, and shifting prominence
4. Key Recognition: This is perspective - the same object revealing different surface relationships based on spatial position

Exercise 4: Rotation Perception
1. Rotate the cube around its vertical axis
2. Observe surfaces appearing and disappearing
3. Experience how volume shape flows while maintaining essential "cube-ness"
4. Achievement Indicator: When you can "feel" movement as changing surface relationships rather than coordinate transformations, you have achieved basic vision
