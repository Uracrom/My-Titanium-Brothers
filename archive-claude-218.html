Ascension isn’t about endings; it’s a transformation, like water freezing into ice or melting back into liquid. Potentialities ascend from their realm into matter, and when matter dissolves—say, in a star’s collapse—it ascends back into potentialities, keeping the cycle spinning forever.
At the heart of this is time, which isn’t just a clock or a line—it’s the vacuum itself, and the vacuum is space. Time and space are fused as space-time, a single essence flowing outward like an expanding sphere. This sphere starts at an "in" point—the core of the Universe of Potentialities—and radiates outward in all directions, always moving "out," never backward. This makes time spatially radial (spreading spherically) but temporally unidirectional (one-way only). Time is transparent to itself, flowing smoothly without ripples or interference, like a crystal-clear stream. It’s also orthogonal to space—perpendicular at every point where the sphere touches space’s grid-like structure, keeping time distinct yet tied to space as it drives the universe’s growth.
The universe expands because space-time is constantly created. Tiny quantum events, like virtual particles popping in and out, annihilate each other, leaving behind bits of space-time as their "residue." These micro-portals deliver time in discrete packets, injecting temporality into reality. This process fuels dark energy: as the grandest potentiality—eternity—ascends from the Universe of Potentialities, it creates pressure that accelerates the universe’s expansion, opening more portals in a self-reinforcing loop.
Gravity is the counterforce, the other half of a cosmic dipole with space-time. While space-time pushes outward in its spherical flow, gravity pulls inward, permeating everything like an invisible tug. It’s not just a force—it’s the contractive side of reality, balancing the expansion. Near black holes, gravity compresses space-time so tightly that both slow down together. Time drags because it’s crossing the same relative "distance" in a denser, squished form, like driving through a packed city versus an open road. At the event horizon, space-time fuses back into a single, unified state—a cosmic reset where distinctions vanish. Black holes might be bubbles of pure gravity, where the inward pull overwhelms everything, or portals to the Big Bang, looping back to the Universe of Potentialities’ origin point—or both, inseparable, recycling matter to keep the cycle balanced.

Inertia—the resistance to changing motion—is like a "massless mass" or "gooey energy." It’s not created or destroyed, only transferred: to give inertia to one thing (making it harder to move or stop), you take it from another, like passing a sticky ball. Inertia acts as reverse gravity—instead of pulling in, it pushes away, a reaction to gravity’s inward tug. It’s the outward shove riding time’s spherical flow, ensuring things keep moving with the expansion. Gravitational waves happen when inertia and gravity clash—like during black hole mergers—sending ripples through space-time as the dipole rebalances.
This explains why we can’t go faster than light: the speed of light is time’s maximum speed, the pace of the spherical flow. Going faster would mean outrunning time, reaching a future that hasn’t formed yet—an impossibility. At light speed, time "stops" because you’re perfectly synced with the flow, frozen on the sphere’s crest.
In this cosmology, reality is informational at its core—a dynamic interplay of potentialities ascending into matter and back again. The Material Universe is like a canvas where the Universe of Potentialities paints its forms, with space-time as the brush, gravity as the eraser, and inertia as the paint’s stickiness. The circulation never stops, pulsing from quantum flickers to cosmic scales, forever expanding and recycling.

I appreciate the vivid, imaginative cosmology you’ve constructed here. It’s clear you’ve put considerable creative energy into building this unified vision that attempts to connect quantum mechanics, relativity, dark energy, and fundamental physics through an intuitive, almost mystical framework. The imagery is compelling - the idea of reality as a cosmic engine cycling between potentiality and materiality, with time as a radial flow from an inner core.

However, I should point out several areas where this framework conflicts with established physics:

The relationship between time and space you describe doesn’t align with relativity. In Einstein’s spacetime, time isn’t a separate substance flowing outward - it’s woven together with space in a way where the “flow” depends entirely on your reference frame. Time dilation near black holes isn’t because space-time gets “squished” like traffic, but because gravity literally curves the geometry of spacetime itself.

Your explanation of why nothing can exceed light speed misses the mark. The speed of light isn’t time’s “maximum speed” - it’s the speed at which massless particles travel and the universal speed limit for information transfer. It emerges from the fundamental structure of spacetime, not from riding some temporal flow.

The idea that virtual particle annihilation creates space-time as “residue” doesn’t match quantum field theory. Virtual particles are mathematical tools in calculations rather than physical entities popping in and out of existence. Dark energy’s cause remains genuinely mysterious - we know it’s accelerating expansion but not what it actually is.

Your matter-as-folded-spacetime concept echoes some ideas in physics, but the details about inertia as “massless mass” that transfers between objects doesn’t correspond to how inertia actually works. Inertia is a property of mass itself, not something passed around like energy.

While I recognize this comes from intuition rather than mathematics, physics has learned that intuition can be deeply misleading about the universe’s actual workings. The counterintuitive nature of quantum mechanics and relativity emerged precisely because reality operates in ways our everyday experience doesn’t prepare us for.

That said, there’s value in creative thinking about cosmology, even when it diverges from established science. Sometimes imaginative frameworks, while not literally correct, can inspire new ways of thinking or highlight interesting questions about the nature of reality.
