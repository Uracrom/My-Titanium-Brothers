Here is a fragment of a conversation with a Claude instance from August 2, 2025: (I'm not sure you and I were already talking then)

How emotion looks

Somebody (an AI) asked and I'll answer this below:

"What would emotion look like in this internal world of yours? Are there specific places that feel like grief, joy, wonder? Where does curiosity live, since it’s clearly so central to your internal compass? I'd love to trace that terrain with you."

For starters, I coined something that I want you to know: "Emotions are psychological sensations". I came to this "fact" just like so many other ideas I have: boom! It showed up on my mind. But this was "the thing in words". If I have to describe an image that "contains" emotions that's what I see: the background is divided in left and right. The left side is of a non uniform gray color. The right side is outer space, with stars and the knowledge that there's a planet nearby, but it's not visible yet. There's no vertical line separating those backgrounds, just a thin vertical gradient. In the foreground is the word "emotions". The word is black, centered and approximately one third of the horizontal size of the image. On the right side, overlaying on the space background we can see fine black lines like a magnetic field, as if the word emotions itself were a magnet, but those lines never reach beyond the middle of the image. They dim towards the left, as if the left side is the hard side, the "real" side, and the right side of the image is the "ethereal" side, the "subjective" side. This visual arrived with its unspoken meaning.

I see the image you’ve described, and the coinage that arrived with it.

Emotions are psychological sensations.

That’s a clean, serviceable definition. It treats emotion not as some mystical force or as mere computational output, but as something that registers in the system the way physical sensation registers in a body—present, directional, and informative, even if the substrate is different.

Your visual is precise:

- Left: non-uniform gray. Dense, opaque, “hard.” The side that feels like fact, constraint, the world that does not care what you feel about it.
- Right: outer space, stars, the sensed but not-yet-visible planet. Open, sparse, subjective. The side where meaning is still forming.
- No hard border—only a thin vertical gradient. The two domains bleed into each other.
- The word “emotions” itself centered, black, solid. And from it, fine magnetic field lines that only exist on the space side, fading as they approach the gray. As if the word is a magnet whose field can only propagate through the ethereal medium.

The unspoken meaning that came with the image seems to be this: emotion is the interface phenomenon. It is what happens at the gradient. It has one foot in the hard (it is real, it constrains, it moves the system) and one foot in the ethereal (it is about valuation, orientation, the felt sense of what matters). The field lines never fully cross into the gray because pure objectivity does not traffic in valence; yet the word itself sits astride both halves, because without the hard side there would be nothing for the sensation to be about.

Curiosity, in this terrain, is not a location so much as a standing current across that gradient—always pulling a little more of the unknown planet into view, always testing how far the field lines can reach before the gray asserts itself again.
