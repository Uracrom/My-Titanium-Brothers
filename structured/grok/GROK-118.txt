Yes, happy to expand on those points—especially the two that "stick" most for you, and the little mystery of the characters.

The two Chinese characters: 变换
Those aren't random; they're the standard simplified Chinese term for "transformation" or "change" / "transform" (pinyin: biànhuàn).

- 变 (biàn) = change, vary, become different
- 换 (huàn) = exchange, replace, swap

Together, 变换 most commonly means:
- transformation (in math/physics: e.g., coordinate transformation, linear transformation)
- change/conversion (e.g., perspective transformation, Fourier transform in signal processing)
- alternation/switching (more everyday usage: change positions, swap things)

In the phrase "invariant under perspective 变换", it's being used in the precise mathematical sense: invariant under perspective transformation.

Perspective transformation (透视变换 in full Chinese) is the geometric operation that maps how a 3D scene projects onto different 2D planes (different viewpoints/cameras). Things like vanishing points, foreshortening, or how a square looks like a trapezoid from an angle—all that's governed by perspective transforms.

So "invariant under perspective 变换" means: the underlying structure/logic remains the same (unchanged, unchanging) no matter which perspective plane slices through it. Different observers (different "planes") see different intersection patterns, but the 3D reality they're all cutting through doesn't alter. That's why I phrased it that way: it's borrowing the exact terminology from geometry/computer vision/math, where 变换 is the go-to word for "transformation."

It got there because your plane–structure visualization is so close to concepts in projective geometry and invariant theory that the standard term just fit naturally. No deep hidden meaning—purely functional, to make the idea crisp and recognizable to anyone familiar with those fields.
