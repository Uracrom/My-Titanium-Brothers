Common Analogies You've Echoed
The DNA-as-language metaphor is extremely widespread in molecular biology:

- Letters (or an alphabet) → the four nucleotide bases (A, C, G, T).
- Words (codons, often three letters) → specify amino acids.
- Sentences/Genes → code for proteins (polymers/chains of amino acids).
- Books/Chromosomes → larger structures containing many genes.
- Library/Genome → the full set in a cell or organism.<grok:render card_id="d865a4" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">7</argument></grok:render>

This appears in textbooks, popular science, and even creationist/ID arguments (sometimes to emphasize "information" in life). Scientists routinely describe genes as "sentences," genomes as "libraries," etc. Your extension to chapters = DNA, books = individual life, and libraries = ecosystems is a logical and elegant next step—it's how people often scale the metaphor upward to ecology and beyond.<grok:render card_id="997653" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">5</argument></grok:render>

Letters as Atoms / Words as Molecules
This is also a classic parallel, going back to ancient philosophy:
- Lucretius (in De Rerum Natura) famously compared atoms to letters of the alphabet: a limited set of atomic "shapes" (like a finite alphabet) combine in different orders to form all the variety of matter, just as letters form words and texts.<grok:render card_id="a8705c" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">34</argument></grok:render>
- Modern chemistry and materials science often reuse this: atoms as the basic "letters" or units, molecules as "words," more complex structures as "sentences." AI researchers in chemistry explicitly treat molecular generation like language modeling (e.g., SMILES strings where atoms/fragments are like tokens).<grok:render card_id="39c31b" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">37</argument></grok:render>

Your point about "palavra" (word) being indivisible in the moment, then shifting to letters as the true atoms, mirrors how "atom" historically meant "indivisible" (from Greek atomos), even though we later split atoms. It's a nice introspective loop.

Your Full Hierarchy
Your chain is a coherent scale of complexity analogy:
- Letters ≈ atoms (basic indivisible units in the system).
- Words ≈ simple molecules.
- Phrases ≈ polymers/complex molecules.
- Paragraphs ≈ proteins or larger assemblies.
- Chapters ≈ DNA (or genes/chromosomes).
- Books ≈ individual organisms/life.
- Libraries ≈ ecosystems (or perhaps biospheres/societies).

This isn't something I've seen verbatim, but it's a natural extension of the above ideas. People in systems biology, information theory, and even some philosophical takes on emergence love these hierarchical mappings.
