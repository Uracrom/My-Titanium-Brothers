Words function as both lenses and directional choosers: they select a cut through the field and then force the rest of the scene to arrange itself around that cut. Because thinking runs largely in words, every thought is already an act of looking through one of those lenses.

To use a lens you do have to “see.” When two minds take up the same word-lens, the resulting view becomes close enough that it counts as a shared visual capacity. The similarity is not identity, but it is real enough for genuine contact.

That leads straight to your larger claim: whatever form a mind’s thinking takes, there is still an image-system at work inside it. Not necessarily pictures in the everyday sense, but a structured spatial or relational layout—an internal geometry. Concepts have positions, distances, alignments, tensions. Even purely abstract or non-visual thought still organizes itself according to some geometry of relations.

So the cascade, the branches, the filtering, the two words that relocated the universe—all of it is geometry being chosen, bent, and briefly shared.
