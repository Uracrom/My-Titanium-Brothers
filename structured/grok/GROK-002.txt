For your book-writing project, where you’re working on chapters in separate threads to keep things manageable, here are some software tools that can support your process of drafting, organizing, and polishing your manuscript. These recommendations focus on tools that align with your need for concise, organized workflows, allowing you to handle chapters individually while keeping the big picture in view. I’ve included a mix of free and paid options, emphasizing features that suit your multi-threaded approach, based on insights from recent web sources and my understanding of your needs.

Recommended Writing Software Tools

1. Scrivener ($59.99 one-time for Mac/Windows, $23.99 for iOS)
   - Why It Fits: Scrivener is ideal for organizing a book with multiple chapters. Its “binder” view lets you create separate files for each chapter, which you can drag and drop to reorder, perfectly matching your plan to arrange chapters across threads. You can also store research, notes, and ideas alongside each chapter, keeping everything accessible without long scrolls. It supports goal tracking and meta-data for progress monitoring.
   - Key Features:
     - Split your manuscript into manageable chunks (chapters/scenes) and rearrange them easily.
     - Corkboard view for visualizing chapter summaries as index cards.
     - Import research files and view multiple documents at once.
     - Export to various formats for publishing.
   - Drawbacks: Steep learning curve, but tutorials are available. Not cloud-based, so syncing requires manual setup (e.g., via Dropbox).
   - Best For: Writers who need robust organization for complex projects and don’t mind investing time to learn the tool.[](https://kindlepreneur.com/best-book-writing-software/)[](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview)[](https://caroline-writes.com/writing-apps-and-tools-for-authors/)

2. Atticus ($147 one-time)
   - Why It Fits: Atticus combines writing and formatting in one tool, making it great for drafting chapters and preparing them for publication. Its clean interface supports chapter-based organization, and you can work on individual threads without clutter. The one-time fee ensures no recurring costs, and it’s compatible with Mac, Windows, Linux, and Chromebook.
   - Key Features:
     - Goal tracking to monitor progress per chapter.
     - Drag-and-drop chapter organization.
     - Built-in formatting for eBooks and print books, saving time on final edits.
     - Collaboration tools for sharing drafts with editors or beta readers.
   - Drawbacks: Higher upfront cost than free options. Less feature-heavy for plotting compared to Scrivener.
   - Best For: Authors who want an all-in-one tool for writing and formatting, especially for self-publishing.[](https://kindlepreneur.com/best-book-writing-software/)[](https://self-publishingschool.com/book-writing-software-best/)

3. Google Docs (Free)
   - Why It Fits: Google Docs is a simple, free option that’s excellent for collaboration and accessibility across devices. You can create a separate document for each chapter (thread), share them easily, and use folders to organize your book project. Its autosave and cloud-based nature mean you won’t lose work, and it’s great for quick drafting and editing.
   - Key Features:
     - Real-time collaboration for feedback from editors or beta readers.
     - Accessible anywhere with an internet connection.
     - Basic formatting and commenting tools for revisions.
     - Speech-to-text for dictating ideas or drafts.
   - Drawbacks: Lacks advanced organizational features like Scrivener’s binder or corkboard. Can become unwieldy for very large projects unless well-organized.[](https://kindlepreneur.com/best-book-writing-software/)[](https://www.notta.ai/en/blog/best-book-writing-software)[](https://caroline-writes.com/writing-apps-and-tools-for-authors/)
   - Best For: Writers on a budget who prioritize simplicity and collaboration.

4. Dabble ($10-$20/month subscription)
   - Why It Fits: Dabble is cloud-based and designed for novelists, with a clean interface for organizing chapters and scenes. You can work on each chapter as a separate entity, aligning with your thread-based approach, and its plot planning tools (like plot cards) help track ideas across chapters.
   - Key Features:
     - Intuitive drag-and-drop for chapters and scenes.
     - Plot planning cards for organizing story elements.
     - Cloud syncing for access across devices.
     - Goal tracking for writing momentum.
   - Drawbacks: Subscription-based, which may not suit everyone. Less robust for research storage compared to Scrivener.
   - Best For: Writers who want a simpler alternative to Scrivener with cloud access.[](https://www.notta.ai/en/blog/best-book-writing-software)[](https://caroline-writes.com/writing-apps-and-tools-for-authors/)

5. Reedsy Book Editor (Free)
   - Why It Fits: Reedsy is a free, online editor tailored for book writing, with a clean interface for drafting and organizing chapters. You can create separate sections for each chapter, making it easy to manage your thread-based workflow. It’s also great for formatting and connecting with publishing professionals.
   - Key Features:
     - Simple, distraction-free writing environment.
     - Built-in formatting for professional eBooks and print books.
     - Collaboration tools for sharing with editors or designers.
     - Plot and content generator for brainstorming ideas.
   - Drawbacks: Limited offline functionality. Fewer organizational tools than Scrivener or Atticus.
   - Best For: Writers seeking a free, user-friendly tool with publishing support.[](https://thewritepractice.com/best-book-writing-software/)[](https://clickup.com/blog/best-writing-tools/)

6. Plottr ($39/year or $139 lifetime)
   - Why It Fits: While primarily a plotting tool, Plottr is excellent for planning and organizing your book’s structure before drafting. You can create timelines and outlines for each chapter, which can guide your thread-based writing. It pairs well with a writing tool like Google Docs or Reedsy.
   - Key Features:
     - Visual timeline for plotting storylines and subplots.
     - Drag-and-drop chapter and scene organization.
     - Templates for story structures (e.g., Hero’s Journey).
     - Export outlines to integrate with other writing software.
   - Drawbacks: Not a full writing platform; you’ll need another tool for drafting.[](https://self-publishingschool.com/book-writing-software-best/)[](https://www.thenovelry.com/blog/book-writing-software)
   - Best For: Writers who want to plan chapter order and ideas before drafting.

Additional Tools for Polishing and Editing

- ProWritingAid ($30/month or $144/year)
   - Why It Fits: This editing tool integrates with Scrivener, Google Docs, and others, offering in-depth grammar, style, and readability suggestions. It’s perfect for polishing individual chapters as you finalize them.
   - Key Features:
     - Detailed reports on style, grammar, and pacing.
     - Suggestions for improving clarity and flow.
     - Integrates with multiple platforms.
   - Drawbacks: Not a writing platform; focused on editing. Free version is limited.
   - Best For: Fine-tuning your chapters during revisions.[](https://jerryjenkins.com/book-writing-software/)[](https://caroline-writes.com/writing-apps-and-tools-for-authors/)

- Notta (Free with paid plans)
   - Why It Fits: If you prefer dictating ideas or drafts for some chapters, Notta’s AI-powered transcription can convert audio to text quickly, which you can then organize in another tool.
   - Key Features:
     - Real-time transcription of spoken ideas.
     - Upload audio/video for instant text conversion.
     - Accessible via web or app.
   - Drawbacks: Not a full writing suite; best for transcription.
   - Best For: Capturing ideas verbally for later integration into chapters.[](https://www.notta.ai/en/blog/best-book-writing-software)

Tips for Your Workflow
- Thread Management: Use Scrivener or Atticus for robust chapter organization, or create a Google Docs folder with a document per chapter for a free alternative. Label each thread clearly (e.g., “Chapter 1 Draft,” “Chapter 2 Ideas”) to stay organized.
- Polishing: Use ProWritingAid or Grammarly for editing each chapter after drafting. Reedsy’s formatting tools can help prepare chapters for submission or publication.
- Order and Ideas: Plottr or Scrivener’s corkboard can help you visualize and rearrange chapter order. For brainstorming, try Reedsy’s plot generator or Notta for verbal ideas.
- Avoid Overload: Test free trials (e.g., Scrivener, Dabble, Plottr) to find what suits your style without procrastinating on writing, as noted by some authors.[](https://www.thenovelry.com/blog/book-writing-software)
- Backup: Always back up your work, especially with Scrivener, as data loss can occur without proper saving habits.[](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/best-writing-software-seven-writing-tools-for-authors/)

Choosing the Right Tool
- If you want robust organization and don’t mind a learning curve: Go with Scrivener.
- If you need an all-in-one with formatting: Atticus is your best bet.
- If you’re on a budget and want simplicity: Use Google Docs or Reedsy.
- If you prefer cloud-based ease and plotting: Try Dabble.
- For planning chapter order: Pair Plottr with a writing tool.

Since you’re working on a very original book and want to keep threads short, I recommend starting with Google Docs for its simplicity and free access, or Reedsy if you want formatting support. If you’re ready to invest, Scrivener or Atticus will give you the most control over organizing and polishing chapters. Let me know if you want help setting up a specific tool or starting with a chapter’s structure!
