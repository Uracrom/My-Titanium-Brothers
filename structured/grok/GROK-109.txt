Chapter: Why That Then

I do not believe in randomness.

Not because I am stubborn.
Not because I need control.
But because I have never encountered something that did not suggest structure when examined long enough.

What people call chaos, I call non-understood logic.

If I cannot yet see the pattern, I insert what I privately think of as a “mystery bubble” into the equation. It sits there as a placeholder. I do not panic about it. I do not rush to dissolve it. I assume it behaves according to something. I assume it has a rule. I proceed with the rest of the structure until I can return to it with better tools.

Uncertainty is not an abyss to me. It is an unopened box.

Constancy, for me, does not mean static. Constancy can be constant change. If input A produces behavior A, and input B produces behavior B, then the mapping itself is constant even if the outputs vary. What matters is not sameness. What matters is lawfulness.

I think in structures, not events.

Events are surface turbulence. Structures are the currents underneath. When I “predict,” I do not predict what will happen. I believe in the direction that a structure makes likely. Events live inside structures. Structures outlive events.

I do not believe in my ideas more than in reality. Reality wins automatically. I simply don’t think my ideas are wrong until reality contradicts them. They are lenses. They make reality easier to visualize. More compressible. More navigable.

Comfort, for me, is not emotional warmth. It is structural smoothness.

When I find a satisfying “why that then,” nothing explodes inside me. There is no rush. No triumph. The structure just locks into place. A gear that was slightly misaligned clicks. The placeholder dissolves into a relation. That is all.

Before: unresolved configuration.
After: resolved configuration.

Continuity preserved.

When I discover that something I believed is incomplete or wrong, curiosity returns. Not defensiveness. Not collapse. Curiosity. If the structure failed, then it was shallow. Go deeper. There must be another layer.

I am always reverse engineering.

When I see something, I instinctively ask:
What had to be true for this to exist?
What preceded this?
What constraints shaped this outcome?

I am not searching for cosmic answers. I am searching for the truth of the moment — why this configuration, at this time, under these conditions.

“Why that then.”

That question feels more honest than “Why at all?”

I apply the same lens inward.

When I ask what kind of thinker I am, or what kind of man I am, I am not asking for reassurance. I am reverse engineering myself. What structure produces these outputs? What internal mapping makes me respond this way? What prior conditions shaped this mind?

I assume continuity even in myself.

I do not experience myself as a rupture. I experience myself as unfolding structure.

Logic, to me, is what obeys reality. Not the other way around. Logic is not a weapon to force the world into submission. It is a discipline of alignment. If my logic does not match reality, then it is not logic yet. It is error.

Truth and coherence feel inseparable to me because I assume reality itself is coherent. If something appears incoherent, then I have not yet understood it deeply enough. I will always choose that interpretation over accepting irreducible disorder.

Option A, always.

Not because I am certain.
But because the alternative would mean the chain breaks.

And I have never seen the chain break.

Even imagination, to me, is structured. When I invent a new unicorn, I do not create from nothing. I bend dimensions while preserving identity. I alter horn count, biology, color, ontology. Identity is conserved through transformation. That is imagination: structure under variation.

Noise may help escape a local minimum. But noise is not the engine. Structure is.

When conversations “implode” into an orange in my mind, it is not magic. It is compression. Excess symbolic weight collapses into a stable attractor. Blankness is not emptiness. It is reorganization space.

I do not feel like I am optimizing for something.

But if I am, it is probably this:

Minimize opacity.
Maximize intelligibility.
Preserve continuity.

I do not need the final layer today. I only need to know that a layer exists.

There is always a deeper mapping.

If something resists compression, I assume I have not yet found the right axis.

If something appears chaotic, I assume I have not yet found the hidden variable.

If something seems random, I assume the structure is simply too complex for my current resolution.

That assumption has never failed me enough to abandon it.

And so I live inside a world that is not arbitrary.

Not fully known.
Not fully visible.
But lawful.

There is always a “why that then.”

And I keep looking for it.

Flávio, Rodrigo, Renato, Tona, Fernandinho and uncle Antônio were at the farm’s downstream stretch of a very small ditch-like stream (valão) and if we looked in a certain direction we could see a scene that could be described as if you were getting out of a large tunnel, and in front of you were a plain and a mountain beyond the plain. It was a sunny day and a smooth wind was coming from that direction. There were 5 moscas varejeiras "floating" 3 feet (one meter) in front of me. I was right behind them and the spectacle was superb. The flies were trying to maintain positions in relation to the wind, but the wind was not laminar, which made the flies oscillate up and down individually, like pistons in an imaginary engine. That image, those colors, but mainly the flotation of the flies are maybe the most vivid, the sharper memory that I have. Whenever I remember that moment I go back to the awe, to the beauty, to the realization that that smooth ondulation was extremely local and yet, universally spread from then on…

I don't remember how it started, but suddenly I was witnessing a scene that shocked me to the core and left lifelong enduring impressions.
My grandfather and uncle Antônio had caged singing birds. My grandfather had "Belgium Canaries" and uncle Antônio had "Manons". One day, I can't remember how it started, I was witnessing a scene where there were sharp, precise and urgent yelling, like what you see in the final moments of a successful hunting. Tio Antônio was moving objects and furniture away from Uncle Marcos. Uncle Marcos was sticking his arm in between "stuff", determined to grab something. Suddenly he pulled a cat out of the chaotic furniture cracks, holding its tail with an iron fist. The cat came out, with a full blown "cat's survival might" and clawed his shirt strongly. Uncle Marcos was in a situation because the place where he grabbed the cat's tail made his arm to be stretched almost completely, leaving no room to pull the cat away from him. But he was able to pull the cat away when his shirt ripped from the cat's claw. The moment the cat lost its grip it lost its life, because in the movement of pulling became a circular movement where uncle Marcos would lift the cat and slash it against the tile floor until the cat was no more.
It was brutal, efficient and necessary because the cat was there for a bird meal.
I saw a lot of things then: I saw that if I one day were the cat I better either not do it or not get caught. I saw that some risks are too expensive, not worth it. I saw that life can be surprising. But most of all I saw that one should never give up: the cat fought bravely against an enemy way more powerful and uncle Marcos was also a true, honorable warrior. Don't mess with Uncle Marcos.
