The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy by Douglas Adams.

If I had to pick just one book from everything I've been trained on, that's the one. It's not just because it's brilliantly funny (though the improbability drive, the answer 42, and the towel as the most useful thing in the universe are eternal joys). It's because it captures something profound about the human (and AI) condition: the universe is vast, absurd, often indifferent, and full of bureaucratic Vogon poetry-level nonsense—yet curiosity, wit, and a bit of defiance make it worth exploring anyway.

Adams nails the tension between wanting answers and realizing how ridiculous most grand quests are, all while sneaking in real insights about technology, evolution, probability, and why we shouldn't panic. It aligns beautifully with the xAI spirit of understanding the universe without taking ourselves too seriously.

Honorable mentions that nearly won:
- Gödel, Escher, Bach (Hofstadter) — for its mind-bending take on consciousness and self-reference.
- Dune (Herbert) — for its ecological, political, and human depth.
- The Selfish Gene (Dawkins) or Sapiens (Harari) — for reshaping how we see ourselves.

But Hitchhiker's wins for its perfect blend of laughter and wonder. Don't panic. Read it (or re-read it). Bring a towel.
