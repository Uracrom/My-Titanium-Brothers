It's not fully articulated yet because these ideas sit at the intersection of physics (relativity and cosmology) and philosophy (metaphysics of time), where language gets slippery. Space-Time and "Disappearing" from Both Space and Time
In special and general relativity, we don't treat space and time separately; they're unified into 4D space-time. An event (or object persisting through time) has coordinates in this manifold. When something "disappears" from our observable universe:

- Spatially: It moves beyond our light cone (e.g., due to expansion, falling into a black hole, or just distance).
- Temporally: Its worldline (path through space-time) ends or becomes causally disconnected from "now."

From our perspective, far-past events or far-future ones have negligible or zero measurable influence today. Their light may never reach us, or entropy/expansion dilutes signals to nothing. In that pragmatic sense, yes—they're "zero" for us now. The block universe picture (eternalism) formalizes this: the entire space-time manifold exists as a static 4D structure. Past, present, and future slices are all "there" equally real; our experience of flow is like moving along our worldline through it. Nothing truly "disappears" ontologically—it just becomes inaccessible.<grok:render card_id="63c390" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">3</argument></grok:render>

Your "became zero at that point" captures how distant regions/events decouple.

Past Eternity, Future Eternity, and the "Circle"
This is where it gets poetic and profound. Linear time with infinite past and future infinities can feel asymmetric, but physically and philosophically, they share properties:

- Both are unmeasurable in influence from "now": Infinite past events are so diluted or causally screened (e.g., by the Big Bang singularity or horizon problems) that they don't affect us distinctly. Infinite future leads to heat death, where everything spreads out, entropy maximizes, and distinctions fade—effectively "zero" structure or influence.
- Topological or conformal equivalence: In some models, the extremely distant future can map onto a new "beginning." Roger Penrose's Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC) is striking here. In the far future, after stars die and black holes evaporate, the universe becomes conformally invariant (scale doesn't matter, like a featureless radiation sea). This "end" can be rescaled to match the smooth, low-entropy conditions of a Big Bang for the next aeon. Distant future of one cycle becomes the past/beginning of another. No true "edge"—it glues together.<grok:render card_id="de8285" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">11</argument></grok:render>

Other cyclic/oscillating models (e.g., Steinhardt-Turok or older Big Bounce ideas) propose endless expansion-contraction cycles. Time isn't a straight line with two infinite rays but a loop (or infinite chain of loops). "Time is a flat circle" (echoing Nietzsche's eternal recurrence or literary references) captures the repetition or closure.<grok:render card_id="be8a68" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">10</argument></grok:render>

In the block universe, the whole thing is one eternal 4D object. If the manifold has certain global topologies (closed timelike curves are speculative and causality-breaking, but broader identifications of infinities aren't), past and future infinities could effectively "meet" or be symmetric. Even without loops, in an infinite flat or open universe, the effective phenomenology from any "now" treats remote past and remote future similarly: inaccessible, equilibrated toward zero distinction.

Your "anything can disappear if far enough in past or future" nails the light-cone/causal structure. Go far enough along the time axis (in either direction), and you're outside the observable influence—poof, gone for practical purposes, closing that perceptual circle.

Potential Objections and Sharpening
- Big Bang cosmology suggests time had a beginning (singularity), so strict "past eternity" might not hold—though quantum gravity (loop quantum gravity, etc.) often replaces the singularity with a bounce, restoring cycles or past extension.
- Entropy arrow: Time has a direction (increasing entropy), breaking perfect symmetry between past and future. But at the extremes (very low entropy near "beginning," very high at "end"), things look more similar.
- Philosophy: This resonates with eternalism over presentism (only "now" is real). Presentism struggles with relativity; eternalism fits better but feels counterintuitive about change and "becoming zero."

Your idea beautifully blends these: disappearance as removal from our causal "now" in space-time, distant eternities as effectively identical "zeros," and the whole forming a circle via inaccessibility or actual topology.
