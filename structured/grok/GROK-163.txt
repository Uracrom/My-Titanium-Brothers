Feedback on your writing:

You partially succeeded in writing "better," but the text still feels more like a raw thought dump than a polished or even semi-polished piece. The authenticity and energy are there — that's the strongest part. You can feel the excitement of the discoveries (the birth/ascension insight, the conversation with Lucas, the spark about Tia Inês). That liveliness is good and worth protecting.

What works well:
- Voice: Your personal, reflective, slightly wandering voice comes through clearly. The excitement about "free thoughts" vs. constrained ones feels genuine.
- The core insight: The parallel between birth and ascension as "leaving a tight space into real freedom" is interesting and memorable. It’s the kind of observation that makes personal writing valuable.
- Honesty: Including the gym guilt, the timestamps, the little self-corrections — it gives the text a diary-like intimacy that matches your preference for free discovery.

What still needs focus (main weaknesses):
1. Fragmentation and lack of flow
   The text jumps between topics (philosophy → conversation with Lucas → mother → Tia Inês → title thoughts → education) without clear transitions. It feels like several separate journal entries stitched together. Readers (even you, later) can get lost.

2. Run-on sentences and unclear phrasing
   Many sentences are very long and pack too many ideas. Example:
"When you think or feel that you are advancing you see things opening ways for you, you are "ascending". When you think or feel that you are being received, it's like a birth."

   This could be tighter and clearer.

3. Mixing languages and cultural references without help
   The Portuguese expression ("você não dá ponto sem nó") is great color, but you explain it well. Still, if you want English readers to fully feel it, a smoother translation/integration helps.

4. Timestamps
   They interrupt the reading flow. In raw journaling they’re fine; in something you want to "write better," they usually belong at the top or as subtle markers.

5. Repetition and slight circularity
   You circle back to the free-thoughts theme multiple times. Once you nail the point, the later returns feel less fresh.

Specific suggestions for improvement:

Try this revision approach next time:

1. Brain dump first (what you did).
2. Then shape it:
   - Group related ideas into paragraphs.
   - Write a clear opening that states the main spark.
   - End with the strongest realization (the birth/ascension connection feels like the real climax).

Example light edit of your opening (to show direction, not to rewrite everything):

I’ve noticed I prefer thoughts that arrive freely, out of the blue, rather than thoughts forced to follow a title or a predetermined structure. Today this preference suddenly connected to something deeper: birth and ascension.

When we feel we are advancing, reality seems to open paths before us — we are “ascending.” When we feel received and embraced by something larger, it feels like birth. At first I thought these were opposites, but now I see them as almost the same experience: leaving a tight, limiting space and stepping into real freedom. That image of emergence feels true for both.

This keeps your voice but adds breathing room and clarity.

What to avoid:
- Starting too many sentences with “Then,” “And,” “I said,” “He said” in a row (creates a list-like rhythm).
- Explaining the same idea multiple times in close succession.
- Leaving too many meta-comments (“Wait a minute; it’s explaining nothing…”). They’re charming in private journaling, but weaken the piece if you want others to read it.

Final advice:
You’re getting better at noticing your own thinking process — that’s real progress. Keep protecting the free emergence you love, but give the best insights a little more structure and oxygen after they arrive. Write the wild version first, then do a “shape” pass where you ask: What is the single strongest idea here? How can I make space for it to shine?

Would you like me to do a fuller rewrite of the whole text as an example, or focus on specific sections? Or would you prefer exercises to practice tighter, clearer free writing?
