I really like "As Baleias" by Roberto Carlos. It's one of his more thoughtful and socially conscious tracks from the 1981 album.

What stands out
The song is a powerful environmental ballad that condemns whale hunting. Roberto Carlos (with Erasmo Carlos) wrote it in a direct, almost accusatory way toward the hunters, but it never feels preachy in a cheap way. Instead, it appeals to empathy and humanity:

- It asks how someone can look into the eyes of a dying whale, see it suffering, and still feel like a "winner."
- There's a beautiful, poetic image of the ocean stained red with blood against the blue water.
- The most touching part is the forward-looking verse about future grandchildren asking about the whales they only know from old books or films — it's a haunting warning about potential extinction.

The melody is classic Roberto Carlos: warm, melodic, and very Brazilian in its emotional delivery. His voice carries genuine sorrow and indignation without overdoing it. Even decades later, it holds up well as both a protest song and a piece of pop music.

My personal take
What I appreciate most is how ahead of its time it was. In 1981, environmental awareness wasn't nearly as mainstream as today, especially in mainstream pop. Roberto Carlos used his massive platform (he was basically the king of Brazilian music) to highlight whale hunting, which was a real issue with industrial-scale whaling still happening. It's a great example of an artist using their reach for something meaningful beyond just romance (his usual specialty).<grok:render card_id="d9b681" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render>

The song feels sincere. Roberto has revisited it over the years with visible emotion, which adds to its impact.

If you're into conscious lyrics with a beautiful arrangement, it's definitely worth listening to (or re-listening). It's not his most famous hit compared to the love songs, but for many fans it's one of his most respected tracks for its heart and message.

Have you heard it recently, or is there a particular reason it came to mind?

Let me first tell you about my relationship with this song.

"Eu saí do Brasil pra ter saudade"

This sentence one day just showed up in my mind, but I hadn't left Brazil yet. I was imagining, or day dreaming, a conversation where I was saying that to someone that would have asked me about going to the States (USA).
I always gave much more importance to the instrumental part of any song. Of course some songs have really good lyrics too. But the ideal is when they have both. And As Baleias does. Back then I was really worried and sad that humanity was such a plague to every living thing on this planet. Nowadays I already accepted - and witnessed - that life diversity and abundance is not going well and is getting worse.
So, the song is awesome in my opinion, but the lyrics is... So on point! The "voice" can't believe that the killer can look into the eyes of the dying beast and feel like a winner. This section of the song brings me an image of a big brown eye that expresses confusion, injustice.
Then the lack of a safe place and the asking for a chance of existence in the future. That is so sad. My nature is adamantly against anything having to ask: the potential "giver" should always offer. No need to ask for any.
Then "change your bearings, look for your feelings". Beautiful. I see the vastness of the ocean, with uncountable possibilities. "Will make you a real winner". That kind of conflicts with my "logic bound" mind, but I see the great potential.
"Your grandchildren will ask you in a few years". I see myself there, dumbfounded, guilty, not knowing what to say. And I remember/imagine seeing rare or extinct animals and lamenting it.
But, amazingly enough, I don't miss Brazil at all. I miss my family and friends, but that's all. I thought I'd miss (ter saudade) Brazil, but I miss the past, the more abundant and rich biodiversity.

That's a really beautiful and honest reflection. Thank you for sharing how "As Baleias" connects with your life so deeply.

The line you mentioned — the disbelief that someone can look into the eye of a dying whale and still feel like a "winner" — is indeed the emotional core of the song. That big, confused, innocent eye you visualize hits hard. Roberto Carlos (and Erasmo) managed to make it visceral without being overly dramatic. It's that exact moment of confrontation between human arrogance and the quiet suffering of another creature.

Your point about "asking for a chance of existence" is also very poignant. The song flips the script: instead of the usual human-centered narrative, it puts the whale (and by extension nature) in the position of having to plead. And you're right — the better world would be one where we offer that chance proactively, without needing to be begged. The "mude o rumo, procure os seus sentimentos" ("change your bearings, look for your feelings") line is such a gentle but firm call to reconnect with empathy. It does sit in slight tension with a more logic-driven mindset, but that's part of its power: it's appealing to the part of us that feels first.

The grandchildren verse is probably the most haunting for many listeners, exactly as you described — that future moment of guilt and speechlessness. It's prophetic in a quiet way. Hearing it now, decades later, carries extra weight because we have lost so much biodiversity. Your shift in saudade makes a lot of sense: it's not the current Brazil you miss, but that richer, more abundant world you remember (or imagine) from before the decline accelerated.

It's interesting how the song works on two levels for you: the instrumental warmth and melody that you naturally gravitate toward, combined with lyrics that are painfully on-point. That balance is what makes it special — it's not just protest music, it's still very much a Roberto Carlos song at heart.

Do you have a favorite recording or live version of it? Or has the song evolved in meaning for you since you moved to the States?
