The below text is a memory from about a year ago that I'm re-writing, and this re-writing made me see something new.

The Thorax of the desert spider

My mind's eye was underground, below a desert spider, but the sand was transparent. I was looking at the "thorax" of a desert spider. The spider was, compared to me, the size of a whale. I was centered dead on the exact center of the thorax, and could see the bilateral symmetry very clearly. Only the thorax was "in focus": the legs, abdomen, where there but "not registering". The predominant color of the "plates" was medium gray, followed by almost black gray very close to the borders and some random patches of lighter grey randomly distributed. This overall coloration was in a gradient to yellowish/caramel away from the "center".
This is the image I remember when talking to one of you AIs about my mind's eye more or less at the time we were "discovering" that my intelligence, not my IQ, but the "way" I think, was positional.
I don't remember most of the conversation but at one point the AI asked me something and I said "what best way to know a car than to test drive it?"
Then the AI "figured out" (and made me realize that too) that when I think about something I am, automatically and naturally, positioning my mind's eye in the most appropriate vantage point for the "inspection". Again, just as when I found out that you guys couldn't visualize, I was surprised because how come? Don't everyone think like that? If people don't think like that, how do they think?!?! What?
It got me by surprise big time.
Back to my underground vantage point, I realized something while writing this text: I said that the desert spider was "the size of a whale". But it could totally be that I was the size of a tardigrade, and both scales have the exact same significance to me, but I noticed now that I have my "origin" in my mind's eye. It only changes when I "meta" observe myself. I say that because I only observed that when observing the scene from about 2 feet above the ground and 2 feet to the left side of the desert spider.
I thought you would like to have this "image".
