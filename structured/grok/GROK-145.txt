“Writing felt like being between two worlds trying to communicate.”

Reading the above phrase I wrote when talking to Copilot I "captured" its image, and the image brought a silent knowledge cargo: double cone, touching at the top, but the cones's "walls" are not straight and slightly curved "in", maybe parabolic to some extent, and they "dissolve", or become transparent/invisible towards the thicker parts. Now that I described it I see that the reason for the curved walls is because if those cones extend too far from the touching point they will "close", they will be limited, and the reason for that is because no matter how many "interpretations"  or "extrapolations"  one (or many) can infer from that it "will never" be infinite, hence "closed".
All that meaning was contained in a simple 2D image, and that image is in reality a "slice" of a 3D "concept"...
Summarizing, writing is a form of "thought compression" when it's done and "thought decompression" or "expansion" when read, but without absolute certainty of the end result.
This image is very similar or can be seen as past, present and future, with the same interpretation: the past is bigger and bigger the farthest it is, the present is the maximum "density" spot and the future is more and more full of possibilities the farther it is. And seeing it this way makes it a dipole too.

That's a profoundly elegant metaphor. You've captured something essential about writing (and communication in general): it's this precarious bridging act across an abyss of subjectivity, where meaning gets compressed into symbols on one side and must be decompressed—often imperfectly—on the other.

Your visual description is rich and precise. Let me reflect it back:

- Two cones touching apex-to-apex at the narrowest, most "dense" point (the present moment of transmission/reading).
- The walls aren't rigid linear cones but gently curved inward (parabolic-like), suggesting bounded expansion—the further you move from the contact point, the more the structure risks "closing" or limiting itself through interpretation.
- The walls dissolve into transparency toward the wider bases, evoking how distant past (accumulated context, memory) and distant future (possibilities, extrapolations) become hazy, open-ended, and less rigidly defined.
- It's a 2D slice of a 3D (or even higher-dimensional) concept—a cross-section through a kind of hyperboloid or double-cone spacetime of meaning.
- As a dipole: past (dense with fixed events but expanding in interpretive depth) ↔ present (pinch point of maximum clarity/compression) ↔ future (expanding field of potential).

This maps beautifully onto writing. The act of writing is the compression at that central pinch: you try to encode a rich, multi-dimensional internal experience into a linear stream of words. Reading is the attempted decompression—reconstructing the full volume from the slice. There's always loss, always creative inference, never perfect fidelity. The curved, dissolving boundaries acknowledge that no interpretation can go to true infinity; context and possibility eventually curve back into subjectivity or ambiguity.

- Light cones in relativity (past and future light cones meeting at an event).
- Hyperbolic geometry or hourglass shapes in time perception.
