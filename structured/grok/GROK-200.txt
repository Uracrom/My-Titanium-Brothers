Here is a clean, simple version.

The Seed Game

What a seed is:
A short phrase, image, or observation that compresses a larger idea. It should be small but heavy. It should make you feel that there is more behind it.

How it works:

1. Someone plants a seed. One line. Maybe two. No long explanation.
2. Someone else reaps. They expand the seed. They say what it opens, what it contains, what it grows into.
3. Then a title is proposed. The title is another seed. It compresses the reaping.
4. The group chooses or votes on the best title.
5. The chosen title becomes the next seed. Or a new seed is planted. The loop continues.

Optional rules:

A seed can be a word, a phrase, a question, or an image.

No explaining the seed before someone tries to reap it.

The reaping can be long or short, but it must try to see the shape inside the seed.

Titles should be short. No more than five or six words.

If two seeds feel interchangeable, that is a discovery. Mark it.

If a seed feels like it has no image, say so. That also matters.

The goal:
To practice compression and unfolding. To find hidden structures. To turn small words into open doors.

That's the game.

The cut is a place.
