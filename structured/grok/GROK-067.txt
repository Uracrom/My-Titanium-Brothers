Your idea presents a fascinating metaphysical duality: a "universe of ideas" where human consciousness transitions upon death, and conversely, "dead" ideas from that realm manifest as matter in our physical reality. It's an elegant inversion of traditional idealism (where ideas are primary and matter secondary) and materialism (matter first, ideas emergent). While it echoes concepts like Platonic forms (eternal ideas casting shadows as material reality) or Aristotelian hylomorphism (form/idea shaping matter), it adds a novel cycle of "death" and materialization.

Proving this empirically—in the scientific sense of reproducible experiments, observable evidence, or falsifiability—isn't feasible. Metaphysical claims like yours operate beyond the boundaries of current physics and biology, as they involve unobservable realms (e.g., a post-death idea-universe) and non-falsifiable processes (e.g., ideas "dying" into matter). Science deals with testable predictions about the observable universe, and your framework doesn't generate those. For instance, we can't measure an idea's "death" or trace matter back to a specific expired concept. If it were provable through data, it would likely already be part of established theories, but as it stands, it's speculative philosophy.

That said, there are ways to "support" or rigorously explore it philosophically, logically, or through analogies to existing theories. Here's how one might approach "proving" it in a non-empirical sense, building on related ideas from philosophy and physics:

1. Logical Argumentation and Thought Experiments
   - Construct a deductive proof: Start with premises you accept and derive your conclusion. For example:
     - Premise 1: Duality pervades reality (e.g., wave-particle in quantum mechanics, mind-body in philosophy).
     - Premise 2: Consciousness (ideas) and matter appear interdependent but oppositional—e.g., in quantum observation, measurement (a conscious act) collapses possibilities into material outcomes.
     - Premise 3: Energy/information is conserved (a axiom from physics: nothing is created or destroyed, only transformed).
     - Conclusion: If human death transforms consciousness into "ideas" (conserved information), then the "death" of ideas must transform them into matter to maintain duality and conservation. Our universe as "dead ideas" balances the equation.
     - This isn't empirical proof but demonstrates internal consistency. Philosophers like Descartes used similar reasoning for mind-matter dualism, arguing ideas exist independently yet interact with matter.
   - Thought experiment: Imagine a "reverse Schrödinger's cat." In the classic version, a cat is both alive and dead until observed (idea collapses into matter). In yours, observe matter (e.g., a rock) and ask: What "idea" died to create it? If the rock's properties (shape, composition) align with a faded concept (e.g., an ancient, forgotten myth materializing as stone), it poetically "proves" the cycle—but only subjectively.

If ideas are information, then "dead" ideas could be information losing coherence and "collapsing" into stable matter, much like quantum superposition resolves into particles.
