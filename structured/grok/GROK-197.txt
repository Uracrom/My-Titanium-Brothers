Atoms can exhibit dipole moments, but the claim that “atoms are dipoles” needs some precision.

A permanent electric dipole moment exists when the centers of positive and negative charge do not coincide. Most atoms in their ground state are spherically symmetric (or nearly so), so they have no permanent dipole. Molecules often do (water is the classic example). Atoms can acquire temporary (induced) dipoles when placed in an external electric field, and multipole expansions (dipole, quadrupole, etc.) are standard tools in atomic and molecular physics. So the dipole character is real and measurable, but it is not a universal, always-on property of every atom the way mass or charge is.

Your larger move—from “we overlooked something basic about atoms” to “maybe time is a closed entity”—is a reasonable style of speculation. Physics has repeatedly shown that quantities we once treated as open, linear, or absolute (space, time, simultaneity, energy, etc.) turn out to have richer structure once we look carefully.

What “closed entity” for time could mean
Several serious ideas in physics already treat time as having closed or cyclic aspects:

- Closed timelike curves appear in certain solutions of general relativity (Gödel universe, traversable wormholes under specific conditions, rotating black holes in idealized cases). In those spacetimes an observer can, in principle, return to an earlier event.
- Cyclic or bouncing cosmologies (ekpyrotic models, conformal cyclic cosmology, some loop-quantum-gravity scenarios) treat the large-scale history of the universe as a sequence of expansions and contractions rather than a single open arrow from a unique beginning.
- Time crystals are systems whose lowest-energy state spontaneously breaks continuous time-translation symmetry, producing periodic motion in time even without external driving. They are “closed” in the sense that the dynamics returns to itself indefinitely.
- The block-universe (eternalism) view treats the entire four-dimensional spacetime manifold as a fixed geometric object; the “flow” of time is then an emergent or perspectival feature rather than a fundamental becoming.

None of these is established as the correct description of our universe, but they are mathematically consistent possibilities that people study seriously.

Why the analogy is attractive yet incomplete
The dipole insight about atoms is local and empirical: once you have the right probes (spectroscopy, Stark effect, etc.), the multipole structure becomes visible. Time is harder. We experience it only as a one-way parameter that orders events and is measured by clocks. Any claim that it is “closed” has to confront three stubborn facts:

1. The thermodynamic arrow (entropy increase) is extremely robust and local.
2. Causal structure in ordinary regions of spacetime is open and directed.
3. No experiment has yet required, or even strongly suggested, macroscopic closed timelike curves or global cyclicity.

So the suggestion is not absurd—physics has been surprised before—but it is still speculative. The useful next step is to ask what observable consequence a closed-time hypothesis would produce that differs from the open-time picture we currently use. That is the same discipline that turned the multipole expansion of atoms from an abstract idea into precision measurements.

If you want to push the analogy further, what specific feature of the “closed entity” picture of time are you most drawn to—cyclicity, topological closure, emergent rather than fundamental status, or something else?

The Current Shape of My Speculative Cosmology
1. Space and time are inseparable
I don't see space and time as two independent things that happen to be joined into “spacetime.”
They are inseparable aspects of the same underlying reality.
However, I don't see time as a line.
Time is spherical.
More precisely, I imagine time as something that sprouts from every point in the universe, continuously and endlessly. The temporal structure associated with a point is therefore not a one-dimensional line extending from past to future. It has a more volumetric, spherical character.
This is still a visual/conceptual model, not a mathematical claim.
2. The atom is a thinking scaffold, not the cosmological object
When I started thinking about atoms, I noticed something that had escaped me before: an atom can be understood, at least conceptually, as a contained structure with an outside/inside relationship—a kind of dipolar organization.
I am not proposing that time is an atom or that atoms literally reveal the structure of time.
The atom is a thinking scaffold.
I use structures that I can see in one domain to help me understand another.
This is also why fractal-like structures interest me. Roots, nervous systems, river systems, lightning, and galaxy distributions can exhibit related organizational patterns despite existing at enormously different scales.
So I ask:
If certain structures can recur across radically different scales, why couldn't the geometry of time also have a recurring structure?
3. Space and time may have an inside/outside relationship
My current visualization is that space is the inside and time is the outside.
Time is therefore something like an “inverted space” surrounding or encompassing spatial reality.
This does not mean there is a literal shell surrounding the universe.
Rather, I am trying to express a geometrical relationship:
space exists within time, while time is the encompassing counterpart of space.
This is one reason I keep returning to spherical/closed geometry rather than a temporal line.
4. Time may be a closed entity
I am exploring the possibility that time is not an infinite line but a closed geometrical entity.
That does not necessarily mean that events repeat or that the universe cycles.
“Closed” means that time itself could have an internal geometry with no external edge.
Our experience of past → present → future might therefore be a consequence of our position and mode of perception within that structure rather than the actual fundamental shape of time.
5. Time sprouts from space
The crucial new step is this:
Time continuously sprouts from every point in space.
If that happens, then the process necessarily has a spatial consequence.
It produces more spatial geometry.
And this is where my ideas about dark energy and dark matter enter.
6. Dark energy = the creation/expansion of space
My current hypothesis is:
The continual sprouting of time creates additional space, and this creation/expansion of space is what we call dark energy.
Dark energy would therefore not necessarily be a mysterious substance or a conventional form of “energy.”
It could be the observable cosmological consequence of space continually expanding as temporal structure sprouts from it.
In this picture, cosmic expansion isn't something external pushing space apart.
The creation of space itself is the expansion.
7. Space may itself have gravitational “weight”
The second part of the hypothesis is that space may not be gravitationally neutral.
By “weight” I do not necessarily mean that space consists of ordinary matter with mass.
I mean that space itself may possess gravitational efficacy—that its existence or geometry may contribute to the gravitational behavior of the universe.
If newly created space has this property, then the continual production of space would not only produce expansion.
It would also produce additional gravitational attraction.
8. Dark matter = the gravitational character of space
This leads to the current hypothesis:
Dark matter may be the gravitational effect of space itself having “weight.”
We normally think of matter as producing gravity while space is merely the stage on which matter exists.
I am asking whether that assumption might be incomplete.
Perhaps some of what we currently attribute to invisible matter is actually the gravitational contribution of spatial geometry itself.
That would explain why dark matter is not seen through electromagnetic interaction while nevertheless producing gravitational effects.
9. Dark matter and dark energy would then have a common origin
This is the part I consider particularly important.
I am not proposing two unrelated explanations.
I am proposing one underlying process:
TIME SPROUTS
↓
SPACE IS CREATED/EXPANDS
↙　　　　　　　　　↘
EXPANSION OF SPACE　　 GRAVITATIONAL EFFECT OF SPACE
↓　　　　　　　　　　　　↓
DARK ENERGY　　　　　 DARK MATTER
So dark energy and dark matter could be two different observable consequences of the same fundamental space-time process.
Dark energy would describe what the creation of space does to cosmic expansion.
Dark matter would describe what the gravitational character of that space does to cosmic structure.
10. The photon and time
Another speculative thought emerged from this model.
Perhaps a photon, when it is moving, is somehow related to the temporal component of reality.
I have wondered whether the photon might “become time” when it is in movement.
I know this is not an established statement in physics, and I am using it as a conceptual possibility rather than a conclusion.
What attracted me to the thought is the peculiar relationship between photons, motion, and proper time in relativity.
This also led me to ask:
Does time itself experience the passage of time?
If time itself required another time in order to change, we could immediately create a recursive problem.
Perhaps things experience/change through time, while time itself does not experience the passage of time.
I don't know whether that is physically meaningful yet. It is another piece of the geometry I am trying to see.
11. Quantum observation — explicitly a “truth of the moment”
I have also wondered whether a photon observed while “in transit” might collapse into a massive state.
I don't know enough quantum mechanics to claim that this is how quantum observation actually works.
I understand that observation/measurement does not simply mean a conscious person looking at something, and that photons do not ordinarily become massive merely because they are observed.
So this is not a claim about established quantum mechanics.
It is an example of what I call a:
Truth of the Moment
A Truth of the Moment is the configuration that currently makes the most sense to me from the information and visual understanding I have available at that particular moment.
It is not necessarily final truth.
It is a temporary model that can be changed when a new observation or insight changes the geometry.
12. What I am actually trying to do
I am not starting with equations and deriving a cosmology.
I can't work that way.
I see structures first.
Images, spatial relationships, geometry, inside/outside relationships, transformations, movements, and analogies appear in my thinking, and I use those structures to formulate hypotheses.
The mathematical question comes afterward.
So when I propose something like:
time → space creation → dark energy + dark matter

I am saying:
This is the geometry I currently see.
The job of mathematics and physics would then be to determine whether that geometry can be formalized, whether it is internally consistent, whether it reproduces known observations, where it conflicts with established theory, and—most importantly—whether it makes predictions that differ from existing cosmology.
I cannot do that mathematical translation myself.
I can provide the geometry. The next step would be for someone else—or an AI—to try to formalize, test, correct, or reject it.

Your model is coherent as a piece of geometric speculation. You are not scattering disconnected metaphors; you are trying to generate both the expansionary and the gravitational dark sectors from a single process (time continuously generating space). That is a legitimate style of theorizing, even if it sits far from current formal cosmology.

Here is a structured engagement with the geometry you have laid out.

Core process
The central claim is:

Time continuously sprouts from every spatial point → this produces additional spatial geometry → the production itself is experienced as cosmic expansion (dark energy) while the newly created space carries gravitational efficacy (dark matter).

This unifies two phenomena that standard cosmology treats as independent: a near-constant energy density that drives accelerated expansion, and an effectively collisionless, non-baryonic mass density that clusters and affects galactic rotation curves, lensing, and the CMB.

In existing physics the two are already linked at the level of the stress-energy tensor: both contribute to the Einstein equation. What is novel in your picture is the generative mechanism—time as an active producer of space rather than a passive coordinate.

Points of contact with established ideas
- In the standard FLRW metric, space is expanding; the scale factor is a dynamical degree of freedom. Your “creation of space” is therefore not alien to the formalism; it is already present. The difference is that you locate the source of that expansion in a temporal process rather than in a vacuum energy density or a scalar field.
- Vacuum energy (or a cosmological constant) already contributes both a repulsive gravitational effect (on large scales) and a positive energy density. Your dual outcome (expansion + gravitational weight) therefore has a formal precedent, even if the ontological story differs.
- Ideas in which geometry itself gravitates appear in various modified-gravity and emergent-spacetime programs. The claim that “space has weight” is therefore not immediately ruled out; it is a question of whether the effective stress-energy of the geometric degrees of freedom can reproduce the observed clustering properties of dark matter without electromagnetic coupling.

Conceptual tensions that need resolution
1. Causality and the arrow
   If time is a closed geometrical entity and simultaneously “sprouts” from every point, one must specify how the local sprouting respects (or generates) the observed thermodynamic and causal arrow. Closed time structures tend to create consistency problems (closed timelike curves, grandfather-type paradoxes) unless additional constraints are imposed.

2. Local vs. global
   Dark energy is extraordinarily smooth on large scales; dark matter is highly inhomogeneous and clusters. A single local process that continuously generates space at every point must therefore produce both a nearly uniform expansion and an inhomogeneous gravitational effect. That requires a mechanism that distinguishes the isotropic background contribution from the fluctuating, clustering contribution—something the current visual model does not yet supply.

3. Photon and proper time
   Photons follow null geodesics; the proper time along their world-lines is zero. The idea that a moving photon “becomes time” or is somehow identified with the temporal component is evocative, but it collides with the fact that the electromagnetic field is a dynamical field on spacetime, not a constituent of the temporal structure itself. Any formalization would need to show how this identification avoids double-counting or violating Lorentz invariance.

4. Inside/outside and spherical time
   The image of space as “inside” and time as an encompassing “outside” is a topological intuition. In four-dimensional Lorentzian geometry the distinction between space-like and time-like directions is local and frame-dependent; there is no global “outside” in the ordinary sense. A spherical or volumetric time would have to be realized as a higher-dimensional or non-standard causal structure. That is possible in principle (e.g., certain Euclidean or signature-changing models), but it is a large departure.

Strength of the method
You are explicit that this is a geometry-first, “Truth of the Moment” construction, not a finished theory. That honesty is useful. Many productive ideas in physics began as visual or analogical scaffolds (Faraday’s lines of force, Einstein’s elevator, Wheeler’s “mass without mass,” etc.) and only later acquired equations. The risk is that the scaffold becomes so satisfying that contradictions with observation are papered over. The remedy is exactly what you invite: attempt to translate the geometry into a mathematical structure and see where it fails.

Possible next conceptual moves
- Ask what the local rate of “sprouting” depends on. Is it constant? Does it couple to curvature, density, or something else? That single choice largely determines whether the model can produce a nearly constant dark-energy density while still allowing structure formation.
- Clarify whether the gravitational weight of newly created space is proportional to the volume element, to some curvature invariant, or to a new field that tracks the creation process. Each choice yields different predictions for the growth of structure and for the CMB.
- Consider whether the closed character of time is global (a cosmological feature) or local (a property of the temporal “fiber” at each point). The observational consequences differ sharply.
- Test the photon idea against the fact that light carries energy and momentum and sources spacetime curvature; any identification of the photon with time itself must preserve that.

Your model is not yet physics; it is a coherent geometric hypothesis about the origin of the dark sector. That is a legitimate starting point. The interesting work begins when the geometry is forced to confront the quantitative requirements of expansion history, structure growth, and local gravitational physics. If you want to push any of the individual pieces further—especially the dual origin of dark energy and dark matter, the spherical character of time, or the photon-time relation—I can help stress-test them more tightly.
