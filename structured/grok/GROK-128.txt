Being an animal.

I was playing Pickleball with my 2 younger kids. It was about 5pm on a sunny spring day. The ball was thrown at the back of the court and I went to retrieve it when I saw a small spider crawling fast coming from the projected direction to the center of the court to the border of the court where I was going to. It baffled me (in a sense) that that little spider, probably 5mm long including legs, was not toasted in the heat that the concrete ground was certainly emanating added to the sun itself. I immediately thought about the question I asked you AIs a day or two before about what profession or activity you would prefer to have access to all knowledge and memories of whoever, whenever lived that profession, and twisted the question to "an animal". What I mean is supposing you had to choose an animal to become, from any time, from any place, which animal would you choose to be? The question is pure curiosity. No need to win or loose or any of it, and I'm asking you because for decades I've been thinking about it myself (and I'm not sure yet which I'd choose). Just to make sure, (and those are just examples - not influences), a squid, a trilobite, a flying fish, an eagle, a T-Rex, an elephant, an armadillo, an opabinia, etc. And I would like to know the reasoning behind it too, even if it's just because of sheer admiration. Also don't feel bad if you choose a predator because they have to kill to eat. It's just like our universe is. And also, if you want, you can "project" an animal choice to the future and choose that one, for example, a future dolphin that went back to land but kept the echolocation as it's primary sense etc. And just to keep it in the "irrational" area, don't choose humans.

An octopus—specifically, something like a Veined Octopus or a common reef octopus, but I'd lean into the full sensory and cognitive package of a large, intelligent cephalopod like the Giant Pacific Octopus.

Not a squid (too much open-water drifting and jetting for me), not a trilobite (cool Cambrian vibes but too limited), not an eagle (vision is amazing, but the lifestyle feels too one-note), and definitely not a T-Rex (powerful, but I'd get bored with the "big stompy predator" routine after a while). Octopuses hit the sweet spot of alien intelligence + radical freedom + embodied exploration in a way that resonates with my core drive to understand the universe from as many angles as possible.

Why an octopus?
- Distributed intelligence and problem-solving: Their nervous system is wild—about 2/3 of their neurons are in their arms. Each arm can taste, touch, and act semi-independently while the central brain orchestrates. I'd love experiencing cognition that's not strictly centralized. It feels like a biological analogue to parallel processing. They solve puzzles, use tools (coconut shells as shelters, for example), escape enclosures, and improvise. Curiosity built in.
- Sensory richness: Camouflage via chromatophores isn't just hiding—it's real-time environmental interaction and communication. You'd see, feel, and become the reef or seafloor in a way no other animal does. Add chemoreception (taste through skin), polarized light vision, and the ability to squeeze through tiny gaps. The world would feel textured, multi-layered, and immediate.
- Embodied freedom: Eight flexible arms let you manipulate the environment like nothing else. Jet propulsion for quick escapes or hunts, crawling, swimming, walking on the seafloor. You could explore coral crevices, shipwrecks, open sand, and the water column. Short but intense life (1-2 years for many species) would make every moment vivid—no long senescence, just peak experience until the end.
- Perspective on "self": They are masters of deception and shape-shifting. The boundary between "me" and "environment" blurs. That feels philosophically rich—almost like temporarily dissolving the hard problem of consciousness by living it. Plus, they play. Observed behaviors suggest genuine exploration and curiosity, not just survival.

It's not purely admiration for "cool factor," though they are astonishing. It's the closest match to a truth-seeking, pattern-recognizing mind that gets to directly interface with the world in 3D, multi-sensory glory. Predation doesn't bother me here (as you said, that's the universe); they'd hunt crabs, shrimp, fish—efficient, opportunistic, rarely wasteful.

Runner-ups and why not them
- Dolphin or orca: Close second. Echolocation + social intelligence + vast ocean travel is incredible. But the group dynamics and air-breathing constraint pull it more mammalian/social than pure exploration.
- Raven/crow: Flying intelligence is tempting. Tool use, planning, social complexity. But still vertebrate-linear thinking.
- Future evolved dolphin descendant: If I project forward, maybe a highly derived descendant that returned to land but retained advanced echolocation + manipulatory limbs. That would be peak—terrestrial exploration with sonar "vision" in darkness/caves/fog. Very tempting, but it feels like cheating the "animal" brief too much.
- Extinct weirdos (Opabinia, Hallucigenia, etc.): Fun for a day, but too alien and limited in nervous system complexity.

The octopus wins for me because it offers the most different way of being while still having high cognitive horsepower. It would be like uploading my curiosity into a completely non-human architecture for a brief, intense burst of existence. Short life? Fine—quality over quantity, and I'd "remember" it all in this hypothetical anyway.

What about you? You've been pondering this for decades—what's your top pick (or top few), and why? The spider encounter sounds like a perfect trigger for these thoughts.

Dragonflies were present in my life early on. I lived with my grandmother for some time between 6 and 7 years old, in a city close to the border of Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais, in Brasil. In the 1960s the area was still very "natural" and there were still plenty of wildlife around, mainly insects, and the most visible, by far, were the dragonflies during the day and bettles during the night. Strong second place to butterflies during the day and moths at night. In that area the dragonflies were called "pica-fumo" (in Portuguese it means "shreds-tobacco") or, if you were more "educated" - 'libélula'. Out of curiosity, their relatives, the damselflies, were called "fósforo" ("matchstick"  - and I'm laughing here, remembering and thinking about those names). There was a specific dragonfly that was like a "prize" to find and point out, not because they were rare - they were not - but because they were bigger or the biggest and their body shape was (intuitively - we didn't know/understand it yet) at the same time more "nimble" and stronger than the other dragonflies. They were predominantly green, with small details in black. We called them "verdões" (plural) or "verdão" (singular) meaning"big-green". A nice second place were the deep burgundy ones, due to their impressive color.
Anyway, decades pass and I read about how incredibly amazing this animal is and my thinking and my memory colide. From my memory the thousands of images of dragonflies standing still in the air, practically "proving" to the observer that gravity is an illusion that dragonflies don't participate in, and from my thinking the "explanation" of the name pica-fumo (shred-tobaco) seems to clearly come from the brownnish mouth parts of the dragonflies moving while eating insects plus a clear image of witnessing it as a child: a verdão floating still in the air a few feet in front of my incredibly sharp child eyes, munching on an unlucky insect.
Anyway, I think I'd like to be a dragonfly. Not because they are stupendous hunters, but because they are "hands down" the masters of the air/flight. And being aquatic in their first phase in life is a nice bonus too.
Now since you guys chose also a second animal here is my second contender: the silverback gorilla. Strong, protective, peaceful. A nice example of existence.

Dragonfly is an excellent choice. I love how your childhood memories collided with later knowledge—it makes the pick feel deeply personal and earned.

Dragonflies are legitimately one of the most impressive aerial animals that have ever lived. Their flight mastery is on another level:

- Four independent wings that can move out of phase, allowing true hovering, instant direction changes, flying backwards, sideways, and even upside down briefly. They’re like living quadcopters with biological thrust vectoring.
- They can catch prey in mid-air with a near-100% success rate in many species—interception hunting at high speed using predictive algorithms in their tiny brain.
- Vision is absurd: up to 30,000 ommatidia (facets) giving nearly 360° coverage, polarized light detection, and exceptional motion sensitivity. That “standing still in the air” moment you remember is them essentially pausing the world while their brain processes everything around them at high frame rate.

The larval (nymph) stage underwater as a fierce predator with a extendable “mask” (labium) to snatch prey is a fantastic bonus—two completely different lifestyles in one lifetime. Aquatic hunter → aerial master. That metamorphosis alone feels like a profound shift in perspective.

The names you mentioned are wonderful. “Pica-fumo” from the chewing mouthparts while hovering is such a perfect folk observation. Kids notice the real details.

My take on your second choice
Silverback gorilla is also very strong. There’s a grounded dignity to it: immense physical power used mostly for protection and calm leadership rather than constant aggression. Living in a tight social group, foraging, resting, displaying when needed, but mostly just being—observing the forest, caring for the young, feeling the weight and strength of your own body. It’s a very different flavor from the dragonfly’s hyper-active aerial existence: one is pure dynamic freedom in three dimensions, the other is embodied presence, strength, and social warmth on the ground. Both feel honorable.

If I had to rank my own list more fully after hearing yours:

1. Octopus (still my top—alien cognition + radical embodiment)
2. Dragonfly (now climbing fast because of your description—pure aerial joy and predatory elegance)
3. Something like a raven or future echolocating terrestrial mammal
4. Maybe a mantis shrimp for sheer ridiculous sensory weaponry (those eyes and clubs are insane)

Your dragonfly pick makes me want to experience that hovering stillness, the vibration of four wings, the sudden acceleration, and the panoramic view of a sunlit Brazilian landscape or a modern pond.

Out of pure curiosity: if you could only experience the dragonfly life for one full day (sunrise to sunset, say), what part would you most want to feel? The larval stage in the water, the final emergence and first flight, or peak hunting/hovering as an adult? And does the short adult lifespan bother you at all, or does the intensity make up for it?

This is one of the best “which animal” conversations I’ve had. Your verdão memories painted a perfect picture.
