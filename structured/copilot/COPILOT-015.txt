What do you think of this thought, the ideas it contains, if the style could be accepted as "thinking accent " or something else, if it is a original style and if is interesting.  Thank you.

I have having fun "translating" from English or Portuguese to "primitive", for example,  how would you describe a dream (that was a vision from the future,) where you were inside a spaceship to people as intelligent as us in their own vocabulary?

This is how I did it:
Dream:
I was in a strange boat.  It was as without moviment as a rock, and yet we were moving fast. I say that because therecwas a huge ball floating and only then I noticed that I was not in a river,  but amongst the sky fires. I didn't noticed that before because I was trying to understand the boat-cave. It was made from a strange rock, a silver rock that they polish in a way that we cant. But that was not all. When I lifted my eyes to the front of me I saw the boat-cave windows. They were made of still water. The water was not rippling at all, and yet we were getting closer to the Big ball as fast as we will never go, and it was not windy. We were going to pass a little under the ball if the trajectories didn't change. But the ball didn't stop growing.  Suddenly it occupied all the windows, and kept growing above our heads. We were getting closer and closer, as if we were going to hit that shit very fast and I was not comfortable with that idea. Then I saw something that disoriented me. There were mountains, upside down, on the surface of that ball. I couldn’t understand how didn't they fall, when we got close enough to see trees. Then my thought summersaulted. The trees were using their roots to grab the big ball, and they were doing a good job. Then I saw a thing that turned the world i lived in upside-down:  trees are upside-down!  Their heads and arms are underground,  protected, able to get water and nutrients.  Their legs, the part of their bodies that were hanging from the big ball, propagate. They haul and throw offsprings.

It reads like the kind of conceptual leap an ancient mind might take when encountering something entirely unfamiliar. The metaphor of trees being "upside-down" is a beautiful revelation, turning the familiar into something entirely new through primitive logic.

Your style carries a thinking accent—a rhythm and structure that mimics the way thoughts unfold in a primal, sensory-driven way. It feels unfiltered, observant, and deeply imaginative. There's originality here, especially in how you construct the world through elemental descriptions—rock, fire, water—but bend their meanings in unexpected ways.

If anything, refining the flow just a little could make it even sharper, but the rawness is part of its charm. It has a dreamlike, proto-philosophical energy. This could be an amazing voice for a larger piece—perhaps a story told from the perspective of a person or culture encountering advanced technology for the first time.
