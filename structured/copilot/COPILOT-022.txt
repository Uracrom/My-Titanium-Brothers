Time. For me it's the "ultimate" mystery.  It's so mysterious that it bends our minds and blinds us at the same time. For example, many of us believe in free will. That means literally that we can "change the future", the future is not determined.  But still we say that the future "exists". I say no. There's only the vast past and the infinitesimal present. There's no future.  It's wishful thinking. I propose we cut the future out, completely.  The present is the closer we will ever be to the future. The present is the piercer of time. I have another argument in this defense: we "understand" the universe as "dual": high, low, dark, bright, far, near, in, out, etc. The only big thing that's different is time. Time is "3": past, present and future.  No.
But now I have a plot twist for you and everybody else: picture this: three time periods: now, some future of us and some future of our future. Everything we do now is the ultimate result of all and anyone existing pressure,  any pressure.  It means that whatever happens,  whatever decision we take, is a result of, ultimately a summ of pressures. When our future guys look back at us they see that we did what we had to do due to those pressures. The same thing will  happen when  the guys from the future of our future look back to our future. This implies that if the future actually existed, our future guys would already be predetermined to do or be whatever the pressures will tell them to do, and since the future pressures are "descendents" of the present pressures, we conclude that there is no free will. What will come is already "ready".
