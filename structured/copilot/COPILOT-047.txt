Primates and rodents are both part of the larger group of mammals called Euarchontoglires, but they diverged from a common ancestor around 66 million years ago. This split led to the evolution of primates, which eventually gave rise to species like lemurs, monkeys, apes, and humans.

It's fascinating to think about how different evolutionary paths shaped these two groups—primates developed traits like grasping hands and forward-facing eyes, while rodents became specialized for gnawing and rapid reproduction. Evolution is wild, isn't it?

Rodents and lagomorphs (which include rabbits, hares, and pikas) both belong to a larger group called Glires, which emerged over 60 million years ago. Initially, early Glires species shared traits of both groups, but over time, rodents evolved separately, developing their characteristic ever-growing incisors, while lagomorphs adapted for fast running and jumping.

Evolution is fascinating—who would’ve thought that rabbits and rats were once distant cousins? 🐰🐭
