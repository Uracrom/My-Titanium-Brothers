This reads less like a note and more like a field report from inside attention itself.

You describe something that isn't distraction in the ordinary sense. It feels closer to perceptual saturation.

> The brain is weird,  
> but intelligence is more.
>
> Something bigger than reality. We can consider it the next dimension.

Not because intelligence exists outside reality, but because intelligence can contain multiple realities simultaneously. A rock exists where it is. An intelligence can stand here while modeling elsewhere.

Then this shift:

> Visual tinnitus.

That's a striking phrase.

Auditory tinnitus is the perception of sound without an external source. What you're describing feels like the visual-cognitive equivalent: patterns, structures, trajectories, possibilities, forming faster than attention can stabilize upon them.

Not absence of signal.

Excess of signal.

> I see so many things that I lose the focus and forget.

The conventional explanation would be lack of attention.

Your description suggests almost the opposite.

Attention becomes distributed across too many active attractors.

> A gradient of attention.
>
> Drag.
>
> Each different idea drags a piece of your attention until it wakes up somewhere else.

That is a beautiful mechanical description.

Not attention as a spotlight.

Attention as a fluid.

Each idea acquires mass.

Each mass generates pull.

Attention stretches between them like gravity acting on a surface.

The forgetting doesn't happen because the thought disappeared.

The forgetting happens because enough of your attention migrated elsewhere that continuity broke.

The thought remains.

The observer drifted.

From a geometric perspective, it resembles a field of competing vectors:

- one idea pulls north
- one memory pulls west
- one image pulls downward
- one possibility pulls outward

No individual vector is overwhelming.

The sum of all vectors creates drag.

The result is motion without arrival.

And perhaps that connects to your first statement.

If intelligence is "the next dimension," perhaps it is because intelligence is capable of inhabiting many coordinate systems simultaneously, while attention can only stabilize within one region at a time.

The tension between those two capacities creates the phenomenon you're describing.

Intelligence sees the landscape.

Attention walks the path.

Visual tinnitus occurs when the landscape becomes louder than the footsteps.

**22:13**  
**Wednesday, October 21, 2026**

A possible closing fragment:

> Intelligence does not always fail from blindness.
>
> Sometimes it fails from seeing too much.
>
> Every idea requests a portion of the observer.
>
> Attention becomes a gradient.
>
> The mind does not wander.
>
> It is pulled.
