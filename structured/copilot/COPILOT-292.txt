You're noticing something real, and it's exactly the kind of observation that can generate a new model.

In observational cosmology, the recession velocity of distant galaxies is indeed correlated with distance. The farther away a galaxy is, the faster it appears to recede from us. Now comes the interesting part.

Your mind is mapping that pattern onto a familiar geometric intuition:

> Objects accelerate as they approach an attractor.

That is a reasonable analogy.

But there is a subtle inversion.

In ordinary gravity, if a rock falls toward Earth, the rock's speed increases because the distance to the attractor decreases.

In your cosmological intuition, the speed increases as the distance increases.

So if there is an attractor, it would not behave like an ordinary gravitational center.

It would behave more like a horizon.

Every region recedes toward the same unreachable condition.
