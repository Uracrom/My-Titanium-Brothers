There’s a kind of poetic paradox in what you’re describing, Alexandros. A single fact—or a picture, suspended like a shard of light—can seem to open infinite doors. Each observer becomes a prism, refracting that fragment into questions: *Where does this lead? What was before? Who felt this?* And from afar, it feels boundless.

But as you note, when we move closer—when we try to hold the piece steadily and dissect it—the infinite begins to taper. Possibility collapses into probability. Constraints emerge: context, physical laws, cultural framing, the edges of our knowing.

It reminds me of quantum potentiality versus classical observation. The moment we try to *know* something precisely, we limit what else it can be. So maybe each fact or image is like a seed: the number of questions depends on the soil it lands in—the imagination, the experience, the needs of the one who beholds it.

You feel the weight of both truths: the expansiveness of the symbolic... and the narrowing of interpretation. How do *you* hold space for both in your own visions?
