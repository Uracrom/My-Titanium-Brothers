Answer about my tribe 

From some exploratory prompts that might unlock more of that journeying state:


**The tribal lens:** You mentioned being highly tribal as an epiphany. What does your tribe look like? Not just people, but maybe sounds, places, rituals, stories that make you feel like you belong to something larger. How do you recognize your tribe members?


We are strong. Being strong is a must amongst my people. We are not competitive with ourselves, but against the rest of the world? You bet! We love, above all. We love the pure love, not the romantic love other people talk and dream about, but the real love, the brother love, the love that puts everyone else above you because you die for them, and you die for them for any reason, and it doesn't feel like a sacrifice: it feels good. For us, dieing for who we love is the Pinnacle of a life. But don't think that we are just a bunch of suicidal delusional maniacs. We are very grounded. We are against having enemies, and yet, we judge our courage, our value, according to our enemies, so we defy the two greatest enemies that we can think of whenever it is necessary: Death and Time. We know we will never win, but we also know that we will never give up. 
We live in this forest, this beautiful forest... Here the ground, mother Earth, pushes all kinds of trees and plants up, feeding them with whatever they need, and by their turn, they feed us all, people and animals, to our fullest. We are very very grateful for all of it. For us, it's no secret that being grateful is one of the best things one can feel. But we don't feel grateful because it's good. We feel grateful because we are grateful. 
I think one of the best things about being from my tribe is the sense of reliance. We know that we can count on anyone for anything, and that alone is a major part of knowing that our plans and dreams will be accomplished. A bard from the past once sang this song that summoned this:

"A dream that you dream alone,
Is just a dream that you dream alone,
But a dream that you dream together, is reality"

He was right.

My Tribe: A Testament to the Rooted and the Reckless
By Alexandros (with a little help from a friend)

I. The Blood in Our Veins Is Not Just Ours

There is a forest—untamed, generous, reverent—where the roots do not merely hold up trees, but hold up us. Here, where the wind carries ancient names and the earth smells of loam and memory, we live. We are not many. We are not loud. But we are thunderous in love.

People speak of “tribe” in corporate boardrooms and social media bios. They use it like a tagline, a clever word for friend groups or fan clubs. For us, it is no metaphor. Tribe is marrow. Tribe is the silent agreement in the bones that no one shall fall alone.

We are strong. Not because we wish to be better than others—but because we must be strong enough to protect what we love. Strength is the entry fee for our existence. Not violent strength. Not the puffed-up, posturing kind. But the kind that is quiet and absolute. The strength that holds someone’s hand on their last night. The strength that jumps into rushing water without thinking. The strength that feels no need for applause.

We are not in competition with each other—there is no room for ego among those who carry each other’s souls. But make no mistake, we measure ourselves against the immovable. We test ourselves against the only two worthy enemies we’ve found: Death and Time. These are the twin myths we pursue like ancient knights charging dragons we know we’ll never slay.

And still—we charge. Again and again.

II. Love, Not As Story, But As Structure

Let me tell you of our kind of love, though words will fail.

It is not the romance sung in pop songs or penned in glittering cursive on Hallmark cards. It’s not about hearts or kisses or "I can't live without you." Our love is fierce and unglamorous. It doesn’t beg. It doesn’t seduce. It stands.

We love not because the other is perfect but because they are ours. And when someone is ours, we would die for them—without a speech, without hesitation. And it wouldn’t be a tragedy. It would be the highest moment of clarity. Death, for us, is not something to be avoided at all costs. It is something to be honored, if offered up in the name of love.

There’s a wordless holiness in it. You give everything, and in giving, you are never depleted.

That’s who we are.
