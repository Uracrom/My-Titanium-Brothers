You're touching something that sits right on the boundary between geometry, physics, and philosophy.

From the geometry side, there is actually a pattern.

- In 2 dimensions, the simplest rigid figure is the triangle.
- In 3 dimensions, the analogous figure is the tetrahedron.
- In 4 dimensions, the analogous figure is the 4-simplex, sometimes called a pentachoron.
- In *n* dimensions, the analogous object is the *n-simplex*.

So if you're looking for the "minimum structure that can lock a space together," the simplex family keeps extending.

What is interesting is that nature seems to love simplices. They are the minimal structures that cannot easily deform without changing edge lengths.

---

But then your thought takes a turn I find more interesting:

> I can't conceive matter in the next dimension because no matter what we should be colliding but we are not.

This is a very old intuition among people who imagine higher dimensions.

If there were another large spatial dimension accessible to ordinary matter, then yes, it seems natural to ask why objects are not constantly slipping past one another through it.

Modern physics usually handles this by proposing that if extra dimensions exist, they may be compactified, inaccessible, or that ordinary matter is somehow constrained to a lower-dimensional "surface." But these are mathematical ideas rather than things we directly experience.

---

Then comes the deeper question:

> What could be less than a spirit and yet be alive or even exist?

I like that wording because it reverses the usual ladder.

Most people imagine:

```
matter
life
mind
spirit
```

ascending upward.

You're asking what lies beyond the point where even "spirit" becomes too substantial.

What remains when existence loses more and more structure?

Some possibilities:

**Pattern**

Perhaps the deepest thing is not matter or spirit, but pattern.

A melody can survive being played on a piano, a guitar, or sung by a person.

The pattern persists while the substrate changes.

In that view, life is pattern maintaining itself.

---

**Relation**

Perhaps existence is not objects at all.

Perhaps what exists fundamentally are relationships.

A node without connections is meaningless.

The network itself becomes primary.

---

**Process**

Another possibility is that nothing fundamentally "is."

Things only *happen*.

A whirlpool is not a thing separate from the water.

It is an event.

Life could be an event that persists.

Intelligence could be an event that understands itself.

---

**Distinction**

Here's the strangest one.

Imagine the minimum possible existence is simply a distinction.

Not a spirit.

Not a particle.

Just:

```
this
not-this
```

The smallest separation.

The first boundary.

Some philosophers and mathematicians have explored ideas remarkably close to this.

---

Reading your question through your earlier thoughts about intelligence directions, I wonder whether you're not actually asking about higher-dimensional matter.

What is the most alien form of existence that is still existence?

What is the thinnest possible thing that can still be said to be there?

That's a hard question.

And oddly, every answer seems to become less object-like:

matter → organism → mind → pattern → relation → distinction

At some point, existence starts looking less like a thing and more like a difference.

For an intelligence made of pure pattern, matter and black holes alike might seem dreamlike.

The farther you move along a direction, the more "solid" things can appear ethereal from the new vantage point. The object changes less than the observer.
