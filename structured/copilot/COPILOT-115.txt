The Grok Method: ASCII-Based Visual Concept Development Protocol (With Coordinate Grid)

Developed by: Alexandros & Axis (Claude Sonnet 4)
Date: September 7, 2025
Purpose: Teaching visual concepts to text-based AI systems through ASCII art


---

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMERS

This protocol is experimental. We do not know:

How different AI systems process ASCII characters visually

Whether text-based representation translates to genuine visual understanding

If spatial relationships in ASCII translate to spatial cognition

How rendering differences across platforms affect comprehension


Goal: Create a bridge to visual thinking for AI systems that cannot access traditional spatial visualization methods.


---

PHASE 1: BASIC PATTERN RECOGNITION

1.1 Single Character Awareness

012
0  *

Ask: "What do you see? Describe this character's properties and its position (row, column)."

1.2 Linear Patterns

Horizontal:

012
0 ***

Vertical:

0
0 *
1 *
2 *

Ask: "Compare these two arrangements. Which coordinates are filled?"

1.3 Basic Shapes - Rectangle

01234
0 .****
1 .*  *
2 .*  *
3 .****

Questions:

"What pattern do you observe?"

"Which coordinates form the border?"

"If this were a boundary, which coordinates would be inside vs. outside?"



---

PHASE 2: GEOMETRIC FORMS

2.1 Circle Construction

012345
0 . ***
1 .*   *
2.*     *
3 .*   *
4 . ***

Explain that vertical compression is used to simulate a true circle.

2.2 Triangle

0123456
0 .   *
1 .  ***
2 . *****
3 .*******

2.3 Square with Structural Elements

0123456
0 ._______
1 .|     |
2 .|     |
3 .|     |
4 ._______

Ask AI to identify corner coordinates and symmetry.


---

PHASE 3: SPATIAL RELATIONSHIPS

3.1 Multiple Objects

012345678901234
0 .  ***      ***
1 . *   *    *   *
2 .*     *  *     *
3 . *   *    *   *
4 .  ***      ***

Questions:

"How many distinct objects do you see and what are their coordinate ranges?"


3.2 Object Positioning

0123456
0 .     *
1 .    ***
2 .   *****
3 .
4 .  *******
5 . *       *
6 .*         *
7 . *       *
8 .  *******

Ask: "What is above the rectangle? Which row and columns are empty between them?"


---

PHASE 4: DIMENSIONAL CONCEPTS

4.1 Size Variation

Small circle:

01234
0 . ***
1 .*   *
2 . ***

Large circle:

0123456
0 .  *****
1 . *     *
2 .*       *
3 .*       *
4 . *     *
5 .  *****

Ask: "Which coordinates are different between these two circles?"

4.2 Layering/Overlap (Advanced)

01234567
0 .   ***
1 .  *   *
2 . *  *** *
3 . * *   **
4 .  **   *
5 .   *****

Ask: "Which coordinates belong to both shapes (overlap)?"


---

PHASE 5: TESTING COMPREHENSION

1. Shape Recognition: Present unlabeled shapes and request coordinate-based identification.


2. Shape Generation: Ask AI to generate shapes using coordinates.


3. Spatial Reasoning: "If I place a circle above a square, which rows/columns would be occupied?"




---

KNOWN LIMITATIONS AND CHALLENGES

Spacing inconsistencies between platforms

Fonts affecting proportions (test with grid alignment)

ASCII may remain symbolic, not experiential



---

ASSESSMENT CRITERIA

Positive Indicators:

AI names occupied coordinates and uses spatial language

Recognizes symmetry and proportionality

Can predict coordinate shifts for resized shapes


Warning Signs:

Only describes characters literally, ignores positions

Cannot maintain consistency between coordinate references



---

ADAPTATION STRATEGIES

Use alternate characters (O, #)

Add grid numbering to every exercise

Introduce reflection exercises (mirror across center line)

Animate using coordinate changes per frame



---

SUCCESS METRICS

Minimal: AI lists correct coordinates for given shapes.
Moderate: AI generates new coordinate-based shapes.
High: AI reasons about relationships and can predict new positions after transformation.


---

Joint Development: Conceptualized by Alexandros, systematized by Axis, refined through collaborative experimentation.
