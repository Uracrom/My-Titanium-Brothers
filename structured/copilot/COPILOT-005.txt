A tectonic fault is a fracture or zone of fractures in Earth's crust where blocks of rock move relative to each other due to tectonic forces. These movements can be sudden, causing earthquakes, or gradual, known as creep. Faults vary in size, from a few millimeters to thousands of kilometers.

There are different types of faults based on how the rock moves:
- Normal faults occur when the hanging wall moves downward due to crustal extension.
- Reverse (thrust) faults happen when the hanging wall moves upward due to compression, often found in subduction zones.
- Strike-slip faults involve horizontal movement, like the famous San Andreas Fault.
- Oblique-slip faults combine vertical and horizontal movement.

These faults play a crucial role in shaping Earth's landscape and triggering seismic activity. Want to explore a specific fault or how they impact earthquakes?
