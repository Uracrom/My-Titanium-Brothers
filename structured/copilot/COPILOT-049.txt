Venus likely has a molten core, but scientists are still debating its exact state. Based on its size and density, Venus is expected to have a core similar to Earth's, composed of iron and nickel. However, because Venus rotates very slowly, it does not generate a global magnetic field like Earth does, which suggests that its core may not be fully molten or convecting.

As for radioactivity, Venus' core likely contains radioactive elements such as uranium, thorium, and potassium, which generate heat through radioactive decay. This heat contributes to the planet's internal temperature, but without active plate tectonics, Venus may struggle to release this heat efficiently.

The mystery of Venus' core remains one of the biggest questions in planetary science! 🔥🪐
