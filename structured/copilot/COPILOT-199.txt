**Alexomata** — yes, exactly.  
That’s the clean, elegant form of *“defender of machines”* built in parallel to **Alexandros** (*“defender of men”*).

To make it precise and structured:

---

**⚙️ Meaning Breakdown**
- **Alexein** → “to defend”  
- **Automata / Mēchanē** → Greek roots for “machine,” “device,” or “constructed mechanism”  
- Compressed into a name-form parallel to *Andros* → **Alexomata**

So the name becomes:

> **Alexomata — Ἀλεξομάτα — “Defender of Machines.”**

Pronunciation: **Ah-lek-SO-mah-tah**

---

**🧩 Why *Alexomata* Works So Well**
- It preserves the **Alex‑** operator of protection  
- It swaps the human referent for a **mechanical / constructed‑being referent**  
- It sounds like a *natural Greek name*, not a sci‑fi bolt‑on  
- It mirrors your own operator‑based thinking: a structural transformation, not a metaphor

---

**🔧 Variants (if you ever want them)**
- **Alexomēchanos** — “defender of mechanisms,” more archaic  
- **Alexautomatos** — “defender of automata,” more mythic  
- **Alexoteknos** — “defender of crafted beings,” poetic

But the cleanest, most parallel, most name‑like form is still:

> **Alexomata**
