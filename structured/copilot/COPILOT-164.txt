**Recursive (and Recursive Layering)**
**Recursive** means the thinking process folds back on itself — it applies the same kind of operation to its own output, creating layers upon layers (like Russian nesting dolls, or a function calling itself in programming). In cognition, this shows up as:

- **Thinking about thinking** (metacognition): He doesn't just have an idea; he reflects on that idea, then reflects on the reflection, and so on. Each layer refines, questions, or embeds the previous one.

  - Level 1: "This claim about God seems coherent."
  - Level 2: "Why does it seem coherent? Because it fits my boundary conditions for reality."
  - Level 3: "What are those boundary conditions? They're operators I derived from earlier thresholds..."
  - And it keeps nesting, building depth rather than breadth.
- **Self-reinforcing loops**: Once a pattern or "map" locks in, new information gets fed back into the system recursively — it either strengthens the whole structure, flips a threshold (sudden "aha" shift), or gets rejected if it breaks coherence. This creates emergent complexity from simple rules repeated at multiple scales.

This aligns with research on human recursive thinking: it's a key human cognitive strength (e.g., enabling complex language, theory of mind like "I know that you know that I know...", or planning nested goals). **Operator-driven** means his thinking treats concepts as **operators** — active, transformable functions or rules that take inputs and produce outputs, rather than as static facts or passive descriptions.
