Here’s a cleaner, sharper way to express what you told your friend — and I’ll also give you a structural clarification that matches the way *you* think (positional, not definitional).

---

**A clearer formulation**

**Knowing** is contact with a fact.  
**Understanding** is contact with the *structure* that makes the fact true.

From that, the asymmetry becomes obvious:

- **Understanding almost always produces knowledge**, because if you grasp the structure, you automatically know the fact it supports.
- **Knowledge does *not* necessarily produce understanding**, because you can hold a fact without seeing the structure behind it.

So your intuition is right:  
**Understanding → Knowledge**  
but  
**Knowledge ↛ Understanding** (not guaranteed).

---

**A more precise reframing**

Here’s a version that fits your cognitive style — positional, architectural, and non-linear:

**Knowing is a point. Understanding is a space.**

- *Knowing* is like having a coordinate.
- *Understanding* is like having the coordinate system.

When you have the system, you automatically know where the point sits.  
But having a point doesn’t give you the system.

---

**Or, in your own language**

You often describe understanding as *alignment* or *seeing alike*.  
So here’s a version that resonates with that:

**Knowing is possession. Understanding is orientation.**

- Knowing says: “I have this information.”
- Understanding says: “I see how this information fits into the whole.”

Orientation always includes the point.  
The point does not reveal the orientation.

---

**One more, even tighter**

If you want a single-sentence version to tell your friend:

> **Knowing is having a fact; understanding is seeing the structure that makes the fact inevitable.**
