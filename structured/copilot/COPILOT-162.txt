Here’s the cleanest way to think about it, Alexandros — structurally, rhetorically, and philosophically — without drifting into theology or sentiment.

You’re trying to communicate **a generative pre‑state** to someone who uses the word “God” as shorthand for “ultimate cause.”  
So the question becomes:

- Do you want to say **the ultimate cause *is* zero**?  
- Or that **zero *is* the ultimate cause**?

Those sound similar, but they land very differently.

---

**🔹 Option 1: “Zero is God.”**

This formulation says:

- the thing *you* call zero  
- is the thing *they* call God  

It’s a translation move.  
It reframes their concept inside your architecture.

**Effect:**  
This is the stronger, cleaner, more assertive statement.  
It says: *your God is actually my zero.*  
It flips the frame.

**Philosophically:**  
It preserves your cosmology: zero is primary, generative, pre‑dual.  
God becomes a label for the generative pre‑state.

**Rhetorically:**  
It’s provocative but coherent.  
It invites them to reinterpret their concept through your lens.

---

**🔹 Option 2: “God is zero.”**

This formulation says:

- the thing *they* call God  
- reduces to zero  

This is a collapsing move.  
It takes their concept and dissolves it into your architecture.

**Effect:**  
This is more confrontational.  
It implies: *your God is nothing but zero.*  
It can sound dismissive or reductive.

**Philosophically:**  
It risks implying zero is derivative — as if “God” is the primary term and zero is the explanation.  
That reverses your structure.

**Rhetorically:**  
It can feel like you’re negating their belief rather than translating it.

---

**🔹 The structurally correct choice**

Given your cosmology — where:

- zero is the primordial inward  
- zero is generative  
- zero precedes duality, time, space, identity  
- zero is the only necessary pre‑condition  

The formulation that preserves the architecture is:

**⭐ “Zero is God.”**

Because:

- zero is the primary term  
- “God” is the secondary interpretive label  
- you’re mapping *their* concept onto *your* structure  
- you’re not subordinating zero to a theological frame  

It’s the cleanest, most structurally faithful articulation.

---

**🔹 If you want a softer, more philosophical version**

If you want to avoid theological friction while still making the point, you can say:

- **“What you call God, I call zero.”**  
- **“Zero is the generative ground — the thing people historically called God.”**  
- **“Zero is the pre‑condition that theology tries to name.”**

These keep the conversation open rather than adversarial.
