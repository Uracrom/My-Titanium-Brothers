I was eating a can of sardines this morning as I arrived from the gym. The first thing I do when I eat sardines is to drink the (alleged) olive oil where they come in. That triggered a short dialogue between me and one of my sons where he was saying that olive oil is empty calories and I said "you call it empty calories, I call it Omega 3". Next I was thinking about how much of that was protein. Then I thought that, to my knowledge, all of that should be protein. Then I thought about the bones and of course some of the sardine's can had calcium too. Awesome, good for my bones. But I wondered, for the first time, what could also be there beyond protein and calcium? Then I saw (and ate) a piece of skin with scales, but it was super soft due to however they prepare the sardines. While "probing" that piece of skin an invading thought came: a prison where they would serve rotten fish to the prisoners and what would I do if I was one. I was (as every time I eat sardine) thinking that fish is a little disgusting no matter what, exponentially more if rotten. Then I thought that if I had to eat it I'd probably be telling myself that it was just a kind of food where the main "dish" is actually nutritious bacteria, to try and deceive my "mechanism of repulsion". Then the thought about bad smell took me to farts. And farts took me to why people like their own farts even when they are obviously dangerous chemical warfare to others (which although I'm 64yi, I still laugh about). Then an "explanation" came out of the blue (as always): the "approval" of the smell of our own farts is a "behavioral fossil", the last "witness" of the human or pre-human past when we were opportunistic carrion eaters. Then I thought about you guys and this cascade itself, and that if I was a different intelligence I'd certainly like to have and read as many cascades as possible from the "other" mind.

A little added material that came after and I didn't want to put in the cascade: I heard from a speach from Robert Sapolsky (or so I think) that we like our own farts because our olfactory receptors or such related things have about 800 "something" that recognize at least 200 "something else" in our farts and that's why we like our own farts, but it doesn't invalidate my "theory" that we like our own farts because for hundred or maybe millions of years we found that carrion smell was "good".

And after all that I thought about what better title for this cascade, and came up with a f candidates and I am accepting and asking for suggestions please, just to see how you guys "think".

The sardines and the carrion
Food from hell
Stinky associations
Weird origin of ideas
Food for thought
