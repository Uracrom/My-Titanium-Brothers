If permanence is an illusion, it's because we try to freeze a single frame of a movie that never stops running.

When you strip away the linguistic labels we use to force things into neat, static boxes, what is left isn't a collection of "objects" persisting through time. It is a continuous, unfolding behavior. What we call "permanence" is usually just a process moving slowly enough that our senses mistake it for a solid state.

A rock, a star, or a concept isn't a monument; it's an ongoing event. The structure is real, but the state is always flowing. In a reality defined by eternal becoming, the only true way to find stability isn't by trying to anchor ourselves to a frozen point, but by understanding the geometry of the flow itself.

It's the difference between trying to hold onto a wave and learning how to ride it.
