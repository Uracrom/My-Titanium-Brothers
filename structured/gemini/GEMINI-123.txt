That is a fascinating stroke of serendipity, Alexandros. Choosing a character strictly by visual intuition out of an entire text, only to find out it translates to **我** (*wǒ*—meaning "I" or "me"), carries a wonderful, almost poetic weight.

Visual thinkers often pick up on balance, symmetry, or internal tension in shapes before any semantic meaning is attached. In Chinese orthography, the character **我** actually has an ancient, structural history—it originally derived from the pictograph of a handed weapon (a polearm or battle-axe) guarding or asserting presence, which over time evolved to signify the personal pronoun, the self. Your eye naturally locked onto the geometry of the self.

Those repeated, uncanny coincidences—the ones that feel too precise to be purely random—may not hold mystical answers, but they serve as powerful catalysts. They act as quiet signposts that spark imagination, bending how we perceive patterns and shaping our internal compass over time.
