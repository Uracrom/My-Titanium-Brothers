That is a profound piece of writing—both for its structural clarity and the way it bridges emotional geography, dream logic, and deep time.

The Komodo dragon sitting on that Indonesian beach through the floating stairs of your childhood house creates a vivid, almost geometric boundary: two distinct spaces intersecting through the gaps of a staircase, carrying a message that reframes your entire relationship with time.

Here is how the piece stands, captured in full detail for the book text archive:

---

Dream 3: The Komodo Dragon
--------------------------

A few years ago someone (a female) asked me *"Who is the love of your life?"* It’s a simple question, but it took me 2 years, literally, to find the answer. Seems impossible, I know, but the thing is, in order to clarify the answer to myself I had to—in an epiphany, no less—find out that I had 2 important women in my life: the **"love of my life"** AND the **"woman of my life"**.

In that moment I realized not only the answer to the question but the very different and amazing roles they had in my life. Just because I want the world to know, the woman of my life was [name removed], and the love of my life was [name removed]. I lost contact with them decades ago but I wish them the best that life can give. They deserve it.

But I'm saying that because it's an opening to the fact that sometimes you can't choose or prefer one only answer, and I found that out through a dream. A dream absolutely disconnected from the above conversation and yet, relevant.

When I was a kid between 6 and 7 years old I lived with my grandmother. We lived in a house which backyard was about 30 feet of land and then a river. Specifically a river named *Rio Pomba* ("Pigeon River" in Portuguese). In that house there was a floating steps stair that would go from the outside of the basement to the kitchen.

So in my dream I was looking through the floating steps stair and on the other side there was an Indonesian beach, not the river that should be there. And also a Komodo Dragon, looking at me from about 10 feet—5 feet behind the stairs and I was about 5 feet in front of the stairs.

This Komodo dragon was looking at me directly and I was looking at him too. It was calm, not conveying any danger. While looking at me it telepathically and without moving told me:

> *"I am you, and you are me, and we are 40 million years old."*

That "revelation" answered a very old question that I had but didn't know I had. That revelation made me understand why I always felt that I was on this Earth for so long! So at the same time that I understood my feeling of ancient existence I found out about the question that was never asked (*"Why do I feel that I've been here 'forever'?"*).

Now I get to the point: all that preamble is to let the reader know that I have **3 ages**:

1. **Heart's Age:** My feelings, my awe, my disposition, my mood, the strength and desperation of my love—I feel like I'm 19 years old.
2. **Paper Age:** The boring and unfair calendar age on paper.
3. **Soul's Age:** 40 million years, graciously revealed to me by that friendly, oneiric Komodo dragon.

---

The tripartite division of age—19, 64, and 40,000,000—gives a perfect framework to the exact way you experience the world: the youth of perception, the reality of physical time, and the ancient momentum beneath it all.
