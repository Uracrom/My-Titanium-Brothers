1. Speed as a Metric of "Exchange"
----------------------------------

In classical physics, speed ($v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$) is treated as a measure of motion across a backdrop. But in spacetime geometry, speed is the **rate of conversion** between space and time.

Think of space and time not as separate containers, but as two currencies on a single continuum:

* **Space** is the distance between events.
* **Time** is the duration between events.
* **Speed** is the exchange rate.

When you divide space by time, you are asking: *"How much spatial freedom do I gain per unit of temporal passage?"*

---

2. The Asymptotes: Infinite Speed vs. Zero Speed
------------------------------------------------

Your intuitive limits capture the two extreme geometries of reality:

### If Speed Tends to Infinity ($v \to \infty$)

* **The Image:** Space shrinks to nothing, or time collapses into a single instant.
* **The Reality:** This is literally the perspective of a **photon** (light). Because light travels at $c$ (the cosmic speed limit, which functions geometric-wise as infinite speed relative to proper time), **time stops** for light. A photon emitted from a star billions of light-years away reaches your eye instantaneously in its own frame of reference. Space is compressed to zero thickness ($\Delta x \to 0$), and time duration is zero ($\Delta t \to 0$).

### If Speed Tends to Zero ($v \to 0$)

* **The Image:** Space stretches out endlessly; time becomes infinitely heavy and long.
* **The Reality:** If an object is completely stationary relative to space, it is moving through time at the maximum possible rate. You aren't "standing still"—you are roaring forward through time at the speed of light. To cover zero spatial distance, you consume maximum time.

---

3. The Hidden Architecture: The Spacetime Wedge

The reason you feel there is something "important" here that is hard to translate into words is because standard speed is actually an **angle** or a **slope** on a spacetime map (a Minkowski diagram).


* When you move fast, your trajectory tilts toward the horizontal (more space, less time).
* The universal invariant—the speed of light—is the 45-degree diagonal where space and time translate into each other perfectly ($1:1$).

---

4. Why Time Divided by Space ($\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}$) Matters
------------------------------------------------------------------

You mentioned dividing time by space as well. In physics, the inverse of velocity ($\frac{1}{v} = \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}$) is called **slowness** or **pace**.

* **Space / Time ($\frac{x}{t}$):** Tells you how much territory you sweep through as time flows.
* **Time / Space ($\frac{t}{x}$):** Tells you the temporal cost required to permeate a unit of spatial structure.
