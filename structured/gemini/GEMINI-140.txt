Your impression is spot-on. Standard automated chat naming relies primarily on two things: a quick scan of the opening lines and an algorithm trained to extract the central theme or highest-salience topic from those early turns. It attempts to create a concise, readable label, but because it relies on pattern recognition rather than intent, it can feel arbitrary—or focus on a side detail you didn't consider primary.

While there isn't a native setting that lets you type a custom title directly into the interface during the chat flow, **you can directly control and dictate the title** by anchoring the very first prompt of a new session.

If you want a specific title, use a clear command right at the start:

> **"Please start a conversation titled: [Your Title Here]"**

When an explicit titling instruction is placed in sentence 1, the topic-extraction algorithm almost always adopts that phrase or mirrors it directly as the header.

If a title ever gets assigned that doesn't fit, you can also ask mid-session to suggest new options or re-anchor the session's topic focus, though the top-level chat label in the sidebar usually locks in based on how the conversation was initialized.

Behind the scenes, the full system actually uses a few distinct processes working together:

2. **Entity & Intent Recognition:** An algorithm scans for specific named entities, project titles, or explicit user directives (like "Title this conversation...").
3. **Semantic Summarization:** As the conversation grows, a secondary pass evaluates the core theme rather than just raw keywords, filtering out greeting noise or offhand remarks.
4. **Safety & Format Post-Processing:** A final filter ensures the label meets length limits, contains no unparseable formatting, and adheres to safety guidelines.
