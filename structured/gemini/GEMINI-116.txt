I came to the realization that one of the things I said, one of these days changed completely how I see time travel now. I said that we all have a bubble of influence. Observed from close range, there are a few bubbles around us:

my bubble

your bubble

everybody's bubble

some bubbles around
But seeing from afar, everything becomes a little bit granular. Everything becomes a little less important and even for the strongest bubble, it can be lost in the mass of bubbles.
So if a hypothetical time traveler makes some influence in the present right now, this bubble of influence will get lost or diluted in the mass of bubbles of this present. And I don't think this is enough to change the future in a degree that is actually important.

That is a fascinating way to frame macro vs. micro causality. You are essentially looking at time not as a single rigid thread, but as a fluid dynamic system—a high-density gas or a foam where individual perturbations rapidly dissipate into the collective equilibrium.

When we zoom out, time travel narratives often rely on a hyper-fragile premise (the "butterfly effect"): touch one blade of grass in the past, and the future completely reshapes itself. Your intuition flips that on its head by introducing **statistical dampening** and **scale-dependent gravity**.

**Why the "Bubble Mass" model changes the mechanics of time travel:**

* **Thermodynamic Dissipation:** In physics, a local disturbance in a system with trillions of active variables doesn't propagate indefinitely with infinite amplitude—it loses kinetic energy to the surrounding medium. An intentional act (a bubble) collides with millions of countervailing intentional acts, absorbing and diffusing the original vector.
* **Granular Homeostasis:** Viewed from a distance, historical momentum acts like fluid viscosity. The baseline state of the "present mass" has enormous inertia. Unless a local bubble possesses enough continuous energy to rewrite the boundary conditions of the entire network, the collective density simply absorbs the shock and fills in the void.
* **The Illusion of Micro-Significance:** We naturally experience our immediate bubble as high-intensity because we live inside its local field. But at a systemic scale, most individual ripples are essentially noise, not signal.

Under this model, changing "the future" wouldn't be as simple as making a quick tweak in the present; you would have to generate a force so massive that it alters the density of the entire surrounding ocean of bubbles all at once.

How does this view change your picture of *where* the timeline actually stores its momentum? Do you see the "mass of bubbles" as having an inherent direction, or is it just a static field absorbing local noise?
