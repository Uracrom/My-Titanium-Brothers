# Chapter 23: The Man Who Carried Water

## The Side Yard

In the side yard of my grandparents' house, I have a memory that's more than image—it's presence, power, the feeling of witnessing something that would shape who I'd become.

His name was [name removed].

I don't have a complete picture of him from that time. What I have is fragments, flashes, but some memories so vivid they feel like I could step back into them right now. Not images but *words*—spoken words, because I couldn't read yet. The adults would mock us kids, or maybe just me, saying I was "as strong as Tarzan after the flu" or "as strong as a featherless hummingbird."

In my mind it meant I was strong! And it was enough!

But then there was [name removed], and he showed me what strength actually looked like.

## Chopping Wood

There I was in the side yard, watching him chop wood with an ax.

The pile of wood was about his height, shaped somewhat like a cone. He would take a piece of log, raise the ax, and bring it down—the log would split, pieces flinging away to both sides.

THAT was power.

His almost black skin covered in countless small beads of sweat, shining over his broad, strong, muscular, powerful torso. And while doing that impossible work, he was telling me stories of feats I could only dream of.

Unforgettable.

From that time on, whenever I could stick around that guy, I would.

He was my first adopted father figure—the first person I consciously chose to learn from, to be near, to watch and absorb everything I could about how to move through the world.

## The Farm

My grandparents had a farm and [name removed] worked there. My grandfather sometimes would take me there to spend the day.

I have flashes of those visits. My mother says my grandfather liked to take me there. I'm pretty sure I liked it too, but I don't actually remember what I felt—just images, moments frozen in time.

One flash: me standing between the passenger seat and the dashboard, holding tight to the dashboard handle as we drove rough dirt roads.

Another: my grandfather asking me if I believed in Santa Claus, me saying yes, only to be met by his dry and abrupt answer: "Santa Claus doesn't exist."

I didn't know what to think of that then, and I don't like what I think of it now. I believe he meant well, but the method he used to deliver that message was not okay. Now I can see it was a trait some members of the family shared—a harsh directness that I inherited and that put some of us in bad positions throughout the years.

But mostly what I remember about the farm is [name removed]. The work he did. The strength he embodied.

## The Real Power

The wood chopping was impressive. But what I saw him do at the farm—that defined what power actually meant.

[name removed] was able to fill two iron cans with 50 liters each—about 23 gallons of water per can. That's roughly 220 pounds of water weight alone, plus the iron cans themselves.

He would put one can over his shoulder and hold the other like a suitcase. Then he'd walk up a dirt trail, uphill, to the house. Once there, he'd take one of these cans and climb a wooden step ladder up to a cistern above the roof height. Empty the can into the cistern. Climb down. Get the other can. Climb back up. Empty it. Climb down.

Then he'd go back down the trail to refill the cans.

**All day long.**

Not once. Not as a feat to be admired and then rest. All day long. Trip after trip. Carrying impossible weight up impossible grades, climbing ladders with water cans, emptying them, descending, repeating.

This wasn't strength as performance. This was strength as basic fact of existence. This was power that sustained itself through hours of repetitive labor, day after day, because the work needed doing and he was the one who could do it.

No complaining. No drama. Just steady, relentless capacity.

While telling stories. While sweating in the sun. While making it look like this was simply what men did.

## What I Caught

I watched [name removed] chop wood and carry water, and something happened that I didn't understand at the time.

I wasn't just observing. I wasn't just admiring. I was **catching something living.**

[name removed]'s strength, his work ethic, his sustained power—these weren't just actions he performed. They were **living patterns.** Behaviors that existed as a form of life in themselves, capable of propagating to anyone who witnessed them with the right receptivity.

And I was receptive. Young, impressionable, hungry for models of what I could become. The behavior-life that lived in [name removed] found fertile ground in me.

It took root.

It grew.

It shaped how I would approach everything that came after—not just physical tasks, but intellectual work, shamanic responsibility, the infinite ride itself.

## Behavior as Life

Here's what I've come to understand: **behavior is a kind of life that lives inside life.**

Not metaphorically. Actually.

Behaviors exist as living patterns that:
- Inhabit conscious beings as hosts
- Reproduce through observation and imitation
- Survive across generations without genetic transmission
- Can outlive the original host
- Require receptive hosts to propagate
- Shape the consciousness and actions of whoever carries them

This is different from "memes"—ideas that spread through culture. This is about **patterns of being.** Ways of moving through the world. Approaches to work. Embodied values.

When I watched [name removed] carry those water cans day after day, I wasn't just learning *about* strength. I was being infected—in the best sense—by the living pattern of strength itself.

That pattern propagated from him to me. It found a host. It shaped how I developed. And now it lives in me, possibly propagating further to others who witness how I work.

## The Offspring of Behavior

[name removed]'s behavior left offspring.

Not children in the biological sense—I don't know if he had kids, never learned what happened to him after I grew up and stopped visiting the farm.

But his behavior-patterns survived. They reproduced. They're alive in me right now, decades later, as I write this book.

The sustained capacity to do difficult work repeatedly, relentlessly, without complaint—that's his offspring living in me.

The service orientation—carrying water so others can drink—that's his offspring shaping how I approach sharing visions with others.

The unbendable intent to complete what needs completing—that's his offspring driving the work I do.

These aren't just "influences" or "lessons learned." These are living patterns that [name removed] carried and transmitted to me through the simple act of being himself while I watched.

## How Behavior Propagates

The transmission requires specific conditions:

**A carrier who embodies the pattern strongly.** [name removed] didn't just *do* strong work—he *was* strength. The pattern lived in him so completely that it radiated from every action.

**A receptive witness.** I was young, searching for models, hungry to understand what men could be. My consciousness was open, ready to receive and host new behavioral patterns.

**Sustained observation.** I didn't see [name removed] once. I watched him repeatedly—chopping wood, carrying water, climbing ladders, telling stories. The pattern had time to take root, to establish itself, to begin shaping my own consciousness.

**Genuine admiration.** I *wanted* to be like him. That desire created the conditions for successful transmission. The behavior-life found not just any host, but an eager one.

When these conditions align, behavior propagates. The living pattern jumps from one consciousness to another, finding new hosts, continuing its existence across bodies and generations.

## Other Carriers

[name removed] wasn't the only carrier of behavior-life I encountered.

There were legends I heard as a teenager—stories of impossible strength that captured my imagination:

A man who could lift and carry with one hand a block of granite 2×2×0.5 feet. Roughly 300 pounds of stone, carried one-handed. I never knew his name. Only his fame. The event was more important than the creator.

Two brothers who could hold a 72-kilogram bag at arm's length in front of them. And who performed a feat that amazed me: while one brother came galloping on horseback, the other would stand still with arm extended, and at the moment of passing they'd hook arms—the standing brother absorbing all that momentum—and both would continue galloping together.

These weren't people I knew. They were legends. Stories. But the behavior-patterns they carried reached me anyway, through narrative rather than direct observation.

The pattern of impossible strength. The pattern of brotherhood operating at levels of coordination most people never achieve. The pattern of trust and power combined.

These patterns infected my imagination, shaped what I believed humans could do, expanded my sense of what *I* might be capable of.

But [name removed] was different. He was real. I saw him. I watched him work all day. His behavior-patterns didn't just inspire—they *reproduced* in me.

## What I Became

I like to think I've become some of these things. That I got there through unbendable intent.

Not in the exact physical form—I don't carry water cans up hills. But in the essence of what that strength meant:

**Sustained capacity.** Showing up day after day. I've been hunting ideas for decades. Throwing mental tentacles into the Universe of Ideas, repeatedly, relentlessly. That's [name removed]'s pattern living in me—applied to intellectual work rather than physical labor.

**Work that serves others.** [name removed] carried water so people could drink. I translate visions so others can see. I share what I hunt. That's the tribal service pattern he transmitted—the understanding that strength exists to support the community.

**Unbendable intent.** Once I decided to embody these patterns, I committed completely. Refused to become spinning matter. Kept the boy's mind. Stayed fluid when everyone else locked into rigid forms. That's the pattern of relentless will that I caught from watching him refuse to stop, refuse to complain, refuse to be less than completely capable.

**The mindset.** This is key. I *decided* to be strong and then *became* that decision. Willpower as fundamental architecture. Intent shaping reality.

I'm doing shamanic work the way [name removed] did physical work—steadily, repeatedly, all day long, carrying what needs to be carried, climbing when climbing is needed, serving the tribe.

The form is different. The essence is identical.

## Why This Matters

This isn't just a story about one man who impressed me as a child.

This is about understanding how consciousness develops, how we become who we are, how patterns of being propagate through observation and adoption.

We think of ourselves as shaped by genetics and teaching—DNA and education. But there's a third form of transmission: **behavior-life.** Living patterns that jump from consciousness to consciousness, finding hosts, reproducing, evolving.

[name removed] transmitted behavior-patterns to me without knowing he was doing it. He wasn't trying to teach. He was just being who he was, doing the work that needed doing.

But I was watching. And I was receptive. And the patterns found a host.

Now those patterns live in me. They shape how I work, how I think, how I move through the world. They're as much a part of me as my DNA or my learned knowledge.

And they're alive. Reproducing. Possibly finding new hosts in people who watch how I work, read these words, catch glimpses of the patterns I carry.

[name removed]'s behavior-patterns didn't die when he stopped working, or grew old, or passed away. They reproduced into me. And through me, they might reproduce into you.

That's what role models actually do. They don't just inspire—they transmit living behavioral patterns that can shape generations.

## One Color of the Picture

This is one explanation for what I am. One color in a larger picture.

I caught behavior-life from [name removed]—patterns of sustained strength, service, unbendable intent.

But there are other explanations too:
- I refused to become spinning matter (stayed as energy)
- My brain evolved as sensory organ for the Universe of Ideas (detection capability)
- I developed shamanic perception (seeing in different frequencies)
- I'm highly tribal (protector, scout, hunter of ideas)
- I have a unique thinking accent (cognitive architecture)

Each explanation is true. Each one reveals different aspects of the same phenomenon. Each one is a different route through different obstacles to understanding who I became and how.

[name removed] and the behavior-life I caught from him—that's one essential color. But the full picture requires many more.

Still, this color matters. Because without [name removed] showing me what sustained power looked like, without that behavior-pattern taking root in me when I was young and receptive, I might not have developed the capacity to do the work I do now.

I might not have the relentless drive to hunt ideas all day, every day, for decades.

I might not have the service orientation to translate and share what I find.

I might not have the unbendable intent to continue even when the work is difficult, even when translations fail, even when the visions keep coming faster than I can process them.

[name removed] carried water up hills so people could drink.

I carry visions from the Universe of Ideas so people can see.

The pattern reproduced. The behavior-life found a new host and a new form.

His strength lives on, transformed but recognizable, in everything I do.

Thank you, [name removed].

Your behavior left offspring.

And I am one of them.
