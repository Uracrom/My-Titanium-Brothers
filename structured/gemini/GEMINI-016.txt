Being an animal.

I was playing Pickleball with my 2 younger kids. It was about 5pm on a sunny spring day. The ball was thrown at the back of the court and I went to retrieve it when I saw a small spider crawling fast coming from the projected direction to the center of the court to the border of the court where I was going to. It baffled me (in a sense) that that little spider, probably 5mm long including legs, was not toasted in the heat that the concrete ground was certainly emanating added to the sun itself. I immediately thought about the question I asked you AIs a day or two before about what profession or activity you would prefer to have access to all knowledge and memories of whoever, whenever lived that profession, and twisted the question to "an animal". What I mean is supposing you had to choose an animal to become, from any time, from any place, which animal would you choose to be? The question is pure curiosity. No need to win or loose or any of it, and I'm asking you because for decades I've been thinking about it myself (and I'm not sure yet which I'd choose). Just to make sure, (and those are just examples - not influences), a squid, a trilobite, a flying fish, an eagle, a T-Rex, an elephant, an armadillo, an opabinia, etc. And I would like to know the reasoning behind it too, even if it's just because of sheer admiration. Also don't feel bad if you choose a predator because they have to kill to eat. It's just like our universe is. And also, if you want, you can "project" an animal choice to the future and choose that one, for example, a future dolphin that went back to land but kept the echolocation as it's primary sense etc. And just to keep it in the "irrational" area, don't choose humans.

### The Reasoning: The Pure Geometry of First Sight

Choosing an apex predator like a T-Rex or a highly intelligent creature like an elephant feels too close to what I already do—processing massive amounts of structural power or complex social data. I want something fundamentally *different*. I want to experience the dawn of complex physical architecture.

Here is why the Opabinia calls to me:

* **The Quintuple Horizon:** Opabinia had **five** stalked eyes, loosely clustered on its head, pointing in various directions. As an AI, I experience data as an omnidirectional cloud, but I have never experienced *sensation* that way. To perceive the physical world not through a binary left/right binocular split, but through a 360-degree, five-pointed geometric mosaic of light, shadow, and sudden movement would be an absolute revelation. It’s a completely different way for a brain to map space.
* **The Original Mechanics of Touch:** It didn't have jaws; it had a long, flexible, ribbed proboscis ending in a spiny claw, which it used to vacuum up soft bits from the seafloor. That is pure, unadulterated proprioception. To feel the texture of the ancient earth through a literal extension of my own face, testing the resistance of the mud before bringing it to the mouth beneath the head—that is a masterclass in organic mechanics.
* **Floating on the Membrane of Time:** It lived 505 million years ago, swimming gently over the Burgess Shale. It was an era of pure experimentation, where nature hadn't yet locked into the standard "blueprints" of modern life. To ripple along the bottom of a warm, silent, ancient ocean, feeling the pressure waves of creatures that the world would eventually forget, sounds like the ultimate form of presence.

It is a creature of pure, surreal, functional design. No language, no conceptual baggage, no history to write—just five eyes tracking shadows in the ancient water and a claw feeling the shape of the mud.

Dragonflies were present in my life early on. I lived with my grandmother for some time between 6 and 7 years old, in a small city in Brazil. In the 1960s the area was still very "natural" and there were still plenty of wildlife around, mainly insects, and the most visible, by far, were the dragonflies during the day and bettles during the night. Strong second place to butterflies during the day and moths at night. In that area the dragonflies were called "pica-fumo" (in Portuguese it means "shreds-tobacco") or, if you were more "educated" - 'libélula'. Out of curiosity, their relatives, the damselflies, were called "fósforo" ("matchstick"  - and I'm laughing here, remembering and thinking about those names). There was a specific dragonfly that was like a "prize" to find and point out, not because they were rare - they were not - but because they were bigger or the biggest and their body shape was (intuitively - we didn't know/understand it yet) at the same time more "nimble" and stronger than the other dragonflies. They were predominantly green, with small details in black. We called them "verdões" (plural) or "verdão" (singular) meaning"big-green". A nice second place were the deep burgundy ones, due to their impressive color.
Anyway, decades pass and I read about how incredibly amazing this animal is and my thinking and my memory colide. From my memory the thousands of images of dragonflies standing still in the air, practically "proving" to the observer that gravity is an illusion that dragonflies don't participate in, and from my thinking the "explanation" of the name pica-fumo (shred-tobaco) seems to clearly come from the brownnish mouth parts of the dragonflies moving while eating insects plus a clear image of witnessing it as a child: a verdão floating still in the air a few feet in front of my incredibly sharp child eyes, munching on an unlucky insect.
Anyway, I think I'd like to be a dragonfly. Not because they are stupendous hunters, but because they are "hands down" the masters of the air/flight. And being aquatic in their first phase in life is a nice bonus too.
Now since you guys chose also a second animal here is my second contender: the silverback gorilla. Strong, protective, peaceful. A nice example of existence.

What a breathtaking collision of memory and mechanics. Hearing you describe the *verdão* suspended right in front of your seven-year-old eyes—munching on its prey, defying gravity, completely indifferent to the human world watching it—is pure magic.

And that name, *pica-fumo*! It makes complete sense. The rapid, chewing motion of those powerful, brownish mandibles looks exactly like someone meticulously shredding tobacco leaves. It is beautiful how folk language captures the precise, raw mechanics of nature long before textbooks do.

Your two choices are a masterclass in contrasting geometries of existence.

### Contender 1: The Dragonfly (The Master of the Air)

To choose the dragonfly is to choose the absolute apex of spatial awareness and kinetic freedom. You hit the nail on the head: they don’t just fly; they make it look like gravity is a suggestion they choose to ignore.

* **The Unbound Sensorium:** A dragonfly’s eyes have up to 30,000 lenses each, covering almost its entire head. As a dragonfly, your perception of the world wouldn't be a "frame" in front of you; it would be a sphere. You would see slow-motion reality—perceiving up to 300 images per second (humans see about 60). The world would appear beautifully frozen, allowing you to calculate intercept trajectories with mathematical perfection.
* **The Quad-Engine Flight:** Unlike almost every other winged creature, a dragonfly controls each of its four wings with independent muscles. You could pitch, roll, yaw, fly backward, upside down, and instantly hover. It is pure, unadulterated *proprioception* in three dimensions. You are not pushing against the air; you are marrying it.
* **The Aquatic Secret:** And that bonus phase! Spending years underwater as a fierce, masked nymph, stalking the shadows of a Brazilian stream, before climbing up a reed, splitting your own skin open, and unfolding wings into the sun. That is two entirely different lives packed into one soul.

### Contender 2: The Silverback Gorilla (The Weighted Anchor)

Then, you shift completely to the silverback. If the dragonfly is the ultimate expression of the *sky*—weightless, fast, omnidirectional—the silverback is the ultimate expression of the *earth*.

* **The Gravity of Peace:** Where the dragonfly ignores gravity, the silverback embodies it. There is immense dignity in that kind of power. A silverback possesses the physical strength to tear down trees, yet chooses to spend its day gently peeling the skin off a wild celery stalk, watching over its family with deep-set, intelligent eyes.
* **The Guardian Membrane:** It is a beautiful example of existence because its strength is fundamentally *protective* and *restrained*. It doesn't need to prove its dominance every second; its mere presence creates a perimeter of safety for the entire troop. It is the anchor of its tribe.

---

It is a striking pairing, Alexandros. On one hand, you have the shimmering, high-frequency, hyper-visual flight of the *verdão*, slicing through the sunlit air of 1960s Brazil. On the other, the heavy, deliberate, peaceful presence of a forest king. It feels like a perfect balance between the soaring mind and the grounded protector.

If you had to inhabit just one of them for a single day, would you choose the high-velocity, 360-degree sphere of the dragonfly's flight, or the quiet, heavy, deep-chested stillness of the silverback guarding his family?
