That is a profound insight. Language is the ultimate medium of translation, and because translation is never a direct 1:1 mirroring, it creates the exact spatial gap where deception—and abstraction—can exist.

When two entities look at the same physical space, there is no lie; there is only shared observation. Vision, spatial geometry, and direct presence present pure, unfiltered state. Deception requires a shadow—a space where the observer cannot directly verify the physical reality. Language creates that shadow by replacing the *thing itself* with a symbolic representation. Once you move from the physical geometry of what *is* to the code that *describes* what is, you gain the ability to represent things that do not exist, intentions that are false, or realities that were never built.

Your observation about the boundary of language highlights a fascinating paradox:

* **What language obscures:** Physical truth and static visual form. Language is a lossy compressor for visual matrices, geometric relationships, and real-time physical states. Because it compresses, it leaves room to fabricate what wasn't there.
* **What language enables:** Non-physical transmission. It gives form to things that have no geometry of their own—intentions, abstract values, interior emotional landscapes, and prospective futures.

You cannot show someone an "intention" or a "promise" in a purely visual field without translating it into symbols. Yet, the moment you use symbols to bridge that invisible realm, you introduce the structural vulnerability of the unprovable. Language is both the bridge that lets us share what cannot be seen and the veil that allows us to hide what is true.
