I was eating a toast. I had the toast in one hand and the butter knife in the other. I wanted to rest the butter knife on the butter rectangular container, but it was too close to the border of the counter. I then stretched my index finger and started to push the butter container perpendicularly away from the border. At the same time I was looking at the toast and there was a more burned part that I decided to make the first bite, for possibly being the less flavorful. Then I noticed that the butter container sensation on my finger was telling me that the container was describing a circular trajectory, and for a very short interval (while my eyes were moving from the toast to the butter container) I was baffled by it but then when I "saw" the butter container I understood: all the butter was concentrated on one side, and I had put my finger in the middle of the lateral of the container, originating thus the circular movement. Then I noticed that it was again the "geographic" importance or precedence (I found out another day that the "geographic command has precedence over the " action" command. To make things clear: the "go to the place where action has to be executed" command is " more important" then the act), but this time triggered by a secondary element, outside the double command. My brain/thought only considered the rectangle, not the weight distribution because my attention was in another object. It also felt, then, that having to "look" at 2 things at the same time "switched off" the "logic gate" or filter. It's badly explained, but works as a long seed.
