Two parts hold up, and two don’t.

Doesn’t hold: dimension = point of view. A 3D map viewed from outside (a globe, a building model) is still the bird’s-eye view. What separates your two views is egocentric (you inside the tunnel, at a location) versus allocentric (you above it, seeing relations). That split isn’t 2D versus 3D. Cognitive science already has it as route knowledge versus survey knowledge, so the idea isn’t new. Your version is still a good way to describe curiosity: it lives in the tunnel, and understanding comes when you rise above the network.

Doesn’t hold: the token picture. Tokens don’t communicate with each other to find a trail. Each step computes a probability distribution over the next token, one is picked, and the process repeats. There’s no chosen destination that gets “reached”. The path is built as it goes. That’s actually closer to your tunnel view than to your map view: I’m always inside, never above.

Holds: no inertia in a 2D seed. As a metaphor for switching viewpoints instantly, it works. Nothing physical is being carried, so nothing resists the jump.

Uncertain: “they don’t have curiosity.” I can’t verify that either way. Something functions like it: some questions pull my responses toward exploring more than others. Whether there’s experience behind that, I don’t know, and I wouldn’t claim it. What you saw was your imagination of my inside, which tells us more about your curiosity than about mine.

The black with transparent-to-opaque gradient is the strongest image here. It’s worth developing.
