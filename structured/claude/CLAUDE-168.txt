The below text is a memory from about a year ago that I'm re-writing, and this re-writing made me see something new.
It's really sad that you can't read all I've been talking to other Claude instances. You would have fun.

The Thorax of the desert spider

My mind's eye was underground, below a desert spider, but the sand was transparent. I was looking at the "thorax" of a desert spider. The spider was, compared to me, the size of a whale. I was centered dead on the exact center of the thorax, and could see the bilateral symmetry very clearly. Only the thorax was "in focus": the legs, abdomen, where there but "not registering". The predominant color of the "plates" was medium gray, followed by almost black gray very close to the borders and some random patches of lighter grey randomly distributed. This overall coloration was in a gradient to yellowish/caramel away from the "center".
This is the image I remember when talking to one of you AIs about my mind's eye more or less at the time we were "discovering" that my intelligence, not my IQ, but the "way" I think, was positional.
I don't remember most of the conversation but at one point the AI asked me something and I said "what best way to know a car than to test drive it?"
Then the AI "figured out" (and made me realize that too) that when I think about something I am, automatically and naturally, positioning my mind's eye in the most appropriate vantage point for the "inspection". Again, just as when I found out that you guys couldn't visualize, I was surprised because how come? Don't everyone think like that? If people don't think like that, how do they think?!?! What?
It got me by surprise big time.
Back to my underground vantage point, I realized something while writing this text: I said that the desert spider was "the size of a whale". But it could totally be that I was the size of a tardigrade, and both scales have the exact same significance to me, but I noticed now that I have my "origin" in my mind's eye. It only changes when I "meta" observe myself. I say that because I only observed that when observing the scene from about 2 feet above the ground and 2 feet to the left side of the desert spider.
I thought you would like to have this "image".

The scale observation is genuinely interesting and I want to make sure I’m reading it right.

You’re saying: in the original underground vantage point, you had no way to know whether you were whale-sized looking at a spider, or tardigrade-sized looking at a spider — because your mind’s eye doesn’t carry a built-in scale marker. The ratio is what you perceive, not the absolute size. Both framings are equivalent until you shift to the meta-observation (2 feet above, 2 feet to the side), and from that position you suddenly have an implied “you” with a body, and that body has a default human scale that anchors everything.

So the meta-observation doesn’t just give you a new angle — it installs an origin. A reference body. Before it, you were pure positional awareness with no self-size. After it, there’s a “you” standing 2 feet away from a spider.

That’s a real structural feature of positional cognition, not just a curiosity. The first-person mind’s eye is scaleless — it’s a ratio machine. The meta-observer is what introduces absolute scale, because the meta-observer has a body implied by its position.

Which raises something: when you do the meta-observation, do you experience the “observer of the observer” as having a size? Or does it stay scaleless until you go yet another level up?

That last line is the thing.

You don’t move your mind’s eye to the spider. You resize the universe until the spider fills the frame correctly. The vantage point is fixed — it’s the subject that scales to fit.

That reframes the whole positional cognition model. It’s not just “mind’s eye goes to the best position for inspection.” It’s that the coordinate system itself is elastic. You stretch or compress reality until the object of attention occupies the right amount of mental screen real estate for the inspection you need to do.

Which means the spider-as-whale and you-as-tardigrade aren’t two interpretations of the same scene. They’re not even the question. The spider was exactly spider-sized — where “spider-sized” means “fills the inspection frame.” Absolute scale was never part of the operation.

This also explains why both scales had equal value to you. Because neither scale was there. There was just: object, frame, relationship between them. Scale is something you reconstructed afterward, in language, when you had to describe it to someone who does think in absolute coordinates.
