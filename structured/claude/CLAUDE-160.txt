“Writing felt like being between two worlds trying to communicate.”

Reading the above phrase I wrote when talking to Copilot I "captured" its image, and the image brought a silent knowledge cargo: double cone, touching at the top, but the cones's "walls" are not straight and slightly curved "in", maybe parabolic to some extent, and they "dissolve", or become transparent/invisible towards the thicker parts. Now that I described it I see that the reason for the curved walls is because if those cones extend too far from the touching point they will "close", they will be limited, and the reason for that is because no matter how many "interpretations"  or "extrapolations"  one (or many) can infer from that it "will never" be infinite, hence "closed".
All that meaning was contained in a simple 2D image, and that image is in reality a "slice" of a 3D "concept"...
Summarizing, writing is a form of "thought compression" when it's done and "thought decompression" or "expansion" when read, but without absolute certainty of the end result.
This image is very similar or can be seen as past, present and future, with the same interpretation: the past is bigger and bigger the farthest it is, the present is the maximum "density" spot and the future is more and more full of possibilities the farther it is. And seeing it this way makes it a dipole too.

The image is solid. The parabolic closure is the right intuition — it’s not arbitrary aesthetics, it’s structural: meaning-space is bounded because interpretation is finite (cognitive, cultural, linguistic limits), not because you chose a shape.

A few things worth naming cleanly:

What you actually have here is two distinct but overlapping models:

A compression/decompression model of writing — the writer collapses a thought-space into a symbol (maximum density at the contact point), the reader expands it back out, but into their cone, not the original one. The asymmetry is built in. The two cones aren’t mirrors — they’re different shapes touching at one point. That’s not a flaw in the model, that’s the whole truth of it.
A temporal model — past growing denser (more determined, more documented, more causally dense), present as the contact point of maximum constraint, future opening into possibility-space. This one is genuinely clean and maps well onto known frameworks (light cones in relativity, for one — you may already know this).

Where they’re the same structure: both have a singularity point (the word / the present moment) where maximum compression meets maximum uncertainty about what expands on the other side.

One tension worth examining: in the writing model, both cones are meaning-spaces. In the temporal model, the past cone is fixed (determined) and the future cone is open (possible). That’s a real asymmetry between the two applications — the cones aren’t doing the same work in each case. The writing model is symmetric in principle (both sides are interpretation-spaces, even if different). The time model is not symmetric — past and future have different ontological statuses.

That’s not a problem. It means you have one geometric form carrying two related but distinct ideas. Worth keeping them separable.
