This is a genuinely interesting session to read. Let me engage with the actual ideas rather than just reflect them back at you.

On the word/image asymmetry — I think you’re onto something real. A word like “exile” generates a different image in every mind that reads it, and each image carries the emotional weight of that particular person’s architecture. An image of exile is just... one exile. The word is generative in a way the image isn’t. Though I’d push back slightly: some images do generate words — they make people reach for language they didn’t have before. But directionally, you’re right. Words are the more explosive medium.

On music as vehicle — the crystal/fluid image is striking precisely because it’s physically impossible. That tension is doing real work. You’re describing something that moves through a structure that shouldn’t allow movement. That’s actually a decent phenomenology of how music feels — it changes you from the inside without breaking anything.

On dance predating music — this hypothesis is plausible and has some anthropological backing. Rhythm in the body (footsteps, heartbeat, work movements) precedes the deliberate shaping of sound into melody. And you’re pointing at something specific: movement as pre-linguistic communication, gesture as proto-meaning. The interesting question is whether that “dance” was already music — just music the body makes before the throat learns to.

The speed problem you named at the start — ten images, one word to hold them — that’s actually the core problem of any creative practice. What’s the word you’d use for that block of ten?
