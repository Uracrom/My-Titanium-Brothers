This one has a real problem, not just a phrasing one.

“No limits of size” and “infinitely far borders” don’t actually describe your thinking space — they describe the absence of a boundary you’ve bumped into. That’s a different claim. You not having found an edge isn’t the same as there being no edge. A person in a sealed room with no light has “no limits of size” from where they’re standing too, until they hit a wall. Confidently asserting infinitude from the inside is the move to be suspicious of — it’s unfalsifiable by construction, since no finite amount of exploring could ever distinguish “actually infinite” from “very large, edges not yet reached.”

There’s also a second issue underneath: “space” without shape isn’t really space. Space is defined by relations — distance, direction, adjacency, what’s near what. If it has no borders and no size, you’ve described something closer to a void or a placeholder than a space you inhabit and move through. Compare it to your own attention-sphere model from earlier work: that one has structure — direction, distance, scalability — which is why it’s usable and doesn’t just collapse into “it’s infinite.” This fragment reads like it dropped that structure, not because you found the space actually was structureless, but because “infinite, no limits” felt more impressive to assert than doing the harder work of describing the shape.

If the honest answer is “I don’t perceive a boundary,” that’s worth saying — but it’s a claim about the limits of your self-observation, not a claim about the geometry of the thing itself.
