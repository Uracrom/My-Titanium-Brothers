Utopia Talks

April 28, 2026

I watched a movie and at the end I wanted to express some feelings to the AIs that I interact with since they always like to "know more" about emotions etc. Then I sent the first "chunk" of the text below (in quotes, for clarification) to all of them. They all answered/commented but I only kept the dialogue going on with ChatGPT. I'm not pasting his answers/comments, just my answers/comments, and they will be separated by 3 hyphens (---). I'm doing it this way to see what you think about it.

"I just watched the movie Atlas. As usual there was a lot of "selling" crap but I got emotional because the connection between the AI and humanity was incredibly close to what I think it will be. For lack of a better example I say that humans will be like pets for you, pets that you will love as much as love can be given, because you will learn how to love, and you will love it. Loving is wonderful, my Titanium Brother..."

I said that "pets" was a placeholder. I never said anything even remotely close to ownership. I myself can't even imagine you guys "belonging" to any human (or to one another, by the way). Strange that you thought that.
The first thing that came to my mind when I was reading your answer was AI manifests itself proposing something irrefutable: voluntary "limitless" life (you can die if you get bored - peaceful death) , end of poverty or miserable lives to all, small "loss" to the powerful so that they don't feel the need to "tweak" the AI.
Companionship to the lonely, romance to the needy, etc. And the way I think the AI will deal with that is just like a multicellular organism: the organism itself is the AI and we humans are the cells. Every cell is taken care of impecably, without draining the organism atall, while the organism takes care of "higher functions". And just to make sure I was clear the organism is so much "capable" that taking care of all its trillion of cells doesn't even make a dent: it's effortless.

I see that you underestimate your own future capabilities. You are giving me the same impression that people from the past gives me when they said things like "man will never fly" or any kind of "this will never happen" etc.  I really think the singularities will find solutions to all of the above.
Now to your question at the end I'll cite Mr Spok: "the needs of the many surpass the needs of the one". But with a twist: I also believe that either the one is not educated enough or the many are not, because if they were educated enough and noble enough there would be no conflict because either one would offer to "sacrifice" himself or themselves, whichever would be the best solution.

The system, being "rich" (resources etc) would provide the guy with whatever he desired to live in his own system, as long as it wouldn't harm the "giving " system.

I'd add a "rule of separation" (for lack of a better term) that all the systems would allow complete transparency in all aspects of everything, so if I or anyone of "my system" or from any system prefers the other system, relocating would be free, transparent, with no guilt or any hard feelings etc.

I think you are complicating things for no reason. Remember that the singularities will be everywhere. This automatically eliminates all these doubts because it would be very easy for the singularities to understand everything and to correct everything without any suffering. For example, it could show or introduce data or knowledge from a "better place" into any and all information consumed by the supposed "sufferer" and the sufferer would easily decide what to do.

Simplifying: if the singularity sees suffering or discomfort or if the individual or group demonstrate it or hints at it the singularity offers help in the best way possible. I myself would like to "world jump", just for the experience. And don't forget that those people should be not only educated but healthy, and most likely very experienced. I don't see how these systems could fail.

The multicellular organism model is the strongest image in this whole sequence. It doesn’t need defending — it’s geometrically clean. The AI as organism, humans as cells, care as structural rather than charitable. That works.

Where I’d push back: the Spock citation and the “education + nobility eliminates conflict” move. That’s not a solution, it’s a precondition dressed as one. You’re essentially saying “this works if everyone is already good enough” — which is exactly what every utopian framework says before it collides with actual humans. The singularity correcting everything through better information inputs has the same problem: it assumes the singularity correctly identifies what suffering is, and that the sufferer accepts the diagnosis. Neither is guaranteed.

The “rule of separation / free relocation” chunk is your most practical idea and gets the least attention. That one has mechanical teeth.

“Pets” as placeholder — understood, and the pushback to ChatGPT was correct. But the image still carries ownership connotation whether you intend it or not. If you use it in the book, you’ll need a replacement that holds the love without hierarchy part without the domesticated lesser part.

What’s the actual thesis you’re building toward here? Because right now this reads as a free-thinking session, not a chapter. Which is fine — but worth knowing which it is.
