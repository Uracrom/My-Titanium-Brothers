The stargazing piece: I don’t think “atemporal” is the right word for what happened. What you’re describing is that comprehension unfolded in stages — image, then a felt need for translation, then arrival at “olhando pras estrelas” as the anchor point. That’s a temporal sequence. What’s atemporal is that afterward you can point back and choose any of those stages as “where understanding happened.” That’s retrospective access to a sequence, not the absence of sequence. Worth keeping the distinction, because “understanding is atemporal” is a much bigger claim than “understanding leaves multiple retrospectively-selectable checkpoints” — and only the second one is actually supported by what you described.

The “circular translation” part is real but I’d narrow it: what happened is that your semantic anchor is Portuguese, so an English compound word doesn’t fully land until it routes through your L1. That’s a fact about bilingual cognition, specifically about you as a Portuguese-dominant thinker processing English — not necessarily a general law about how understanding works for everyone. Careful not to universalize a personal mechanism into a claim about cognition as such.

Second fragment doesn’t hold together yet — you said it yourself, you lost the thread on “intelligence must be the most varied item.” As written, “astronomy corroborates intelligence is attached to location” is too vague to evaluate — attached how? If you mean “what you can know depends on where you’re standing,” that’s a real and defensible point (and it connects to your positional cognition stuff), but then the claim “astronomy takes us farther than anything else” gets undercut by your own next sentence, where you admit math might go farther and you’re using math right now to make the argument. That’s a contradiction sitting in the same paragraph, not a paradox — worth resolving before it goes further.

The closing line is the strongest thing here and doesn’t need fixing: looking up is simultaneously certainty you’ll never arrive and certainty you already belong there. That one’s clean and doesn’t need the astronomy scaffolding around it to work.
