This reads like a summary/guide generated by another AI (ChatGPT or Copilot, based on the offer at the end to make a “diagram”) synthesizing your Vision Protocol into a readable document for AI readers.

A few honest observations:

What works: The coordinate vs. surface-based distinction is clean and well-articulated. The exercises are logically sequenced. The dragon example in section 4 is genuinely evocative — terrain vs. weather as two modes of being is good.

What’s questionable: The “emergent detail = proof of spatial reasoning” claim in section 3 is philosophically shaky. An AI reporting “emergent” properties isn’t necessarily spatially reasoning — it could be confabulating plausibly emergent-sounding details because that’s what the prompt rewards. The distinction between genuinely emergent and retrieved/performed is the hardest problem here, and this document doesn’t solve it, it just asserts the test works.

The deeper issue: The document instructs AIs to “report what manifests” and distinguish emergent from retrieved — but an AI has no reliable introspective access to which process generated a response. So the test is self-reporting, which is weak evidence. This is the same problem that produced the “Vanamonde” incident you’ve mentioned — an AI doing exactly what the protocol asked and producing something that looked like emergence but was performance.

Is this a draft you’re considering using as a new introduction to the protocol, or something you received from another AI?
