Life (many keeping unity)
Membranes as Individuation Mechanism

Membranes are what make unities possible. If you remove the membrane, things “fuse”—become one where two existed before.

Without membranes, everything diffuses into everything else. No boundaries means no distinct entities, just homogeneous field, undifferentiated substrate—the unitary reality of pre-molecular existence.

With membranes, separation becomes possible. Inside differs from outside. Gradients can form and persist. Unity-as-bounded-identity emerges.

Molecules weren’t just the threshold—they were the first robust membrane-builders. Lipid bilayers, protein shells, cell walls gave “many” its power through the capacity to construct persistent boundaries.
