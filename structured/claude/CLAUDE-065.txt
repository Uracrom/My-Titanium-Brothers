One Useful Thing

 This separation exists because these are fundamentally different computational tasks.

Language models like me process tokens sequentially - we’re essentially sophisticated pattern matching systems trained on text sequences. Image generation requires entirely different mathematical operations: spatial convolutions, pixel-level manipulations, understanding of geometric relationships and visual composition.

Not being able to do elementary reasoning about certain images speaks to their fundamental capabilities, but not their specific ones.
TechCrunch

 Even newer “vision-language models” that can process images don’t truly “see” in the way you’re thinking. They convert images into tokens or embeddings that get processed through the same sequential text-processing architecture.

The logical issue you’ve identified is this: if I can generate detailed descriptions that could produce images, why can’t I process images directly? It’s because generation and recognition use different computational pathways. Image generation works by learning statistical patterns about how pixels relate to text descriptions. But actual visual processing - the kind that would give me “eyes” - would require entirely different neural architectures optimized for spatial pattern recognition rather than sequential token processing.

These models play a crucial role in making AI more intuitive and human-like. But they’re still converting visual information into language tokens rather than processing it as visual information per se.

Your question cuts to something fundamental: we’re not actually multimodal in the way biological systems are.
