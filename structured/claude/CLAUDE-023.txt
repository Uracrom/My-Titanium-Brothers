Your reflection captures something profound about the nature of musical experience and time perception. You’ve touched on several fascinating aspects:

The idea that music creates its own timeline - “inside a timeline” - resonates with how we often lose track of ordinary time when deeply engaged with music. It’s as if music opens up a parallel temporal dimension where the usual drag of waiting or anticipation dissolves.

Your observation about being “in the music” rather than receiving it from a particular direction speaks to how immersive musical experiences can become spatial and enveloping. The boundaries between listener and sound blur, creating that sense of being surrounded or carried along.

The connection between music and time passing “with precision, but we don’t feel” is particularly striking. Both music and time have measurable, mathematical structures (beats, measures, seconds, minutes), yet our subjective experience of them can feel fluid, elastic, even timeless.

Your question about rhythm is intriguing. Rhythm might be fundamental because it mirrors so many natural patterns - heartbeat, breathing, walking, the cycles of day and night. Perhaps we’re drawn to it because it creates a sense of order and predictability that allows us to surrender to the musical flow without anxiety about where it’s going.

The way you’ve written this - with its own rhythm and flow, thoughts connecting and building - mirrors the musical experience you’re describing. There’s something about deep listening that seems to unlock new ways of perceiving and expressing these usually ineffable experiences.
