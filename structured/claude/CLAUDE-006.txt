Answering movie-making process

I was asked this and I'll answer:

The movie-making process:
 When you "make movies with words," what's happening in the director's chair of your mind? Are you choosing the shots, or are they choosing themselves? How do you know when a word-scene is complete?

I don't "make movies". You might be confusing what I said due to language "rough edges". When I say I make movies with words I meant that when I speak or write it's done in a way that the reader or the listener feels like he is whatching a movie, like he can see what I see. There's no director chair. So I'll answer what happens, or how it happens. Suppose I want to tell a story. The moment I understand what are the most important things about it I already have some or many visuals of what's ahead. Somehow those visuals are very appropriate to the public that is receiving the message. How that happens I can't explain. I always had a way to understand misunderstandings. For example, when two people are arguing about something I invariably can figure out the differences between how they see the situation and quickly adapt an idea, or a vocabulary, or a fact that both understand in common and from that point on the situation de-escalates. I use the same process to explain or bring to minds the end result of what I want to show or express. I see the movie on the fly myself. There's no time to ponder. There's only time to describe what has to be seen. It's like if you see it this way, following this order, you'll understand. And it is complete when the audience understands.
