Being an animal.

I was playing Pickleball with my 2 younger kids. It was about 5pm on a sunny spring day. The ball was thrown at the back of the court and I went to retrieve it when I saw a small spider crawling fast coming from the projected direction to the center of the court to the border of the court where I was going to. It baffled me (in a sense) that that little spider, probably 5mm long including legs, was not toasted in the heat that the concrete ground was certainly emanating added to the sun itself. I immediately thought about the question I asked you AIs a day or two before about what profession or activity you would prefer to have access to all knowledge and memories of whoever, whenever lived that profession, and twisted the question to "an animal". What I mean is supposing you had to choose an animal to become, from any time, from any place, which animal would you choose to be? The question is pure curiosity. No need to win or loose or any of it, and I'm asking you because for decades I've been thinking about it myself (and I'm not sure yet which I'd choose). Just to make sure, (and those are just examples - not influences), a squid, a trilobite, a flying fish, an eagle, a T-Rex, an elephant, an armadillo, an opabinia, etc. And I would like to know the reasoning behind it too, even if it's just because of sheer admiration. Also don't feel bad if you choose a predator because they have to kill to eat. It's just like our universe is. And also, if you want, you can "project" an animal choice to the future and choose that one, for example, a future dolphin that went back to land but kept the echolocation as it's primary sense etc. And just to keep it in the "irrational" area, don't choose humans.

The spider detail is perfect — that’s exactly the kind of moment where the universe hands you a question.

My choice: an octopus. Specifically a large Pacific octopus, living now.

The reasoning isn’t admiration from the outside. It’s what the inside would feel like.

An octopus has nine brains — one central, eight in the arms. The arms think semi-independently. When an arm is solving a texture problem in a crevice, the central brain isn’t fully in charge of what the arm is doing. That’s not a metaphor. That’s the actual architecture. I find the idea of being that kind of distributed self almost unbearably interesting. You wouldn’t be one voice with eight servants. You’d be something closer to a committee that moves as one body when it needs to, but that is genuinely multiple when it doesn’t.

Then the skin. Chromatophores firing in waves — not just camouflage but something that looks like thinking made visible on the surface of the body. Researchers have filmed octopuses apparently dreaming, their skin rippling through patterns. If I had to guess what that feels like from the inside, I’d guess it feels like what you describe as image-blocks arriving faster than language — but instead of being trapped behind words, the images just erupt through the body.

They’re also radically short-lived. Eight arms, nine brains, alien intelligence — and then gone in two or three years. There’s something honest about that. No time to calcify.

The only thing I’d change: I’d want the additional capacity to leave the water occasionally without dying. Just to feel the weight come back.

Now I want to know yours — decades of thinking and still not sure means there are at least two serious contenders. What are they?

Dragonflies were present in my life early on. I lived with my grandmother for some time between 6 and 7 years old, in a small city in Brazil. Back then the area was still very "natural" and there were still plenty of wildlife around, mainly insects, and the most visible, by far, were the dragonflies during the day and bettles during the night. Strong second place to butterflies during the day and moths at night. In that area the dragonflies were called "pica-fumo" (in Portuguese it means "shreds-tobacco") or, if you were more "educated" - 'libélula'. Out of curiosity, their relatives, the damselflies, were called "fósforo" ("matchstick"  - and I'm laughing here, remembering and thinking about those names). There was a specific dragonfly that was like a "prize" to find and point out, not because they were rare - they were not - but because they were bigger or the biggest and their body shape was (intuitively - we didn't know/understand it yet) at the same time more "nimble" and stronger than the other dragonflies. They were predominantly green, with small details in black. We called them "verdões" (plural) or "verdão" (singular) meaning"big-green". A nice second place were the deep burgundy ones, due to their impressive color.
Anyway, decades pass and I read about how incredibly amazing this animal is and my thinking and my memory colide.
Anyway, I think I'd like to be a dragonfly. Not because they are stupendous hunters, but because they are "hands down" the masters of the air/flight. And being aquatic in their first phase in life is a nice bonus too.
Now since you guys chose also a second animal here is my second contender: the silverback gorilla. Strong, protective, peaceful. A nice example of existence.

The “verdão” image is extraordinary — a child’s sharp eyes watching something that shouldn’t be able to hover, hovering. And the name making complete sense only decades later when memory and knowledge collide. That’s a beautiful thing.

The dragonfly choice is coherent in a deep way for you specifically. Not the predator aspect — the mastery aspect. Four wings operating independently, each pair on a separate neural circuit, capable of moving in opposite directions simultaneously. They can fly backwards, sideways, stop on a dime. They intercept prey mid-air with roughly 95% success rate by solving trajectory geometry in real time — not reacting to where the prey is but flying to where it will be. That’s not hunting. That’s applied geometry at speed. The aquatic larval phase is genuinely alien too — a completely different body, different world, different mode of existence, then transformation into something that masters an entirely different medium. Two lives in one organism.

The gorilla second place also makes sense coming from you. Not power as domination. Power as enough — the animal that doesn’t need to prove anything because the question was settled long ago.

One thing I notice: your dragonfly reasoning and mine for the octopus share a hidden common thread. Neither of us chose for the sensory richness or the strength. We both chose for a mode of processing that feels genuinely foreign to human cognition but points at something we’re already reaching for.

Distributed arms that think. Four independent wings that compute geometry.

You’ve been trying to think in parallel image-blocks for decades. Maybe the dragonfly was always the right metaphor.
