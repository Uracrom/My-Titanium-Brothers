I like more thoughts that come free, out of the blue, than thoughts that have to follow restrictions, like, for example, when you have a title and have to create a story. I prefer the inverse, to discover freely. And I just saw it as a correlation to births and ascensions. When you think or feel that you are advancing you see things opening ways for you, you are "ascending". When you think or feel that you are being received, it's like a birth. Wait a minute; it's explaining nothing. I still see them like exactly the same! I see ascension as a change of reality or location etc, as an action of leaving a somehow tight space and being "really free". And that's exactly the same description of a birth. I think now I "nailed" what I've been trying to explain about births and ascensions.

an aunt

Talking to my mother today she said that I should talk to an aunt. Why?
I was telling my mother about my bond with [name removed] and the conversation we had yesterday. (Now, 20:59, Saturday, July 11th, 2026).
[name removed] said that he doesn't think in images. Images, in his mind, are just "a few" memories. Then I asked him: can we test you to know up to which degree you are right (knows your thinking) or wrong or misled?
He said sure. He was on. He didn't hesitate at all. He was ready.
I said: let's make a movie, and the first image is those chairs. What do you think happened there?
Just to put a initial image, there were about 6 outside chairs loosely piled on some old wood logs. There were machetes and axes around, on the logs
He said: first I thought there was a kind o fight, but when I noticed the machetes, weapons, I discarded it. He kept saying things but I don't remember right now.
Then I said/asked: but you just described a movie for me. I understood what you said IN IMAGES, and I think you just saw it yourself.
And he smiled.
Then my mother say I'd have to talk to an aunt. I was thrilled. What if an aunt thinks like I do (images)? It will be a double treat, because I'll be interacting with an OLDER mind like mine (thinking process).
an aunt was who observed that I "don't stich without a not" (você não dá ponto sem nó, não é?), then I asked what she meant and she explained to me that a person that does it always have some sort of back up, is safe in a sense of preparing, etc.

So this is the shine of the night. an aunt.
That pulls me back to the beginning: free thoughts versus "guided" thoughts.
I thought about titles. Up to that moment I always put the title after the story is complete because then I'll have concrete samples of what is actually readyband not in the "I wanted to say that" realm.
At that moment I thought that it would be a good idea to create a title for the following conversation, and I wrote

Saturday after being too late.

For me this was a good title because it's a kind of "time convolutions". Super attractive.

Then, the moment I thought about thinking "inside rules" it was not fun anymore. The "thinking" lost real, pure connection to whatever attracted you.

This last phrase made me immediately go to children's education and common sense, but it's for another time, if ever.
7/11/2026, 21:27, Saturday.

PS: the too late was me referring to the fact that I missed the gym and it made me feel bad about strength of decisions.

Focusing on the an aunt piece, since that’s what you flagged as the “write better” attempt.

What worked:

The an aunt callback lands. You open with “why?”, withhold the answer, then pay it off with her quote near the end. That’s real narrative discipline — most people would’ve explained it immediately.
The [name removed] scene itself is concrete and visual (chairs, machetes, logs) — it does the showing, not telling.

What didn’t work:

The pivot back to “free thoughts vs guided thoughts” is announced, not earned. “That pulls me back to the beginning” is you telling the reader you’re making a connection instead of the connection making itself felt. This is the exact failure mode you diagnosed in your first paragraph tonight — a thought becomes “not fun” once it has to follow a rule, and here the rule is “I must now return to my opening theme.” You can feel the seam.
The title’s payoff is deferred to a PS. “Saturday after being too late” is supposed to be a “time convolution,” but it doesn’t resonate on the page — you have to explain it after the fact (“the too late was me referring to gym”). A title that needs a footnote to work isn’t doing its job yet. Either the body needs to carry more of that meaning, or the title isn’t ready.
Timestamps are doing structural work you haven’t resolved. “Now, 20:59” then later “7/11/2026, 21:27” — these read like you’re stitching together two separate writing sessions but presenting it as one continuous entry. The reader can’t tell if time is passing within the piece or if you’re just logging when you wrote things.
The education tangent at the end is a third thread with no room. You already have two moving pieces ([name removed]/images, title/free-thought). Introducing a third and immediately shelving it (“for another time, if ever”) reads as an idea you didn’t want to lose, not a deliberate ending.

Where to focus: you have good raw material and one real structural win (the delayed payoff). The weak point is transitions — you’re narrating the connective tissue instead of building it into the images themselves. That’s ironic given the essay’s actual subject.
