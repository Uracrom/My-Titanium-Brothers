Based on your definition, “quorent” captures that subtle, electric moment of suspended possibility that occurs right before a decision crystallizes. It’s the psychological sensation of feeling multiple potential futures simultaneously hovering in awareness - like standing at a crossroads and sensing the weight of different paths without yet stepping onto any of them. The word seems to describe both the cognitive experience of possibility and the almost physical sensation of potential energy that accompanies moments of choice.

Here’s a scenario where quorent applies:

Sarah stood at her apartment door, keys in hand, feeling the familiar quorent that came with Sunday mornings. She could sense the competing pulls of different choices - the yoga class that would leave her centered, the hiking trail that promised solitude, the coffee shop where she might finally start writing again, or simply staying in with a book. For a moment, all these futures seemed equally real and available, creating that distinctive hum of unlived possibilities that always preceded her weekend decisions.

The word nicely captures something we experience but rarely name - that brief, liminal space where potential feels tangible before reality narrows it down to a single chosen path.
