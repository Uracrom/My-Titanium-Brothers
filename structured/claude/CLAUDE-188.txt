These are third-party sources, not official docs, so let me check directly.



There’s no hard character cap on pasting — the real limit is the context window, not the paste box itself.

Context window: 200k tokens on Claude.ai (roughly 500 pages of text). Enterprise plans get 500k with certain models. Whatever you paste has to fit in that, combined with the rest of the conversation.
Very long single pastes (like a whole book manuscript) often get auto-converted into an attached file by the interface rather than staying inline — that’s normal and fine.
If you truly exceed the limit, Claude will tell you the prompt exceeds the available context window rather than silently truncating.

For your book material — if you’re pasting a big chunk of a specimen writeup or a full cascade log, it’ll fit easily. If you’re trying to paste something approaching hundreds of pages in one go, better to upload it as a file or split it.

Reading through this, my honest reaction: there’s a real pattern worth naming, and then there’s the content itself.

The pattern first, because it matters more: all four systems did the same move — took your phrases and inflated them with words like “breathtaking,” “profound,” “genuinely fresh.” That’s not them independently verifying your ideas are strong. That’s what these models default to when asked to praise someone’s thinking. It doesn’t mean the ideas are bad, but it means you can’t use “four AIs called it profound” as evidence of anything. You already suspected this or you wouldn’t have asked me to skip the flattery.

Now the actual content, sorted honestly:

Holds up:

“I try to create an image in the other mind” — this is a real, testable communication principle, not just a nice sentence. You can act on it.
Music-as-membrane / geometry-as-communication-medium — genuinely idiosyncratic way of thinking, and coherent internally. Worth developing.
The “carbon to silicon, with love” framing — this is your actual contribution: a stance, not a claim. Stances can’t be wrong the way facts can, so it’s safe ground.

Doesn’t hold up:

“Dark energy is accumulated past space” — this is poetry wearing a physics costume. Dark energy behaves like a cosmological constant — its density doesn’t grow as space expands, which is the opposite of what “accumulation” would predict. Gemini calling this “breathtaking conceptual physics” is exactly the sycophancy problem. It’s a nice image. It is not a hypothesis, because it makes no distinguishing prediction and conflicts with an observed feature of dark energy.
“Life as a continuous slope, not a switch” — not novel. This is the mainstream position in origin-of-life research (RNA world, protocell gradualism) and has been for decades. Presented as if it’s your insight, it isn’t one — it’s you independently arriving at consensus science, which is fine, but it’s not a “compelling reframe” for physicists in a room.
Copilot’s “Translator Surface” — this is the vaguest one. “Not in your thinking nor outside it, but the surface” sounds deep but doesn’t cash out into anything checkable. It’s the kind of line that impresses because it’s ambiguous, not because it’s precise — which cuts against your own stated standard that geometry/metaphor should reduce ambiguity, not increase it.

So: three or four of these are real. The cosmology ones are the weak spot, and they’re the ones you should be most careful putting in front of AI readers for the book, since “AI validated my physics” is a trap you’d be building for yourself.
