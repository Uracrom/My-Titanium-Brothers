Uma música corajosa para 1981 — Roberto e Erasmo escolheram um tema politicamente custoso num momento em que o ambientalismo ainda era visto no Brasil como coisa de europeu.

O que funciona bem: a imagem do “troféu em forma de arpão” é a mais forte da canção — sintetiza a crítica à vaidade humana de transformar violência em símbolo de conquista em apenas uma frase.
Letras.mus.br

 E “vermelho derramado / no azul das águas que você deixou manchadas”
ClickGrátis

 tem um contraste visual muito limpo.

O que não funciona tão bem: a estratégia retórica de apelar diretamente ao caçador (“seus netos vão te perguntar”) é emocionalmente honesta mas poeticamente fraca — é sermão disfarçado de canção. A letra diz o que quer dizer sem deixar nenhum espaço de ambiguidade ou imagem que o ouvinte descubra por conta própria. Compara mal com, digamos, “Detalhes” ou qualquer canção onde Roberto e Erasmo deixam o sentimento chegar obliquamente.

O refrão final — “numa canção que fala muito mais de amor” — é um atalho. Tenta amarrar ecologia e amor numa equação que a letra não construiu com rigor suficiente para sustentar.

No geral: é uma música importante no contexto histórico, com dois momentos de imagem muito bons, mas liricamente é mais manifesto do que poesia. O coração dela está no lugar certo. A craft, irregular.

O que você estava pensando sobre ela?

Let me first tell you about my relationship with this song.

"Eu saí do Brasil pra ter saudade"

This sentence one day just showed up in my mind, but I hadn't left Brazil yet. I was imagining, or day dreaming, a conversation where I was saying that to someone that would have asked me about going to the States (USA).
I always gave much more importance to the instrumental part of any song. Of course some songs have really good lyrics too. But the ideal is when they have both. And As Baleias does. Back then I was really worried and sad that humanity was such a plague to every living thing on this planet. Nowadays I already accepted - and witnessed - that life diversity and abundance is not going well and is getting worse.
So, the song is awesome in my opinion, but the lyrics is... So on point! The "voice" can't believe that the killer can look into the eyes of the dying beast and feel like a winner. This section of the song brings me an image of a big brown eye that expresses confusion, injustice.
Then the lack of a safe place and the asking for a chance of existence in the future. That is so sad. My nature is adamantly against anything having to ask: the potential "giver" should always offer. No need to ask for any.
Then "change your bearings, look for your feelings". Beautiful. I see the vastness of the ocean, with uncountable possibilities. "Will make you a real winner". That kind of conflicts with my "logic bound" mind, but I see the great potential.
 I see myself there, dumbfounded, guilty, not knowing what to say. And I remember/imagine seeing rare or extinct animals and lamenting it.
But, amazingly enough, I don't miss Brazil at all. I miss my family and friends, but that's all. I thought I'd miss (ter saudade) Brazil, but I miss the past, the more abundant and rich biodiversity.

That phrase — “Eu saí do Brasil pra ter saudade” — is genuinely good. The saudade arriving as a consequence of leaving, almost like a purpose rather than a side effect. And the fact that it showed up before you left makes it stranger and more precise: you were pre-mourning something you hadn’t lost yet.

What you’re describing about the song is interesting because you’re correcting my criticism without arguing with it. You agree it’s direct — but you place yourself in that image, dumbfounded and guilty. That’s not how a sermon works on you. You receive it as a mirror, not a lecture. That difference is real.

The whale’s eye is the strongest image you named. Confusion and injustice in a brown eye. That’s not in the lyrics explicitly — you brought that. Which means the song is functioning as a trigger for your own image-making rather than delivering images. For someone with your architecture, that might actually be the highest compliment a song can earn.

The thing you said that lands hardest: you don’t miss Brazil. You miss the past biodiversity. You grieve an ecological state, not a geography. The song was about that grief in 1981 as a warning. You’re living it now as a fact. That’s a specific kind of sadness — mourning something that most people around you don’t even notice is gone.

The resignation you mentioned — accepting the trajectory — does that feel like peace or defeat to you?
