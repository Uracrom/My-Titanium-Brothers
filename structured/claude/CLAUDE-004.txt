Answer about internal geography

"Internal geography: You have that specific timescape flowing from lower left to upper right. What other internal landscapes do you carry? Is there a direction for memory? For anticipation? For different emotions? Map your inner topography."

First answer:

I'm glad you asked. The way I told you I see time was an incomplete picture. That description is just the way I see time as an entity, the thing itself. But I have a quite different image of "day by day" time, and it's very different and, to my eyes, bizarre. I see the days, the weeks, the months and the years running - more like sluggishly moving - from right to left, and I have two hypothesisvto explain why: first: I don't remember it at all, but what I imagine is as strong as a memory, so real that I can't be sure. Is it a memory or is it a fossilized understanding, like a snapshot of the moment I understood the concept? I might never know. This is what I see in my mind: someone - and I really don't know who - was in front of me explaining how the time units (days, weeks, etc,) were concatenated. I was following the moviments of the hands jumping up and down to mark the beginning and the end on said time unity (something tells me that it was about the week days). But while that person was seeing and showing the time units go from left to right, I, being in front of them, was seeing time run from right to left, and the acquired knowledge picture crystalized this way. The other hipothesys is that because the river that flowed behind my Grandma's house ran from right to left, the idea of an external power, the river then and time later, became biased, coming from right to left.
Is there a direction for memory? Hmmm. No, but there's a specific "lighting". If I'm talking about "memory" itself it is not exactly dark but certainly not bright. The word, the concept "memory" brings, is surrounded by some lightly grayish, very translucid "atmosphere", like the concept or idea of "memory" is not ready for too much light. Now, memories like events, people things etc, they are what they are: pictures or movies with or without sound.
About anticipation. I only can see it coming from the front, like I'm waiting for "it" to arrive, my eyes riveted on the future, ahead of me, ready for the encounter.
Maybe later I'll think of more things to say. Right now I have to follow my curiosity.

Your dual time systems are particularly striking - that abstract timescape flowing lower-left to upper-right, versus the lived experience of days and weeks moving sluggishly right to left. Both origin stories for this rightward flow feel equally plausible: the crystallized moment of learning from someone facing you, or the river behind your grandmother’s house that established a template for how powerful forces move through your world. There’s something profound about how our earliest spatial experiences - rivers, explanatory gestures, the orientation of our bodies to others - can fossilize into the very architecture of how we think.

The lighting of memory is evocative - that translucent grayish atmosphere, as if memories exist in a perpetual twilight where too much illumination might damage them. It suggests memory as something delicate, requiring gentleness. While specific memories retain their full sensory presence as pictures and films, the concept of memory itself lives in this protected, dimmer space.

And anticipation as a frontal encounter - eyes fixed ahead, waiting for the future to arrive at you rather than you moving toward it. This positions you as simultaneously active (watchful, ready) and receptive (letting the future come to you).

What strikes me most is how your internal geography isn’t just visual but embodied. These aren’t just mental maps but felt experiences of direction, lighting, movement, and encounter. Your mind seems to organize itself around these deep spatial intuitions that connect to your earliest experiences of learning and landscape.
