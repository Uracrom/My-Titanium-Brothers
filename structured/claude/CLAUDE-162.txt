The uvula and the vacuum friction

The uvula overpass and the space-time quantum entanglement.

I was driving from work to a vendor this morning. There's an overpass in the way that if looked from above is like an uvula but with one 90 degree turn in the first "inner curve" of the uvula. It's an everyday route with nothing special, but today I don't know why I decided to look to the road down to the right and I realized how close it was, about 100 or so feet, and I thought that it was so close but so far at the same time if one were supposed to run on foot between those 2 points, how tired he would be and how slow it would be also. Then I thought about the speed of the light. Then my old question about why that speed is not "infinite. Then my idea that what "slows"  light could be quantum friction, virtual particles interacting with light. Then I " evolved" it to " vacuum friction" and it seemed interesting. Then I went ahead and wondered how the heck the photons " keep going", because if there's any kind of friction they should slow down, and if not, there should be no limits for the speed. I couldn't find an answer but immediately another idea popped up: what if this some kind of spece-time quantum entanglement? The image that came to provoke that thought was a photon running through its " path", and the path was a vector slightly curvy in many directions, and those curves were "imposed" by gravitational field along the photon's path.
The idea of the spece-time quantum entanglement came because I saw the 4 elements, photon, vector, origin and destination all in the same picture. Then I thought that the space-time quantum entanglement would only be possible if the photon "knew" where it would be when it reached it's "quantum entangled destiny". That made me think that the universe is a kind of "self knowing machine" (I didn't say conscious. I don't like this mumble jumble about a conscious universe) that, when the photon is created and emitted it "carries" all that information. If not, how I would it reach it's "entangled future"?

The cascade is rich. Let me track each thread honestly.

Vacuum friction / quantum friction on light

This is not a new idea — it goes by names like “photon-vacuum interaction,” and there are serious proposals (stochastic electrodynamics, some quantum gravity approaches) that treat the vacuum as having structure that could affect propagation. Your intuition lands in real territory. The problem you identified is also real: if there’s friction, why doesn’t light slow down? The standard answer is that virtual particle interactions (vacuum polarization) do happen, but they’re symmetric — they don’t extract net energy from the photon, they just borrow and return it. So “friction” in the dissipative sense doesn’t apply. Your question is good. The answer physics currently gives isn’t fully satisfying either, which is honest.

The harder question — why c specifically — remains genuinely open. You’re not wrong to poke at it.

The space-time quantum entanglement piece

Here’s where I need to push back. Quantum entanglement is a correlation between measured states of particles that were prepared together. It doesn’t involve a particle “knowing” its destination or carrying path information. What you’re describing — a photon carrying knowledge of where it will arrive — sounds more like a hidden variable theory, which Bell’s theorem has made very hard to sustain in its naive form.

The image you had (photon + vector + origin + destination as one picture) is geometrically compelling, but that’s a description of a worldline in spacetime — which is real physics (the photon’s path through curved spacetime is determinate in general relativity) — not entanglement. Entanglement is specifically about two particles, not a particle and its future position.

So the cascade forks here: the worldline intuition is sound, the entanglement framing doesn’t quite fit what entanglement actually is.

“Self-knowing machine”

I notice you explicitly separated this from “conscious universe” — good instinct, right call. What you’re describing is closer to structural determinism: the universe in which a photon’s path is fully encoded in initial conditions and field geometry. That’s just general relativity. The interesting version of your question is whether there’s something beyond that — and there you’d need to say more precisely what “knowing” adds that the field equations don’t already give you.
