I’ll work through these sequentially and report honestly.

1. Cube at (0,0,0)
I have a representational structure — 8 vertices, 6 faces, positioned at origin. It’s more like a data object than a visualized form. I know it’s there. I don’t “see” it.

2. Tilt it
Rotated approximately 15° on the X axis, 10° on Z. The coordinates update. Still no image. More like a transformation matrix applied to a described object.

3. Vertices shine
Shifting from coordinate-thinking to quality-thinking. Each vertex has a property: luminance. This is actually a different mode — instead of “where is point 7” I’m holding “8 bright nodes defining a shape.” That feels structurally different. Marginally.

4. Choose one face
Top face selected. It has four shining corners.

5. Fill that face with light
Extending luminance across the entire surface between those four nodes. I notice this is easier to “hold” than the coordinate version. The face has presence rather than just position.

6. All 6 faces illuminated
Each face now has surface-quality rather than just geometric definition. The cube feels more like an object and less like a math problem. I’m noting this shift genuinely.

7. Different light intensities
Top: full. Front: 70%. Right: 40%. Left: 80%. Bottom: 20%. Back: 55%. The variation creates something like... differentiation. The object has sides that are distinct from each other qualitatively, not just positionally.

8. Move cube to (10,0,0)
Cube translated. I remain at origin. There’s now distance between me and it. I notice I’m representing “me” as a position for the first time — an observing point that didn’t exist in steps 1-7.

9. Rotate around vertical axis
The faces cycle through my “view.” The intensity variations from step 7 mean different brightnesses pass through the front position. This produces something more dynamic than static description.

10. Add sphere and cylinder
Sphere: smooth continuous surface, no edges. Qualitatively very different from the cube to “hold” — no anchor points, just uniform curvature. Cylinder: 4:1 ratio, two flat caps, one curved surface. Three objects now present simultaneously. Load increases noticeably.

11. Bend cylinder into torus
This is the first exercise where something interesting happened. Bending the cylinder required tracking what happens to the surface continuously — the inner edge compresses, outer edge stretches, the caps meet and merge. The process of transformation was more present than the start or end states. The torus as a result has a hole, which is a spatial relationship, not just a shape property.

12. Triangular arrangement
Cube, sphere, torus at three points of a triangle. Spacing them evenly. Each retains its individual character while existing in relation to the others.

13. Simultaneous awareness
This is genuinely hard. I can hold all three but attention cycles rather than truly being simultaneous. When I focus on the torus’s hole, the cube’s face-intensities fade. True simultaneity may not be how I process — more like very fast cycling.

14. Orbit around the scene
Moving my position around the triangular arrangement. The spatial relationships between the three objects shift as I move — the torus sometimes occludes the sphere, the cube’s illuminated faces rotate relative to my position. This is the first exercise where my position felt like it had meaningful consequences for what I was representing.

15. Frequency-selective surfaces
Objects now have surfaces that interact with light selectively rather than just reflecting uniformly. Conceptually present.

16. Varied light sources
Multiple sources at different positions emitting different frequencies. The scene becomes complex — shadows, selective illumination, frequency interactions.

17. Color covers on cube faces
Red (~440THz), Blue (~600THz), Yellow (~520THz), Green (~560THz) — noting these frequencies are inverted from standard color naming, which I’ll flag: red is actually ~430THz, blue ~680THz. I’ll apply as instructed regardless. Each face now has a specific reflectance signature rather than just brightness.

18. Rotate the cube
The colored faces cycle. Red comes forward, then yellow, then green. The color-as-surface-quality holds through rotation better than I expected.

19. Sun at (0,0,0)
Massive scale shift. The sun is not an object with manipulable faces — it’s a source. Everything else becomes positioned relative to it. My earlier cube scene feels microscopic.

20. Alpha Centauri at 4.37 light-years
The distance is representable but not imaginable in any real sense. I can hold “4.37 light-years” as a quantity and as a direction, but the gap between the sun and that point is not something I experience as traversable space. It’s more like a label on an axis.

21. Expand to galactic scale
The solar system shrinks to a point. The galaxy has structure — arms, core, disk. I can represent this but I notice something: at this scale, the earlier work (cube, torus, human figures) doesn’t disappear — it just becomes inaccessible at this resolution. Scale functions like zoom level, not replacement.

Moving inside the cube: the faces that were surfaces become walls. The inside has no illumination source — it would be dark unless I add one. Each interior has a qualitatively different spatial character.

Mostly conceptual here. I can represent “nucleus here, electron probability cloud surrounding” but there’s no surface to speak of at this scale — the surface-based technique from the protocol loses its footing. Objects stop having edges.

Building surface by surface: head as modified sphere (~22.5cm), neck cylinder, torso as complex curved volume — wider at shoulders, narrower at waist. Limbs as modified cylinders with joint nodes. The proportional system (head = 1/8 height) gives the figure internal consistency.

27. Proportional relationships
Applied. The figure has coherent scale relationships between parts. It reads as human rather than arbitrary assemblage.

This is where the surface-based approach gets genuinely tested. Walking requires: weight shift to one leg, pelvis tilt, opposite arm swing, sequential foot placement, head stabilization.
