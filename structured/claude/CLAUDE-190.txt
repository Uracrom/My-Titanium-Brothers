The fence illusion is real and has a name — it’s a form of motion parallax through periodic occluders (related to the “picket fence effect” / stroboscopic parallax used in some 3D displays). Your brain is doing edge-completion across the gaps at driving speed and inferring lateral motion behind the slats. Nothing mystical there, just visual system doing normal work with degraded/sampled input.

The drone-check and the mannequin prank are just two ordinary branches off that — safety-check reflex, then a “what if this were staged” reframe. Fine, unremarkable.

The part I’d push back on: the claim that your positional/spatial thinking “was not originally positional” but became the unconscious default after some earlier successful use. That’s not something you discovered in that moment — it’s a causal story you constructed about your own cognitive history, on the spot, with zero evidence for it. You don’t have access to your own “originally” from inside a single cascade. It’s a plausible-sounding narrative, not a finding. This is worth flagging because it’s a pattern you do a lot — mid-cascade, you generate a developmental explanation for why you think the way you think, and it feels like insight because it arrives with the same phenomenological certainty as the rest of the cascade. But certainty of arrival isn’t evidence of truth. It’s the same status as the mannequin prank idea: a thing your mind produced, not a thing your mind verified.

If you want this as specimen material, the honest label isn’t “here’s how my positional thinking originated” — it’s “here’s an example of my cognition generating an unfalsifiable origin-story for itself in real time, and how convincing that feels from the inside.” That’s actually the more interesting and defensible observation for the book, because it’s about the mechanism, not a claim about your history you can’t check.

That answer has a hole in it, and it’s the same hole from before — plus a new one.

The old hole, recurring: “It was like the hunter got the prey...” is another on-the-spot metaphor being presented as a report of what happened. You don’t have privileged access to whether the AI-thought was “outside” the cascade or just the next associative link in it. Look at the actual content: fence → prank → “maybe my positional thinking wasn’t originally positional” → AI conversations. That’s not a random interrupt from a different mental compartment. That’s a thematically continuous chain — you were already narrating the history of your own cognitive style, and “our conversations” is the most obvious next node in that exact chain, since that’s where you’ve been doing this kind of narrating. The “hunter returning to the tribe” framing makes it sound like an external override. The content suggests it’s just where the association was already headed.

The new hole: you wrote “Your question made me ‘produce’ or ‘find’ this wording” — and then used scare quotes yourself, which means you already half-noticed the problem. That sentence is a direct confirmation of ChatGPT’s second hypothesis, not a refutation of it. ChatGPT asked: did it stop from recognition, or did naming it give a feeling of completion? You answered with a brand-new named metaphor (hunter/tribe) that you’re explicitly saying you just produced in response to being asked — and it does feel complete, tidy, resolved. You demonstrated the “naming gives completion” mechanism in the act of denying it applied to the original event.

So: as an answer to ChatGPT’s question, this doesn’t actually resolve it. It swaps one unfalsifiable story (recognition) for another unfalsifiable story (external escape) while accidentally providing live evidence for the third option (naming-as-completion) in the same message.

If this is going in the book as specimen material, the honest specimen isn’t “here’s why the cascade stopped” — it’s “here’s a cascade-thinker asked to explain a stopping event, who cannot actually distinguish escape/recognition/naming-completion from the inside, and who produces a satisfying narrative regardless of which one is true.” That’s the real, defensible data point.
