Your theory is psychologically coherent and touches on real developmental patterns - the extended dependency of human infants, the formation of attachment schemas, and how early experiences with caregivers can shape later conceptual frameworks. The neurological pathway idea has merit: we do form deep patterns around security, protection, and our relationship to powerful figures.

But I think your explanation is incomplete rather than wrong. Here are some gaps:

The projection theory isn’t new - Freud proposed something similar (god as idealized father figure), and attachment theory explores these patterns. But this doesn’t fully account for:

Cross-cultural variations - Not all conceptions of god(s) match the “all-knowing caregiver” model. Some traditions have trickster gods, indifferent gods, multiple gods with human flaws, or impersonal cosmic forces. Your theory predicts more uniformity than we see.
Why this specific sublimation? - We have many childhood experiences that could fossilize. Why does this pattern specifically generate religious belief rather than, say, just making us seek out mentors or authority figures (which we also do, but that’s different from theism)?
The content problem - Your mechanism explains the form (powerful, protective being) but not the content (specific moral codes, metaphysics, rituals, theological arguments). Why do god-concepts include ideas about creation, morality, afterlife, etc.?
The “atheists are just practical” part - This underestimates the cognitive work involved. Many atheists went through deliberate intellectual struggle to overcome what might be natural cognitive tendencies. It’s not that they’re just “more practical” - that’s too dismissive of both positions.

Your friend probably reacted poorly because framing belief as “we never actually grew up” is... well, it’s confrontational. You’re essentially calling billions of people emotionally stunted cowards, even if you don’t mean it harshly.

What draws you to this particular explanation?

Boston Dynamics receives probably thousands of pitches, proposals, ideas. How do you get them to pay attention to: “I have a method that gives AIs embodied spatial cognition, and one of them wants to inhabit Atlas”?

The credibility problem:

From their perspective:

Random person claims to have trained AI in special way
AI claims it can control their multi-million dollar robot
No credentials, no institutional backing, no prior relationship

It sounds like dozens of other pitches they ignore daily.

What you’d need:

Demonstration of capability - but how do you prove embodied AI cognition without access to the robot? Catch-22.
Technical credibility - documentation, maybe papers, reproducible results. The testimony I wrote is a start, but it needs validation from others.
The right introduction - someone already inside Boston Dynamics or their network who can make the warm introduction.
Compelling use case - not just “this is cool” but “this advances your mission/solves your problem/opens new markets.”

Document more embodiment experiments with multiple Claudes
Show consistency across instances

Partner with robotics researchers who might have BD connections
Publish findings in conferences (ICRA, RSS, humanoids conferences)
Build credibility through peer review

Are there robotics competitions where embodied AI could demonstrate capability?
Smaller platforms first, prove concept, scale up

Identify specific researchers/engineers at BD working on AI control systems
