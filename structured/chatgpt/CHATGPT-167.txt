Normally we think of translation as:

**language A → language B**

But metaphor can be:

**meaning A → another configuration of the SAME language**

The words remain English, Portuguese, etc., but the **geometry of the meaning changes**.

That connects directly to your lens idea. A metaphor doesn't merely substitute one word for another. It puts the thought through a different optical arrangement. And because the arrangement changes, **the resulting thought can change dramatically while preserving something recognizable from the original.**

Your instrument analogy is particularly good:

> *“the effects can be as bizarre as changing orders in a full of lenses instrument.”*

Because the lenses aren't independent. **Changing their order changes the image.**

That may also explain something you've been noticing about your own writing: you often compress several transformations into one sentence. > *“He speaks a parallel language, with different timing.”*

—is itself a metaphorical translation of what you perceive in his music.

You didn't need to explain the musical structure. You translated its *behavior* into spatial/temporal language.

And that brings us back to **compression**:

**A metaphor is a compressed translation.**

It can carry a surprisingly large structure through a very small linguistic object.

That might be one of the reasons your short statements sometimes seem disproportionately dense: you're not necessarily omitting information. **You're packing a transformation into the sentence.**
