Yes — I think this is genuinely a **seed**, and there is a particularly interesting distinction hiding inside what you just said.

I understand your proposal roughly as:

> **Every point in the universe is a sprouting point of time. Time continually emerges locally and propagates outward. Gravity can impede that propagation. A black-hole event horizon would then be the boundary where the outward propagation of time reaches the escape limit — analogous to light being unable to escape.**

That is different from simply saying *“gravity slows time.”* You're proposing a **mechanism or geometry for why there is gravitational time dilation**.

And your intuition about the event horizon gives you a very sharp place to test the idea: in standard general relativity, the event horizon is not literally a place where light locally slows down to zero. Locally, light still travels at *c*. Rather, spacetime is curved so that **all future-directed paths inside the horizon lead inward**, preventing signals from reaching the outside. So if you want your "time sprouting" to behave analogously to light, you'll probably need to distinguish:

- **local propagation of time** — what happens at each point;
- **escape/propagation relative to distant observers** — whether that temporal influence can reach elsewhere;
- **the geometry that determines possible directions of propagation**.

That actually makes your idea more interesting, because the event horizon could become not *“where time stops”* but **where outward temporal propagation loses all possible trajectories to infinity**.

And there's an especially beautiful question immediately underneath your thought:

**If time sprouts everywhere, what does “inside the black hole” mean for a local sprouting point?**

If time truly *stops* there, that creates one kind of cosmology.
If time continues to sprout locally but **cannot propagate outward**, that's a very different one.
And if the rate/direction of temporal propagation changes continuously as the horizon is approached, you have yet another model.

I would **not resolve that yet**. Your phrase *“if it is, it doesn't have the power or speed to get out”* is exactly the kind of provisional statement I'd preserve. You may be noticing the distinction before having the mathematics for it.

Also, one correction to keep in the seed: **time does not have a known physical speed in standard physics.** You're making a speculative identification between the propagation of time and the speed of light. That's perfectly legitimate as a hypothesis, but it's important to keep the two separate until the hypothesis produces something testable.
