The stepped gradient is what happens when that same probing cannot curve continuously: cognition can only advance along available straight trajectories, reaches a boundary, travels laterally, finds another route upward, and repeats. So perhaps **the spiral and the stepped gradient aren't two different ways of thinking at all; they are two geometries produced by the constraints of the space being explored**. The spiral is what exploration looks like when the possibility-space permits continuous angular movement; the staircase is what the same convergence looks like when movement is constrained to straight segments and thresholds. And the particularly interesting part is that in both cases, **the movement itself is discovering the geometry**—you don't necessarily know the shape of the space beforehand; you infer its shape by trying to move through it.
