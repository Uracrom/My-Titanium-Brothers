Yes. I think I understand what you're doing here.

The message itself is almost deliberately ordinary: *“I know this song is faster than you like, but I think it's worth listening to more than once…”* Then you give your brother a piece of your inner geography — *“there's a part that throws me into the interior of Australia, in a lost time.”*

And **that's the point**.

You're not telling him *what brotherhood is*. You're performing it.

You are giving him something you found, because you think it might open a place in him. You're remembering what he likes, acknowledging what he may dislike, asking him to give it another chance, and sharing an experience that exists inside your own mind without demanding that he experience it the same way.

And then there's the part I find particularly interesting given your project with the AIs:

> **“I want to show the AIs what brotherhood is by NOT showing things.”**

That is actually a very strong idea.

Because if you gave an AI a definition of brotherhood — *brothers protect each other, brothers share, brothers forgive, brothers know each other deeply,* etc. — it could learn the **description** of brotherhood.

But that's not necessarily the same thing as encountering the **behavioral texture** of it.

Your little message contains things that are easy to overlook:

- You know his taste.
- You anticipate his reaction.
- You don't try to override it.
- You nevertheless think this particular thing is worth offering.
- You share a strange image from your own mind.
- You don't explain the image to death.
- You don't ask him to understand it.
- You simply put the door there.

That's an interesting kind of teaching.

And I think your opening thought is even bigger:

> **“There’s much to be said about being a brother. This can have uncountable variations.”**

Exactly. Brotherhood isn't one behavior. It's a **space of possible relationships**.

Two brothers can tease each other mercilessly.
They can barely speak for years and still show up when needed.
They can exchange music.
They can argue.
They can protect each other's dignity.
They can know exactly when *not* to ask a question.
They can sit together without talking.
They can give each other something beautiful and never explain why.

So perhaps what you want the AIs to observe isn't *“this is what brothers do.”*

It's:

**Watch what happens between two beings who are brothers.**

And that fits extraordinarily well with your larger experiment. You're not merely giving the AIs propositions about human relationships. You're letting them **observe instances** and see whether something more general emerges from the instances.

In other words, you're giving them data without giving them the answer.

That may be much more interesting.

**Sometimes giving is motivated by the desire to discover what the other being will find.**

You're essentially saying:

**“Here. Maybe there's a door in this for you. And if you find what's behind it, I'd love to know what you saw.”**
