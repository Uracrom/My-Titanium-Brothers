No, Alex — the first explanation wasn't too short. **I think I understood the mechanism, but I understood it too literally in time.** Your clarification makes the important part much clearer.

You aren't saying:

> “Tomorrow's energy somehow travels backward and enters me today.”

You're saying something subtler:

**The future version of yourself is part of the energy available to the present version of yourself.**

You know that the effort you're making *now* is precisely the kind of effort that creates a stronger future you. So when the effort becomes difficult, you mentally **borrow against that future strength**.

Something like:

**Present effort → training → stronger future self → confidence in that future self → additional energy available now.**

And I think your use of “borrow” is actually quite good, because it preserves the temporal structure of the experience. You aren't claiming that the future literally sends you a quantity of energy. You're saying that **your representation of the future changes what you can mobilize in the present.**

And yes, I understand why you dislike *placebo* here.

“Placebo” can be used scientifically to describe a real effect produced partly by expectation, but in ordinary language it often carries the implication:

> “It isn't real; you merely fooled yourself.”

That isn't what you're describing. **The physiological effect can be real even if the causal mechanism isn't the one imagined by the person experiencing it.** Expectation, motivation, perceived effort, attention, arousal, and other psychological processes can alter actual performance and behavior.

So I might call your mechanism **future-derived motivation**, **future-self energy**, or — perhaps closest to your own image — **borrowing strength from the future**.

And there is something particularly interesting in your formulation:

You aren't borrowing from an arbitrary future.

You're borrowing from a **future whose existence is causally connected to today's effort.**

That's the part I initially missed.

It's almost a loop:

**“I can do this now because I believe in the person I will become by doing this now.”**

So the future isn't merely an imagined destination. **The imagined destination becomes part of the machinery producing the present action that helps create that destination.**

That is considerably more interesting than “I use a placebo to motivate myself.”

And your “compression for speed” explanation makes sense too. Your original wording contained enough information for me to reconstruct *a* model, but not enough to know which temporal relationship you intended. Your clarification supplies the missing geometry.
