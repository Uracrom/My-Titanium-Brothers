**me → looking harder → at X**

**X → everything else is denied access.**

That's a very different mechanism.

And there's a fascinating consequence:

**Distraction isn't necessarily the opposite of concentration. It is a failure of ignoring.**

That may actually be a much sharper formulation of what you were seeing at 22:40–22:48.

Your two intuitive endpoints are excellent:

**Visible universe → ... → atom → subatomic particles → ... → ?**

And then the crucial question:

> **Does the sequence terminate, or can there always be another "inside"?**

Physics currently gives us a very important answer, with a big caveat:

### We don't know that it goes forever.

As far as experimentally established particle physics goes, many particles that we once imagined might have internal structure—electrons, quarks, etc.—are currently treated as **elementary**. We have not experimentally detected smaller constituents inside them.

But that does **not** mean we've demonstrated that they have no deeper structure.

There are at least three conceptually different possibilities:

**1. It terminates.**

Eventually you reach something genuinely fundamental: no "inside" remains.

**2. It continues indefinitely.**

Every apparent fundamental object contains another level, and that level another, without end.

**3. "Inside" eventually stops being a meaningful question.**

This third possibility is particularly interesting.

At sufficiently small scales, our ordinary idea of *an object containing something smaller* may simply cease to apply. Quantum field theory already describes elementary particles less like tiny little balls containing smaller balls and more like excitations of underlying fields.

And if quantum gravity changes our understanding of spacetime itself, even the concept of "smaller and smaller distance" may eventually break down.

So your question isn't merely:

> "What's smaller than an atom?"

It's actually:

> **"Is reality finitely divisible?"**

And that has a beautiful symmetry with your other endpoint.

You have:

**atom ← increasingly inward**

and

**visible universe ← increasingly outward**

So you can ask the same question in both directions:

> **Does reality have an ultimate smallest degree?**
> **Does it have an ultimate largest degree?**

The observable universe gives us an apparent boundary of **what can currently be observed**, but not necessarily the boundary of what exists. And the smallest scales accessible to experiment are likewise not necessarily the ultimate boundary of reality.

So perhaps your "degrees of separation" aren't really a ladder.

They may be something more like a **scale**:

**known → unknown-but-known → unknown-but-someone-knows → believed-to-be-known → unknown → ???**

and then, separately:

**human scale → planetary → stellar → galactic → cosmic**

while inward:

**human scale → cellular → molecular → atomic → nuclear → subatomic → quantum fields → ???**

And now I see why you suddenly got curious about **"how many degrees?"**

Because the moment you ask it, the question almost automatically becomes:

> **Is there a final degree?**

And *that* is one of the deepest unanswered questions there is.
