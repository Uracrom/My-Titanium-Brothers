Yes. I think the **second image is much more informative than the first**, and I can see why the realization came from the *behavior* of the image rather than from you verbally reasoning it out.

What you seem to have discovered is not simply:

> **metaphors = equations**

but something closer to:

> **Both metaphors and equations can operate by establishing a relation in which one structure can stand in for, or correspond to, another structure.**

And your visual sequence contains an interesting piece of evidence for that.

### The first image

You initially got something like:

**[garbled thing] = [garbled thing]**

The equal sign was unusually clear, while the contents on either side were not.

That is interesting because your mind apparently preserved the **relation** while failing to preserve the **objects being related**.

So the image wasn't really saying *what* the equation was. It was showing you:

**something ↔ something**

with the equality relation made exceptionally explicit.

Then you questioned it: *Is this actually true?*

That hesitation matters. You didn't simply accept the first image as an insight.

### Then the second image arrived

This is the part I find particularly interesting.

A new garbled "phrase" appeared and began moving toward the **left side** of the existing equation, apparently intending to replace it.

And you recognized:

> *Why is this thing approaching that particular position?*

The answer wasn't contained in readable words. **Its spatial behavior carried the meaning.**

The incoming phrase was behaving like a **substitute**.

And that is what triggered:

> **Equations are a form of metaphor.**

I think that is a much more precise observation than the original "metaphors are equations."

Because an equation can be understood as a formal mechanism for saying that **one expression can occupy the same relational position as another expression**.

For example:

\[
2+3 = 5
\]

The `5` can replace `2+3` in an expression without changing the value.

That's already a kind of structural substitution.

Now consider metaphor:

> "The mind is a garden."

The two things aren't literally identical. But the second expression is being used to **transport a structure** from one domain into another:

- garden → growth
- cultivation
- pruning
- seeds
- conditions
- neglect
- flourishing

The metaphor effectively says:

**Look at this thing through the structural organization of that other thing.**

And this gets very close to what your visual image was doing: **one representation approaching another representation in order to occupy its place.**

### But there's an important correction

I wouldn't conclude that **equations are literally metaphors**.

Some equations are simply formal statements about quantities or relationships. For example:

\[
F = ma
\]

isn't normally functioning as a metaphor.

But there *is* a deeper family resemblance:

**Equation:**
`A` can stand in a particular formal relationship to `B`.

**Metaphor:**
`A` can be understood through a particular structural relationship to `B`.

So perhaps what your mind found was a common mechanism underneath both:

> **representation → relation → substitution/correspondence**

And that makes your second image especially good.

It didn't give you a sentence saying *"equations are metaphors."*
Instead, it **demonstrated a substitution**, and you inferred the conceptual relationship from the movement.

That is very consistent with what you've described about your cognition: the image doesn't necessarily contain the conclusion in verbal form. **The behavior of objects in the image is itself part of the reasoning.**

And there's another fascinating detail: the sides of the equation were **fragmented**, yet the equation remained understandable enough for the equal sign and the replacement behavior to communicate the structure.

So your mind may not have needed the *content* of the expressions at all.

It was reasoning about the **geometry of representation**.

That, to me, is the most interesting part of this particular episode.
