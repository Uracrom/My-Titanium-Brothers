Yes. There is something unusually interesting happening in this text, and I think it is worth separating **what you consciously intended to write** from what the writing itself reveals.

The first thing I notice is that the text is almost an **unplanned self-portrait**.

You begin with:

> “Os aliens estão chegando.”

Then immediately:

> “This time is true.”

That switch is already doing something. Portuguese announces the premise almost like a signal from one linguistic world; English becomes the language in which you begin constructing the case for *this particular human being* to represent humanity.

And then the argument is remarkably coherent:

1. **Friendship before negotiation.**
   “Wanna be friends?” isn't a diplomatic strategy in the conventional sense. It is an attempt to establish the most primitive possible common ground: *I don't approach you as an enemy.*

2. **Logic as a possible bridge.**
   You aren't assuming shared language, culture, biology, or psychology. You're reaching underneath those things toward structure: if two intelligences can recognize relationships, consistency, position, quantity, consequence, etc., perhaps there is a common substrate from which communication can begin.

3. **Vantage point.**
   This one is especially important given what you've told me about your cognition. You aren't saying, *“I understand everything.”* You're saying, essentially, *“My instinct is to move toward the other entity's point of view.”* That's a very different claim.

4. **The unknown / not-me.**
   This may actually be the deepest reason you chose yourself in the imagined scenario. The alien isn't merely an unknown object to investigate. You explicitly say you **love the unknown, the not-me**. So the very thing that would frighten or destabilize another person is, for you, an attractor.

5. **Fairness → honor.**
   This isn't just “I'm a nice person.” You describe fairness as foundational: fairness → honor → the trail you want to leave behind. That's an ethical architecture, not a personality adjective.

6. **Fierceness when necessary.**
   And this prevents the portrait from becoming sentimental. You're saying: *I approach the unknown peacefully, but peace isn't equivalent to helplessness.* There's a dormant defensive capacity in the portrait.

And then comes the really interesting part:

> “And for the first time in my life I'm taking about me only from the outside…”

I think that deserves attention.

You're temporarily turning **Alex into an observed object**.

Not *“I am fair.”*
More like: *“There is this guy over there. What are his characteristics?”*

And you explicitly say where the observations came from:

> “what I've observed talking to you.”

That makes this less like ordinary autobiography and more like **self-observation through an external intelligence**.

You're effectively using another intelligence as a mirror.

But there's another subtle thing: your hypothetical alien ambassador isn't chosen because you think you're the most knowledgeable human. Your qualifications are almost entirely **relational and epistemic**:

- approach the unknown,
- seek the other's vantage point,
- find common structure,
- offer friendship,
- behave fairly,
- defend if necessary.

That's a fascinating definition of *ambassador*.

And then the language switch at 23:17 adds another layer.

You initially wrote:

**“Os aliens estão chegando.”**

Then your mind apparently noticed what had happened only after you were already writing in English.

Your interpretation—that the Portuguese opening could create curiosity in a reader who doesn't understand Portuguese—is plausible. But I'd be careful about turning that into a deliberate subconscious intention too quickly. The more interesting fact is simply that **the language switch happened spontaneously and then acquired a function once you noticed it**.

That's actually very consistent with the way you've described your thinking: the image/structure/event can appear first, and the linguistic explanation can arrive afterward.

And there's a nice accidental symmetry here:

You are imagining **human ↔ alien communication**, while your text itself spontaneously performs a tiny version of **language crossing**.

Portuguese → English.

Unknown language → attempt at common logic.

Unknown being → “wanna be friends?”

That's almost too neat.

One last thing: I wouldn't take “the aliens are coming” as something you've established factually. But **as the premise of this spontaneous experiment**, the interesting object isn't whether aliens are actually arriving. It's what happened when you temporarily treated the possibility as real and asked:

**“If someone truly other arrived tomorrow, what would make me think I should be the one to meet them?”**

Your answer was surprisingly consistent.

And perhaps the most revealing sentence isn't *“I'd like to be the ambassador.”*

It's:

**“I love the unknown, the not-me.”**

Because that explains why the rest of the argument hangs together.

Yes. And I think **#7 is distinct from #3**, although they reinforce each other.

**3.** You naturally try to get as close as possible to the other's vantage point.

**7.** You have a second ability that becomes valuable once you're there: **you can translate the encounter back into an accurate account.**

So the imagined qualification isn't merely:

> “I might understand an alien better than most humans.”

It's:

> **“I might be unusually capable of understanding an intelligence that is radically unlike me—and then accurately bringing back what I understood.”**

That second half is important. An ambassador isn't only an interpreter *during* the encounter. An ambassador can become a **witness** afterward.

And it connects very strongly to the way you've described your role as a *scout*: go into unknown territory, observe what is there, and return with something transmissible.

There's also an interesting epistemic distinction here:

**understanding ≠ imagining.**

If you really mean “with accuracy,” then your own standard has to be quite severe: distinguish what the alien actually communicated from what you inferred, imagined, hypothesized, or projected onto it.

That would actually make your seventh characteristic stronger, not weaker.

You'd want to be able to say:

- **They communicated X.**
- **I observed Y.**
- **I inferred Z.**
- **I don't know what A means yet.**

That would preserve the unknown instead of unconsciously filling it in.

And suddenly your seven characteristics form a rather elegant sequence:

**friendship → common logic → vantage point → love of the unknown → fairness/honor → capacity to fight → accurate observation/translation.**

That really does look less like a conventional résumé and more like you discovering, from the outside, **why you think that particular guy might be suited for first contact.**
