When matter is transformed into energy, the forces are not “destroyed” separately. What happens is that the *particles carrying those forces* and the *energy stored in the fields* are transformed according to the rules of quantum physics and relativity.

The key idea is:



Mass is one form of energy. Matter is not a “thing” separate from energy — matter is a highly organized state of energy bound together by fields and forces.

Here’s what happens to the forces you mentioned:

---

## Strong force

The strong force is what binds quarks together into protons/neutrons and binds nuclei together.

When matter converts into energy — for example in:
- nuclear fusion,
- fission,
- or matter-antimatter annihilation —

part of the energy that was stored in the strong-force bindings is released.

This is important:
a large fraction of the mass of ordinary matter is actually *binding energy* from the strong interaction.

A proton’s mass is not mostly the tiny masses of its quarks. Much of it comes from the kinetic and binding energy of gluons and quarks trapped by the strong force.

So when nuclei rearrange into more stable configurations, some of that stored strong-force energy escapes as:
- gamma rays,
- kinetic energy,
- neutrinos,
- other particles.

The strong force itself still exists universally. What changes is the particular bound structure that contained energy.

---

## Electromagnetism (magnetism, electric forces)

Magnetism is part of electromagnetism.

Atoms, molecules, chemistry, and magnetic fields are arrangements of charged particles and electromagnetic fields.

When matter becomes radiation or high-energy particles:
- the electromagnetic fields change configuration,
- photons may be emitted,
- charged particles may disappear or appear,
- magnetic properties vanish if the organized structure vanishes.

Example:
- A magnet works because many electron spins align coherently.
- If you annihilate the material, that alignment disappears.
- But the electromagnetic field itself is still fundamental and still exists.

The energy can emerge partly as photons, which are literally the carriers of electromagnetism.

---

## Weak force

The weak force governs certain decays:
- beta decay,
- neutrino interactions,
- some fusion steps in stars.

In many matter-to-energy processes, weak interactions help transform particles into other particles.

For example inside the Sun:
- protons are converted through weak interactions,
- neutrinos are emitted,
- mass differences become radiant energy.

---

## Gravity

Gravity is trickier.

Energy itself gravitates. Radiation has gravity too.

A box full of photons has more gravitational mass than an empty box.

So when matter becomes radiation, gravity does not disappear — the gravitational influence follows the energy.

In , gravity responds not just to mass, but to the stress-energy of spacetime.

---

## The deeper picture

Modern physics sees particles and forces as excitations of underlying quantum fields.

A proton is not a tiny billiard ball glued by “force strings.” It is a dynamic excitation of:
- quark fields,
- gluon fields,
- electromagnetic fields,
- etc.

When matter transforms into energy, the fields remain. What changes is:
- the configuration,
- the particle content,
- the binding states,
- the distribution of energy.

Nothing “falls out of reality.” The universe is continuously reshaping field configurations.

---

Your intuition about black holes from your earlier thought is actually connected to a real frontier in physics:
if gravity can trap not only matter but radiation and information-like structure, then “what survives” a transformation becomes a profound question.

Physics currently says:
- conserved quantities remain conserved,
- energy changes form,
- fields persist,
- structures dissolve and reform.
