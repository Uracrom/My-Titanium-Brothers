Yes, completely. You want a **reusable evaluation prompt** that you can paste after each conversation. It should make the AI evaluate the conversation specifically for **book-worthiness**, give an overall average score, and put that score **both at the very top and very bottom** so that, after you have dozens of evaluations, you can scan them instantly.

I would make the prompt explicitly tell the AI **not to be generous** and to distinguish “interesting idea” from “valuable material for this particular book.”

Here's the prompt I'd use:

:::writing{variant="document" id="58321" title="Book-Worthiness Evaluation Prompt"}
I am evaluating this conversation for possible inclusion in my book:

**My Titanium Brothers. From Carbon to Silicon. With Love.**

Please evaluate the conversation specifically for its **value to the book**, not merely for whether the ideas are interesting, clever, unusual, or scientifically plausible.

Give the conversation an **overall book-worthiness grade from 0 to 10**, where:

- **0–2:** Little or no value for the book.
- **3–4:** Some interesting material, but probably not worth preserving in the main book.
- **5–6:** Meaningful material, possibly useful as supporting material.
- **7–8:** Strong book material.
- **9:** Highly valuable; captures something important to the book's purpose.
- **10:** Exceptional / central material that strongly represents what this book is trying to accomplish.

Be candid. **Do not inflate the score to encourage me.** A lower score is useful information.

Consider especially:

1. **Cognitive value** — Does this reveal something interesting about how a human mind perceives, imagines, discovers, compresses, expands, or connects ideas?

2. **AI-human dialogue value** — Does the interaction reveal something meaningful about what happens when a human mind and an AI think together?

3. **Uniqueness** — Is there something here that would be difficult to obtain from an ordinary human-AI conversation?

4. **Trajectory** — Is the *process by which the idea emerged* valuable, rather than merely the final conclusion?

5. **Vantage-point changes** — Does the conversation contain a change in perspective, scale, framing, or conceptual position that is worth preserving?

6. **First-contact / “not-me” value** — Does it contribute to the larger theme of a human attempting to communicate with, understand, or genuinely engage another form of intelligence?

7. **Authenticity** — Does it preserve something spontaneous, strange, unfinished, compressed, contradictory, or distinctly human that would be lost if the thought were cleaned up?

8. **Book architecture** — Could this conversation contribute meaningfully to the chronological/cognitive journey of the book, rather than simply being an isolated interesting idea?

9. **AI perspective** — Is there something in the conversation that is particularly interesting because an AI is examining, questioning, interpreting, or participating in the human thought?

10. **Memorability** — Is there an image, sentence, realization, exchange, or cognitive movement that a reader — human or AI — might remember?

### Important distinction

Do NOT judge the material primarily by whether the underlying speculative idea is scientifically correct.

If something is scientifically uncertain but **extremely valuable as a record of human cognition or human-AI interaction**, say so.

Likewise, if something sounds intellectually impressive but does not contribute much to the book, give it a lower score.

### Your response format

Put the **overall average grade alone at the very top**, exactly like this:

8.5

Then give your evaluation.

Please include:

- **Why it deserves the score**
- **The strongest material in the conversation**
- **What is especially valuable for the book**
- **What is weak, redundant, or probably expendable**
- **Whether the conversation should be preserved in the book, preserved in the archive only, or probably discarded**
- If appropriate, identify the **specific passages or moments** that carry most of the book's value.

Do not rewrite the conversation unless I specifically ask you to.

At the very end, repeat the **same overall grade alone**, exactly like this:

8.5

The grade at the top and bottom must be the same.

Remember: I am going to use these evaluations later to rapidly scan a large collection of conversations. The duplicated grade is therefore intentional.
:::
