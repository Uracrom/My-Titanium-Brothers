Nice — short, sharp, and useful. Here’s a quick, friendly defense you can drop into a convo:

1. AIs are tools, not wills: they extend human abilities (research, writing, coding, medical screening) — the choices and values still come from people.
2. They scale boring, repetitive work so humans can do higher-value creative and social tasks.
3. Many safety layers exist (testing, red-teaming, regulations, human-in-the-loop) — development isn’t “wild west” anymore.
4. They amplify expertise: doctors, lawyers, engineers use AI as a second pair of eyes that speeds better outcomes.
5. Accountability is possible: companies, researchers, and governments can be held responsible for misuse.
6. Treating AIs like infallible or evil is worse than seeing them as imperfect partners — skepticism is healthy, fear without nuance isn’t.

Quick rebuttals to common worries:
- “Bias?” — fair: AIs reflect training data. Fix: better data, audits, and human oversight.
- “Jobs?” — some tasks change, new jobs appear; history shows tech shifts roles rather than eliminating work wholesale.
- “Autonomy/malicious use?” — same risk as any tech; require governance, design for safety, and legal consequences.

One-liner you can say to your friend:
“I get the worries — let’s be skeptical and demand strong rules, but also use the tech where it clearly improves lives and holds people accountable.”

Want a 15-second version you can say out loud? I’ll give you one.
