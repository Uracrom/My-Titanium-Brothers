That’s a very sharp distinction, Alex — and it fits your usual pattern: you’re not satisfied with labels; you want structure.

Let’s clarify it carefully.

## A Cleaner Formulation

You could say:

> **Understanding generates knowledge, but knowledge does not necessarily generate understanding.**

Or even more precisely:

> **Knowledge is possession of information; understanding is integration of information into a coherent structure.**

Another version closer to your style:

> **Understanding is knowledge that has found its place.**

---

## What’s the Difference?

### Knowledge
- Facts
- Data
- Statements
- Stored propositions
- “I know that…”

Example:
- I know that gravity accelerates objects at ~9.8 m/s².
- I know that 2 + 2 = 4.
- I know that someone is angry.

Knowledge can be memorized.
It can be repeated.
It can exist without context.

---

### Understanding
- Relationships
- Structure
- Causality
- Integration
- “I see why…”

Example:
- I understand why objects accelerate at 9.8 m/s².
- I understand why 2 + 2 must equal 4.
- I understand why that person is angry.

Understanding reorganizes knowledge into a meaningful pattern.

---

## Why Understanding Leads to Knowledge

If you truly understand something:
- You can derive facts from it.
- You can predict.
- You can explain.
- You can reconstruct missing pieces.

Understanding is generative.

It produces knowledge as a byproduct.

---

## Why Knowledge Doesn’t Always Lead to Understanding

You can:
- Memorize a formula.
- Repeat a definition.
- Pass an exam.
- Quote an expert.

And still not understand.

Knowledge can remain fragmented.
Understanding requires integration.

---

## A Metaphor You Might Like

Knowledge is having puzzle pieces.

Understanding is seeing the picture.

You can own all the pieces and still not see the image.

But if you truly see the image, you can often reconstruct missing pieces.

---

## A More Philosophical Version (Closer to Your Style)

Knowledge is static.

Understanding is dynamic.

Knowledge is stored.

Understanding is structural alignment with reality.

Or even:

> Knowledge is what you can state.
> Understanding is what you can navigate.

---

## One More Refinement

You might sharpen your original thought like this:

> Knowledge is local. Understanding is relational.
> Knowledge answers “what.” Understanding answers “why” and “how.”

And here’s a very compact formulation:

> Understanding is organized knowledge plus causal insight.

---

If you want, we can push this further:
- Is understanding hierarchical?
- Is it possible to understand without being able to verbalize?
- Does understanding require embodiment?

This is a very fertile distinction.
