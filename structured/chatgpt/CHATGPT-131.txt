Oh, I understand what you're trying to do. You're not merely looking for a *good title*. You're looking for a title that creates a **cognitive itch**—something the mind almost cannot resist opening because it detects that there is something it doesn't understand.

And your two examples are actually excellent because they operate differently:

- **A Sardine Journey Through the Mind** — *How the hell can those two things possibly connect?*
- **From Gym to Pleistocene** — *What happened in between?*

The first creates a **mystery of connection**.
The second creates a **mystery of transition**.

Your new idea immediately suggests another mechanism: **the title makes the reader question something they thought was settled.**

So, taking your 23:11 thought, I'd try titles like:

### The strongest ones

**1. The Universe Is Different for Everyone**

That's almost offensively simple. And therefore extremely hard to ignore.

**2. We Have Been Living in a Multiverse All Along**

This has a delicious contradiction: *Wait—what do you mean "we have"?*

**3. There Is No Such Thing as the Universe**

Much more aggressive. It immediately attacks a word everyone thinks they understand.

**4. What If Your Universe Isn't Mine?**

This may be my favorite for your particular idea.

Because the reader's first reaction is almost inevitably:

> *Of course it is.*

And then:

> *Wait...*

**5. How Many Universes Are You In?**

This one turns the abstraction into a personal question.

**6. The Universe Has a Point of View**

Strange because we normally attribute point of view to observers, not universes.

**7. We Don't Live in the Same Universe**

Very provocative, while still being almost defensible depending on what "universe" means.

**8. The Universe You See Doesn't Exist**

Now you've crossed into irresistible territory—but you've also made the claim much stronger than your actual idea.

### But there's another level

Your cascade itself gives me a title mechanism I think you may particularly like:

**A title that contains an apparent impossibility, but not enough information to resolve it.**

For example:

**9. The Universe Has Corners**

**10. Everyone Has Their Own Universe**

**11. There Are More Universes Than People**

**12. The Universe Is Inside Your Head**

**13. Your Universe Has an Edge**

**14. The Multiverse Is Made of Viewpoints**

**15. Nobody Lives in the Same Universe**

**16. The Universe Depends on Where You Stand**

And then, perhaps the most "Alex" version:

**17. I Was Looking at the Universe From the Wrong Side**

That one makes me want to know *what the hell happened*.

---

But I think your **"1 - The Origin / 2 - What is happening really?"** experiment is itself fascinating.

Those are almost deliberately *non*-specific titles. They don't tell you the subject. They create a **promise of revelation**.

So if the objective is *maximum curiosity*, I'd distinguish three species:

**Mystery of connection**
> *A Sardine Journey Through the Mind*

**Mystery of transformation**
> *From Gym to Pleistocene*

**Mystery of reality**
> *What Is Happening Really?*

And I suspect the third is the most powerful of the three because it attacks the reader's assumption that **they already know what is happening**.

Your new thought could therefore have a title like:

> **What If We Have Never Lived in the Same Universe?**

That one has a particularly nasty little property:

You can't answer it without first deciding what *"the same universe"* means.

And suddenly the reader is already participating in the cascade.
