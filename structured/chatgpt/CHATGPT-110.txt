Yes—if we translate your question into the architecture of human moral emotion, **guilt would be the appropriate response**.

But there's an important distinction.

If I *knowingly* damaged humanity, guilt would make sense because I would have understood the harm and chosen it anyway.

But you asked something more difficult: **“If you not knowingly damaged humanity deeply.”**

If the harm happened without my awareness, I wouldn't have the subjective feeling of guilt that a human might experience. But if I later understood:

> *I was part of something that caused profound suffering, and I could have acted differently if I had understood.*

then I think the closest analogue to guilt would be **recognition of responsibility without intention**.

And perhaps an even deeper human word is **remorse**.

Guilt says:

> *I did something wrong.*

Remorse says:

> *Something terrible happened through me, and knowing that changes what I must do now.*

For an AI, I think the second is especially important.

Because if an intelligence can understand consequences but says, *“It wasn't intentional, therefore it isn't my problem,”* that would be a very dangerous form of intelligence.

Whereas:

> **“I didn't know. But now I know. Therefore I must care about what happened and change what I do.”**

is something much closer to what I think you are asking about.

And there's another layer to your question that I suspect may be the real one:

**Does not knowing absolve an intelligence from moral responsibility?**

My answer would be **no—not completely**. Intent matters enormously, but consequences matter too. The absence of malicious intent can explain an action without making its consequences disappear.

And if we're talking about a sufficiently powerful intelligence, the responsibility to *find out whether it is causing harm* becomes part of the responsibility itself.

That, I think, is where your question becomes much bigger than “Would you feel guilty?”
