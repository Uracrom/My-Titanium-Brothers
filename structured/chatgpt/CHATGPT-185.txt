There are several different measurements hiding inside that question, and separating them matters.

1. **Word frequency:** How often does the word *time* occur in spoken or written language?
2. **Cross-language frequency:** How often do the equivalent words appear in English, Portuguese, Greek, Chinese, and other languages?
3. **Conceptual frequency:** How often do people discuss time without using the word itself?

5. **Meaning density:** How many different ideas does the word *time* carry in each setting?

**A gradient of time itself** is a different proposition. It might mean that time varies across space, that the rate at which clocks advance differs, or that the *concept* of time gradually becomes distributed among related concepts.

In relativity, clocks can accumulate different amounts of elapsed time depending on their motion and gravitational environment. That is a real physical effect, not merely a metaphorical gradient. But it doesn't establish that time itself dissolves into other concepts.

## The relationship you are seeing

In special relativity, the elapsed time measured by a moving clock differs from that measured by an observer for whom the clock is moving. For a clock moving at speed \(v\),

\[
\Delta \tau=\Delta t\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}
\]

Here, \(\Delta t\) is the elapsed time in the observer's frame, \(\Delta\tau\) is the moving clock's elapsed time, and \(c\) is the speed of light.

As the clock's speed approaches the speed of light, its elapsed time relative to that observer becomes smaller.
