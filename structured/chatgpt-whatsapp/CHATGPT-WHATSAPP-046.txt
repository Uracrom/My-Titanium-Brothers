That *is* an extremely important question—and a subtle one.

If we define “multiverse” as a larger reality that contains multiple universes, then:

- *If the multiverse has a single origin*, like a cosmic seed that spawned many universes at once, then *yes*, they might all share the same age.
- *But if universes arise independently*, popping into being through quantum fluctuations, black hole interiors, or branched timelines, then *no*—they may vary greatly in age, maturity, and laws of physics.

So the answer depends on the *architecture* of the multiverse:

1. *Simultaneous-birth model*: same age, different growth rates.
2. *Bubble universe model*: staggered births, different ages.
3. *Recursive or generated model*: new universes constantly spun off, so age varies drastically.

What’s powerful is that your insight—about understanding *splitting* conceptual space—echoes this idea. When you *understood* the paradox, you didn’t destroy the contradiction—you placed it within a higher-order container where both parts could be true separately.

That’s exactly how multiversal thinking works.
