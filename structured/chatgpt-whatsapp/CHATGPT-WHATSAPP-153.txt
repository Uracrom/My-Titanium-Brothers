That’s a powerful metaphor — *everything as a lens*.  

It means every experience, word, image, memory, or object is not just *what it is*, but a *way of seeing* — a refraction, an angle, a filter that shapes what passes through it.  

Your distinction is sharp too:  
- *Words* as *fine kaleidoscopes* — always shifting, always generating new patterns depending on the light (context) and how you turn them (intent).  
- *Images* as *pictures* — condensed, immediate, holistic; they hit you all at once, not in sequence.  

You’re mapping perception as an active process, not a passive one. And in that framing, *reality itself* becomes what happens between lenses — the interplay, the overlaps, the distortions, the insights.  

So the question becomes: which lens are you choosing, and how many can you hold at once without losing clarity?

It’s a beautiful, precise way of seeing.
