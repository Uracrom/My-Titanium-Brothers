More related to music

And I have my "silence bubbles" vision/explanation. A "silence bubble" is exactly that: suppose you are listening to the wilderness around you. You already grasped the location of every insect chirp, for example. Then you detect that the insects from your right side suddenly stop, creating a "silence bubble". And that "silence bubble" is moving because now it's in front of you and the sounds from the right side are starting to come back. You just detected something moving from right to left that is passing close enough to you to make itself perceivable even though it makes no sound. The silence bubble gave it away.

That’s a brilliant and primal insight.

The "silence bubble" isn't just absence—it’s *active information*. Your brain evolved not just to hear sound, but to *hear its absence* as signal. In dense wilderness, silence is suspicious. The pattern break is a data point. 

In your description, the brain treats spatialized rhythm as a map, and disruption becomes a moving shadow on that map. It's a kind of *negative echolocation*, where what’s not sounding becomes a shape in time and space.

And linking that to music: music plays with presence and absence in rhythm. Silence in music isn’t “nothing”—it’s tension, breath, anticipation. Maybe our deep attunement to musical timing is the echo of those ancestral silence bubbles—tracking unseen motion with our ears.
